# Feasibility Analysis of a Mobile Slaughter Unit (NMPAN MSU Cost Calculator, 2009)

Author: Kathleen Painter, Analyst, University of Idaho Ag Economics & Rural Sociology (Adjunct, WSU School of Economic Sciences). Spreadsheet dated 18 May 2009, PDF printout 5/22/2009, hosted by NMPAN. The budget has a line for a USDA inspector, so it models a **USDA-inspected** MSU. All dollar figures are **2009**.

**Important framing [inferred]:** the "revenue" is retail value of cut-and-wrapped meat minus the live animal's cost (so it is a producer/marketer owning the meat), not a fee-for-service processor charging kill fees. The $280k "net revenue" is therefore value-added retail margin, not service-business profit. Do not quote it as MSU profitability for a custom-hire unit.

## Throughput assumptions
- Max per hour: cattle 2, pigs 4, sheep 6.
- MSU runs 7 hours/day "to allow for set-up and paperwork"; 50 weeks/year.
- Max per kill day: 14 cattle, 28 pigs, or 42 sheep.
- "The maximum number of kill days per week would be 4 or 5 to allow for transport, off-loading, cleaning, and maintenance."
- Example scenario: 1 kill day/week, 6 cattle + 10 pigs + 9 sheep per day (7.0 hours), giving 300 beef, 500 pork, 450 sheep per year.

## Yield assumptions (beef)
- Live weight 1,250 lb; hanging weight 62% (775 lb); cut-and-wrapped 36% of live (450 lb).
- Retail $6.00/lb cut-and-wrapped ($2,700/head); live cost $1.00/lb ($1,250/head); value added $1,450/head.
- Pork: 260 lb live, 72% hanging (187 lb), 130 lb retail at $4.60. Sheep: 120 lb live, 54% hanging, 48 lb retail at $7.00.

## Capex and ownership costs (2009)
| Item | Cost | Loan | Annual payment | Depreciation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mobile Slaughter Unit | $250,000 | 10 yr @ 7% | $35,594 | $24,000/yr (salvage $10,000, 10-yr life) |
| Tandem axle truck, used | $20,000 | 5 yr @ 7% | $4,878 | $1,600/yr |
| Holding corrals / knock box | $10,000 | 5 yr @ 7% | $2,439 | $380/yr (25-yr life) |

Total ownership costs $68,511/yr (loan payments $42,911 + depreciation $25,600 — note the sheet adds both, which double-counts capital recovery [inferred]).

## Operating costs (example year)
- Labor for MSU $43,100: 3 butchers at $25/hr ($10,000 each/yr), 2 assistant butchers at $15/hr ($6,000 each), USDA inspector at $55/hr ($1,100/yr). (Note: under FSIS, regular-shift inspection is federally funded; the $55/hr line likely reflects overtime/holiday or an assumption [inferred].)
- Supplies $20/day; propane for hot water $18/day; electricity for lights and AC $20/day.
- **Cut and wrap (outsourced) $0.65/lb hanging weight** — 355,260 lb, $230,919/yr. Smoking hams/bacon $1.25/lb. **Storage locker rental $0.10/lb**.
- Fuel to drive to butcher shop 12 gal/trip at $2.50; machinery repairs $60/day; truck driver 1 hr/day at $20; management fee $100/day.
- Operating interest 7% on 50% of operating costs; overhead (legal, accounting, phone) 2.5%.
- Total operating costs $910,918; net above operating $349,282; total costs $979,429; returns to risk $280,771.

## Implications for a fee-for-service plan [inferred]
- The MSU only does kill and chill-transport. Cut-and-wrap and storage are bought from a fixed facility, which is why those lines dominate the non-animal costs.
- Kill-day labor (5 people, 7 hours) costs about $860 per kill day at 2009 wages. At 14 head/day that is roughly $61/head in labor alone before capital.
- Capital recovery of about $43k/yr on 300 head is about $143/head. At 4 days/week x 14 head that would fall a long way, so utilization drives break-even.
