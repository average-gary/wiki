## Equipment on the custom-exempt models (#1-#3)

These models are described as "Designed for Quarter [or Half] Beef Processed Outside". The kill, skinning and evisceration happen in the field, outside the trailer. Only chilling happens inside. Listed features:
- **Lifting:** a "Swinging Crane with Hydraulics" and a "Roof Mounted Crane and hydraulic lift", each with a Mile Marker winch: 8,000 lb on #1-#2 and 10,000 lb on #3.
- **Carcass handling:** 20 stainless trolleys, stainless steel rail and hangers, and an "Aluminum Skinning Cradle and Gambrel" (#2-#3). #2-#3 also have a "Gut Barrel Crane & Hoist".
- **Hot water:** a food-grade aluminium freshwater tank (100 gal on #1, 200 gal on #2-#3), an on-demand hot water heater, a 12 V water pump (55 psi), a frost-free hydrant, a tank heater pad and a compartment heater.
- **Chill:** a 12,000 BTU refrigeration unit (#1-#2) or a 24,000 BTU unit (#3), with 4 in of spray urethane insulation.
- **Power:** a commercial generator, two 12 V deep-cycle batteries, a transfer switch between line power and generator, and a 110 V 100 A battery charger.
- **Chassis:** tandem 6,000 lb torsion axles on #1, or tandem 8,000 lb axles on #2-#3. FRP walls with an aluminium deck floor.
- The home page says the units are "Compact enough for a 3/4 ton pickup ... with dual rear winches".

## USDA models (#4-#5), for contrast
#4 adds a 15 ft interior kill compartment, a third axle, an 8,000 lb interior ceiling winch, hydraulic levelling, a rear wash sink, a knife sanitizer, a rear drain to the ground and a rear man door. It is "Designed for Half Beef Under USDA Inspection". #5 adds a head inspection station, a splitting saw and sterilizer, a rail scale, rail switches, a 60 gal drain capacity and an 18 ft butcher floor with poly tables.

## How carcasses reach the cut shop
On the custom-exempt models, sides or quarters go straight from the skinning cradle onto the rail in the onboard cooler. They are trucked hanging to the cut shop. The capacity ceiling is 3-10 beef per trip, depending on the model. [inferred] That cooler capacity, not the kill speed, sets the maximum head per day, unless the operator unloads mid-day.
