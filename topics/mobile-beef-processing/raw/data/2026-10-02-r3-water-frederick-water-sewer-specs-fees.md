# Frederick Water: sewer discharge rules and fees for a small cooler site

**Name change.** The Frederick County Sanitation Authority (FCSA) now brands itself Frederick Water (315 Tasker Road, Stephens City, VA 22655; 540-868-1061). Its social accounts are still @fcsawater, and its fee tables still have an "FCSA" column. fcsa-water.com does not resolve, and the Wayback Machine has no capture of it. frederickwater.com returns 403 to curl, so pages were read through web.archive.org captures (the 2026 home page and rates page; an older connection-fees capture).

## Standards and Specifications (approved October 15, 2024)

**Approvals (Section 1).**
- "All projects must meet Frederick Water's approval."
- "DEQ must also approve plans that (a) serve greater than 400 persons or have a design flow greater than 40,000 gpd, (b) have pumping stations, (c) have industrial, commercial or private pre-treatment facilities, (d) contain low pressure collection or forced sewer systems, or (e) contain pipe greater than 12 inches in diameter."
- Plans must be prepared by or under a Virginia-registered PE. Frederick Water aims to review them within 20 working days.
- For commercial or industrial projects, service is authorized only after the project is accepted and fees are paid.

**Prohibited discharges (sanitary sewer section).**
- C: "Unmetered water, i.e. swimming pool backwash water, condensation, storm water lines (including sump pumps, trash enclosure drains, battery/hydrogen/fuel cell discharge/byproduct, etc.) shall not be discharged to the sanitary sewer system."
- D: "Brine discharges from ion-exchange water softeners shall not be discharged to the sanitary sewer system."
- [inferred] An outdoor wash pad where a mobile unit washes down would have to be roofed or bermed so rainwater stays out of the sanitary connection. A "trash enclosure drain" type connection is explicitly banned.

**Grease and oil (item FF, repeated in the Construction Notes).**
- "Oil water separators and/or grease traps may be required by Frederick or Clarke County Building Inspections. Both Frederick or Clarke County Building Inspections and Frederick Water will inspect the installation of those facilities ... Ongoing verification of the maintenance of the facilities will be conducted by Frederick Water."
- Tank requirements: sections bolted together; watertight bolt-down frame and covers; banded boots at penetrations; traffic-rated components.
- [inferred] The trigger for requiring an interceptor sits with the county building official, under the plumbing code (the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, which uses the IPC). Frederick Water then inspects maintenance.

**Capacity and connection.**
- Average daily design flows follow 9VAC25-790-460, Table 3, of the Sewage Collection and Treatment (SCAT) regulations.
- Peak design is 250% of average flow for mains and interceptors.
- Sewer mains tying into the Frederick-Winchester Service Authority trunk line need a metering station.

## Gaps [inferred]

- Frederick Water's public site does not show a sewer use ordinance, local limits, a pretreatment or industrial user permit program, or a FOG program document.
- Wastewater from the Frederick Water system is treated at plants run by or with the Frederick-Winchester Service Authority, such as the Opequon Water Reclamation Facility. Local limits for significant industrial users would most likely sit with FWSA or the City of Winchester. Their sites (fwsa-va.org) did not respond on 2026-10-02.
- For a cooler site where a mobile unit washes down, the practical questions for Frederick Water are:
  - whether a sewer tap is available at the parcel (the service area map is ArcGIS);
  - whether the county building official will require a grease or solids interceptor;
  - whether blood must be kept out of the sewer.
