# A Study of Small-Volume Red Meat Processing in Virginia (Matson / VA FAIRS, 2020)

**Provenance:** Prepared by Matson Consulting (Aiken, SC) "in coordination with" VA FAIRS (Richmond), September 2020. The authors describe it as drawn from "a literature and database search as well as the knowledge of the consultants". It is **not a survey of Virginia processors**. VDACS RD894 (Jan 2023) cites it, alongside PEC/Fuller 2021, as one of its two "essential resources." [inferred: it is the closest statewide Virginia processing study before 2023, and it is distinct from the Virginia Tech/VSU assessment the caller is looking for.]

## Virginia industry findings (2000-2019)
- Virginia beef cattle inventory is down 3%, hogs down 20% and meat goats down 26%. Sheep are up 29%.
- Virginia red meat slaughter fell almost 26% by 2019, while national slaughter rose 19% to 55 billion lb.
- "Most of the slaughter and processing facilities located in Virginia are along Interstate 81, with most clustered in Northern Virginia."
- It cites a VDACS list of inspected slaughter plants "that responded to a March 2020 survey". That is a VDACS survey, not a university one.

## Beef-only pro forma (Table 30)
| | 1,000 hd | 2,500 hd | 5,000 hd |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $466,390 | $1,165,975 | $2,331,951 |
| Direct labor | $172,564 | $431,411 | $862,822 |
| Other variable | $163,237 | $408,091 | $816,183 |
| EBITDA | $5,971 | $59,818 | $165,285 |
| Depreciation | $41,506 | $56,301 | $71,890 |
| **Net income** | **$(35,535)** | **$3,517** | **$93,395** |

- Revenue is $466/head, the average across the seven small studies. Per-head figures ranged from Del Norte's $189 (toll) to Central Virginia's $433.
- "Animal purchase is the second largest expense." Labor plus variable costs come to about 73% of sales. [inferred: the model blends a meat-company model, which buys animals, with fee-for-service revenue. Read it as a brick-and-mortar benchmark, not a toll-only one.]
- Contingency is 4% of sales.
- Multispecies nets $(42,236), $17,754 and $151,725 respectively.
- Labor "can use up to 50% or more of sales."
- Financing assumptions (Tables 32-33): 80% financed at 4%. Equipment on 15-year terms costs $202,595, $214,512 and $238,347. Buildings on 30-year terms are 3,000, 4,500 and 6,000 sq ft, costing $840k, $1.26M and $1.68M ($280/sq ft).
- Minimum owner equity is about $209,000 for the 1,000-head beef plant, plus working capital.
- Conclusion: the 1,000 and 2,500-head plants are "in financially distressing positions from the outset." The 5,000-head plant "has the most potential."

## Mobile slaughter units (pp. 57-61)
- The FSIS MSU Compliance Guide (2010) says MSUs reduce processing costs, animal stress, and capital, and meet "less resistance and 'red tape' from local governments."
- An MSU can be an extension of an existing plant "to help reduce wait times at the plant", raising capacity without expanding the plant.
- **Start-up cost:** "the overall cost to build and implement an MSU is under $250,000." This comes from Food & Livestock Planning and NMPAN tables. The tables are images and did not extract.
- Inspection issues:
  - The grant of inspection needs an official address. Use the home plant's address or the manager's home.
  - Inspectors must be told the slaughter location "a week ahead of time."
  - Inspector travel time and cost.
  - Connectivity.
- Water and waste: processing water must be certified potable. MSUs typically carry a 500-gal potable tank and up to 500 gal of wastewater holding.
- Hurdles, per Food & Livestock Planning 2011: higher per-animal cost, weather and road scheduling, limited carcass storage, and waste disposal and county/state-line rules.
- Red-meat MSUs listed: Alaska Meat Co., Univ. of Alaska (reindeer), Central Coast Grown (CA), Ranch Foods Direct (CO), Wild Idea Buffalo (SD), Broken Arrow Ranch (TX), Community Ag Development Center (Colville, WA), Island Grown Farmers Coop (WA), and Puget Sound Meat Producers Coop (WA). **None are in Virginia.**
