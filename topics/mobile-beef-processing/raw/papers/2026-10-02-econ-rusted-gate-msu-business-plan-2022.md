# Rusted Gate Farm MSU Business Plan (2022)

Nonprofit (501c3) demonstration farm, Central Point (Rogue Valley), Oregon. Plan for a **USDA FSIS-inspected** MSU (chosen over Oregon's new state program because of proximity to California and interstate sales) that can also do **custom-exempt** slaughter under Oregon Department of Agriculture licensing ("a unit can do both, just requires different recordkeeping"). All figures are **2022**. NMPAN's hosting page states: "Ultimately, based on the business plan findings and other developments in the region, the non-profit decided NOT to purchase and operate a mobile slaughter unit," and "it is very difficult to breakeven on one of these units."

## Operating model
- Kill-only service. Producers haul to the MSU docking site and unload into on-site pens. Carcasses hang in the MSU drip cooler for about 24 hours, then RGF delivers them to a local **inspected cut-and-wrap partner**. The MSU does not cut.
- Projected volume (anecdotal): **500 beef year 1, 700 year 2, 1,000 year 3**, uneven and fall-heavy. Volume needs the MSU only "1-2 days a week in the first couple years," so the plan suggests leasing it 2-3 days/week to a fixed plant.
- Carcass transport to cut-and-wrap: reefer freight about $3.33/loaded mile (3/2022). Quartered carcasses go in a poly-lined cardboard tote (about $20 each) on a pallet (about $12 each).
- Live-cattle hauling estimate: about $3/loaded mile (DAT, 4/7/22). RGF's own cost to a Eureka, CA plant was $540 round trip plus $250 for pickup, $790 for two beef, or **$395/head transport**.

Quote: "Kill fees for inspected slaughter plants range from $80 on the low end to $205 on the high end, with an average of $134. Mobile slaughter units typically charge even more because they have higher overhead that they have to spread out over a lower volume of animals, with kill fees ranging from $159 - $260 and above." The plan argues an MSU could charge up to $200/hd and stay competitive because it saves the producer $100-$200/hd in transport.

## Capex and labor
- **Total build-out with the Friesla unit: ~$550,000.** Start-up operating capital about $280,000 with financing (covers 3 years of monthly cash flow).
- The appendices compare Friesla (Everson WA, "turnkey") with Mobile Processing Trailers & Supply (Colorado, "bare bones"). "The cost differences (and what is included) between the two units are significant."
- Staff: a lead butcher and an assistant butcher, each **0.5 FTE in years 1-2 and 1.0 FTE in year 3**. Admin, waste handling and carcass driving are absorbed by existing farm staff, so those costs are not in the MSU P&L [inferred from text].
- Liquid waste land-applied on hay fields and solid waste hot-composted on-farm, if permitted.
- Biggest cost lines: "salaries, wage, payroll taxes and benefits", then depreciation, loan interest, and **offal disposal**.

## Financial scenarios (revenue = head x fee; $175/hd in B and C)
| Scenario | Y1 | Y2 | Y3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| B: 80% debt-financed, $175/hd. Revenue | $87,500 | $122,500 | $175,000 |
| B expenses | $144,599 | $151,239 | $254,227 |
| B net | -$57,099 | -$28,739 | -$79,227 |
| C: grant-funded capex, $175/hd. Expenses | $127,828 | $134,468 | $237,456 |
| C net | -$40,328 | -$12,563 | -$62,456 |
| D: grant capex + fees $200 to $240/hd. Revenue | $100,000 | $154,000 | $240,000 |
| D net | -$27,828 | +$17,310 | +$2,544 |

Year 3 losses jump because both butchers go full time. Quotes: "The MSU will likely not be profitable for many years, if ever" and "It is unlikely that another entrepreneur could sustain several years of losses and extremely small profit margins that are typical for inspected mobile slaughter services." The projected subsidy was "$25-50K plus a year."

## Takeaways for the Virginia plan [inferred]
- At 2022 costs, fee-only break-even for a USDA MSU was about $237/head at 1,000 head/year **with free capital**. Carrying a $550k unit on debt pushes that well above $250/head.
- Labor stepping up to full-time is the main cost driver. Utilization (leasing out idle days) is the lever.
- The plan depends on a fixed-facility cut-and-wrap partner with spare capacity. Seasonal bottlenecks there are named as a risk.
- Financial appendices B/C/D (xlsx) on the NMPAN MSU Financials page have line-item start-up costs. They were not extracted here.

## Merged from duplicate extractions (contrarian and case-studies agents)

Three agents extracted this PDF independently. Their figures agree on every number in the tables above. Content only the other two captured:

- **It is not a farm-to-farm unit.** The MSU would dock mainly at RGF's Central Point HQ, and producers would haul animals to it. The nearest inspected plants were 3-4 hours away. Much of the transport saving the plan relies on assumes this hub siting.
- **Funder:** RGF is a 501(c)(3) demonstration farm funded by the Alumbra Innovations Foundation (Christy Walton). It has land, a ~100-head herd and an existing inspected retail business. Even with all that, the unit did not cash-flow.
- **Bookings and seasonality:** in 2020-2022 "some facilities [were] booking out 12 months in advance". An unpublished 2020 survey of Oregon red-meat processors put average slaughter bookings at about 6 months. July-August is jammed with 4-H fair animals. The plan recommends getting producers to finish in the off season (January-June) and warns that cut-and-wrap partners need "the time, space and labor" for the summer-fall surge.
- **Subsidized competition:** regional plants were expanding with "significant federal funding". Masami Foods planned to double fee-for-service clients from 5-10 to 10-20 farmers, and Diamond S Meat Co. was moving from custom-exempt to inspection. This is presumably among the "other developments in the region" NMPAN cites for the no-go [inferred].
- **SWOT threats:** liability insurance with outside producers on the property; "Shortage of meat cutters and labor more broadly"; "Not enough committed slaughter contracts"; not enough retail or DTC outlets; failing to raise funds. Internal weakness: "RGF has no hands on experience with a MSU and neither has any of the staff."
- **Lead butcher scope:** kill, HACCP, SOPs, sanitation, training, QA and USDA inspector liaison all fall on one 0.5 FTE role. Workaround: co-employ part-time staff with other regional slaughterhouses. Suggested exit: lease or lease-to-own the unit to a cut-and-wrap partner.
- "Slaughter is also the riskiest and least-profitable part of most meat processing businesses."
- **Staffing discrepancy:** one extraction lists "a lead butcher and two assistant butchers"; the other two list one assistant. Unresolved without re-reading the PDF.
- **Compost:** it cannot be sold unless RGF becomes a permitted commercial composting site.
