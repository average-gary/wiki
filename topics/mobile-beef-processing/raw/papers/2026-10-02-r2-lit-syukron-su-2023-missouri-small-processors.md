# Understanding small- and very-small-scale meat processors in Missouri (Syukron & Su, 2023)

**Evidence type:** peer-reviewed journal article (Journal of Agriculture, Food Systems, and Community Development, 12(2), 185-200; open access, CC BY). Lincoln University of Missouri; funded by USDA NIFA Evans-Allen 1024566 and AFRI 2022-67024-36111. Small sample (N=26), descriptive statistics only.

## Method
- Online (Qualtrics), mail, and structured phone survey, May 2021 to March 2022. 26 responses, 19.21% response rate.
- Size definitions: "small" = fewer than 750 employees (SBA); "very small" = fewer than 200 employees (authors' definition, note this differs from the FSIS HACCP very-small definition of fewer than 10 employees or under $2.5M sales).
- Context: as of 2021, Missouri had 217 meat/poultry slaughter or processing facilities, 161 USDA-inspected and 56 state-inspected; 90 slaughtering and 107 processing (Missouri Agricultural and Small Business Development Authority, 2021).

## Business profile
- 25 of 26 family-owned; 80.77% LLC/LLP/partnership.
- 61.54% operated at full capacity; 38.46% below full capacity.
- Average 7 full-time-equivalent employees; minimum 2, maximum 40.
- Gross sales: eight firms $300,000-$1,000,000; three firms under $50,000.
- 21 of 26 processed cattle and hogs. Cattle per year: max 1,700 head, min 75. Hogs: max 1,500, min 4. Sheep/lamb max 1,000; goats max 25.
- Revenue share by species (mean): cattle 57.26%, hogs 28.67%, game 21.29%, poultry 8.33%, other 5.50%, sheep 4.76%, goats 3.58%.
- 19 of 26 offered custom processing; 12 offered custom labeling for resale.
- Half of the 26 were state-inspected; the other half federally inspected or custom-exempt.

## Supply geography
- Animals came mainly from the plant's own county or adjacent counties; contiguous states supplied on average 20% of animals. Authors attribute this to transport cost (citing Gwin et al., 2013).

## Marketing and channels
- In-store retail used by 73.08%, and it was 72.00% of revenue for individual processors. Phone/mail-in retail used by 42.31% (8.74% of sales); online by 23.08% (17.50% of sales).
- Word-of-mouth used by 76.92%, social media 53.85%, websites 42.31%.
- 23 of 26 sold direct to consumers. Five named Walmart as a top competitor; ALDI also mentioned.

## Key quotes and findings
- "Using their service reservation systems, many of the processors were booked at least one year ahead."
- "it takes six months to a year for a meat processor to train a skilled worker." Some processors said retention requires "more competitive wages, between US$15 and US$20 per hour." BLS 2021 national average for animal slaughtering and processing: $15.31/hr.
- Seasonality: supply "high in the hunting season but low in other periods" (citing Gwin et al., 2013); no-shows and cancelled appointments cause undersupply.
- COVID effect: 44% better, 32% much better, 12% worse or much worse; two of the three "worse" firms had a 5/5 labor problem.
- Regulation: complex and costly; cites Ollinger & Moore (2009) that compliance costs more for small, diversified plants than large ones.

## Relevance to a Shenandoah Valley mobile beef unit [inferred]
- Labor, not demand, is the binding constraint for existing small plants; a mobile unit with 2-3 trained staff faces the same 6-12 month training lag and wage pressure.
- The one-year booking backlog in 2021-22 is the demand signal a mobile custom-exempt service would target, but it is a Missouri, pandemic-era figure.
- Supply is local (own and adjacent county), which supports a county-radius service model.
