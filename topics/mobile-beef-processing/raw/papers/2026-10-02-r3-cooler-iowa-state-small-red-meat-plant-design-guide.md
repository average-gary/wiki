# Guide to Designing a Small Red Meat Plant (Iowa State PM 2077, 2009)

How it was retrieved: the meatsci.osu.edu link now returns HTML. The PDF came from web.archive.org (`/web/2020id_/`). NMPAN's "Plant Design and Construction" page (https://www.nichemeatprocessing.org/plant-design-and-construction/) links it as "Red Meat Plant Design Guide (from Iowa State, published in 2009)".

## NMPAN framing (current page, fetched 2026-10-02)
- "a very general rule of thumb is about $300/sq ft" to build a meat processing plant. The page gives no date [inferred: current guidance, roughly 2020s].
- The page also links Kennedy/Jenks water quantity and quality tables (Nov 2013), in gallons per animal.
- The guide carries a caution: "not intended for direct construction use."

## Plant capacities and utilities (p. 12)
| | Small plant | Large plant |
|---|---|---|
| Dimensions | 50'x50' + 10'x10' | 70'x75' |
| Size | 2,600 sq ft | 5,250 sq ft |
| Holding cooler | 13 beef | 20 beef |
| Pre-chill cooler | 7 beef | 10 beef |
| Employees | 3-4 | 6-10 |
| Water | 150-200 gal per beef equivalent, average | same |
| Price per sq ft | ~$100 depending on materials (2009) | same |

- "Cooler space for one beef will provide space for 1.5 to 2 hogs, sheep, or goats."
- Heating: you can reclaim hot water heat from the refrigeration compressors.

## Refrigeration room specifications (small plant, p. 38)
Columns: room, size, ceiling/wall/floor R-value, workers in room, product temp in, product temp out, remarks.
- **108 Pre-chill cooler**, 8'x14'6"x14': R-30/23/10, 0 workers, product in 100°F, out 36°F. "Cool 5000# product in 24 hours."
- **109 Holding cooler**, 14'x14'6"x14': R-30/23/10, product in 40°F, out 34°F. "Hold product at 34°F, up to 15000# product."
- 112 Curing cooler, 8'6"x10'x10': hold at 34-36°F.
- 116 Offal cooler, 8'x8'x10': run at 50°F, 3,000 lb in 48 h.
- 101 Freezer, 14'x9'x10': R-40 floor. Run at 0-10°F, freezes 2,000 lb in 24 h.
- The processing room runs at 55°F.
- Notes: "Need to supply electrical needs for refrigeration to electrical contractor." Refrigeration lines should be "insulated and covered with PVC or other sealed vapor barrier." "Condensate drains for refrigeration need to be 2" diameter lines." Each cooler has one floor drain.

## Large plant (p. 62)
- 116 Pre-chill cooler, 11'6"x16'x14': 100°F to 40°F, "Cool 6000# in 24 hours."
- 117 Holding cooler, 11'x28'x14' (11'x18' in the plumbing schedule): 40°F to 34°F, "Hold 20000# at 34-36°F."
- Freezer 23'x35'x10': holds at 0°F.

## Finishes and doors
- Carcass coolers (pre-chill and holding) have **14 ft** ceilings. Curing, product and offal coolers have 10 ft ceilings.
- Finishes: concrete floor, PVC cove base, FRP walls and ceilings.
- Doors: the holding cooler has a 5'-0" x 11'-8" steel double-swing door, and the pre-chill cooler has a 5'-0" x 10'-6" insulated door. These heights clear carcass rails [inferred].
- Evaporator blockouts are 32"x32".

## Design checklist (pp. 12-13)
- "Minimum support column spacing for carcass rails."
- "Are the rails in slaughter and coolers located at an adequate height for the purpose intended?"
- "Do carcass rails lead all the way into the processing areas...?"
- "Do the coolers have drains in them and are they adequately sized?"

## Derived sizing ratios [inferred, computed from the tables above]
- Small holding cooler: 203 sq ft / 13 beef = about **15.6 sq ft per beef** (whole carcass as two sides). 15,000 lb / 13 = about 1,150 lb per beef.
- Small pre-chill: 116 sq ft / 7 beef = about 16.6 sq ft per beef. The pre-chill has to pull about 5,000 lb of warm product down in 24 h.
- Large holding (11x28): 308 sq ft / 20 beef = about 15.4 sq ft per beef.
- So **about 15-17 sq ft of floor per hanging beef** at a 14 ft ceiling, including aisle.
- At 14 d aging, cooler beef capacity ≈ weekly kills x 2 (+ pre-chill) [inferred].
- The guide does not give a rail height or linear-feet-per-side rule.
