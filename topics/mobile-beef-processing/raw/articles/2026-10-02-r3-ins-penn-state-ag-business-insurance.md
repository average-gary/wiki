# Penn State Extension: Agricultural Business Insurance, and Additional Specialty Products Insurance

Second document: https://extension.psu.edu/additional-specialty-products-insurance (updated September 8, 2025). Authors: Kime and McGee (Penn State) with independent agents Faubel (Danner's Insurance) and Garcia-Taylor (Garcia-Taylor Insurance Agency). The specialty guide was funded by USDA RMA Risk Management Education Partnerships, Agreement 14-IE-53102-036. Both are written for farms, not meat plants. They are used here for coverage types only. **Neither gives dollar premiums.**

## Agricultural Business Insurance (updated April 30, 2019)

- **Cost weight:** "As insurance will be one of your top ten expenses, you should explore all your options." Coverage options and premiums "may vary considerably from one company to another."
- **Core business lines:** "general liability, product liability, business property, vehicle, and employee dishonesty." Policies pay only up to their limits: "a $1 million liability policy will only pay up to the $1 million limit."
- **General liability:** "traditionally covers bodily injury and property damage, personal injury, and product liability." Typical exclusions include motor-vehicle damage. Injuries to employees fall under workers' comp, not liability.
- **Product liability:** "Most farm owners' policies include a limited amount of product liability insurance. However, if you direct market any portion of your production, you should consider increasing your level of product liability insurance." The premium "is based on the amount of sales and the owner's experience in producing the product if you have any value-added enterprises."
- **Workers' compensation:**
  - Arrange it before hiring, including for part-time and seasonal staff and volunteers.
  - Self-insuring is "generally not recommended."
  - State-sponsored coverage is "traditionally more expensive" than private coverage, but "private companies may elect not to cover a start-up business because the company does not have a history of employing workers."
  - Trade associations may offer cheaper group workers' comp.
  - The premium is based on "the type of operation, kinds of work your employees do, and amount of your annual estimated payroll". Example given: $20,000 payroll versus $150,000. Office staff cost less per unit of pay.
  - Experience and claims history change the premium. The carrier audits payroll each year and bills any additional premium immediately.
  - Sole proprietors and partners are not covered. Corporate owners may elect coverage.
- **Vehicles:**
  - "Any vehicle licensed by a state should be insured." The insurer may require copies of every driver's license, and some states require a CDL for large trucks.
  - Vehicles may have to be owned by the business rather than the individual.
  - "For commercial vehicles, cargo coverage is required for the contents or load the vehicle is carrying."
  - "Unless specifically listed on the policy, your vehicle policy will not cover animals transported in a truck or trailer."
- **Equipment:** scheduled equipment can be itemized or grouped into a blanket value. Umbrella or blanket policies can raise limits.

## Additional Specialty Products Insurance (updated September 8, 2025)

- **Temperature Sensitive Farm Personal Property:** "broadens coverage on refrigerated farm products and supplies ... in that it covers products rendered unsafe to sell due to refrigeration problems. Examples of covered events would be electrical interruptions, mechanical breakdowns or electrical breakdowns of a refrigeration system that result in spoiled product." It pays for the perishable product and "the loss of revenues for the product." To apply you supply equipment details, the inventory levels you routinely hold, and their value.
- **Disruption of income:** pays part of the anticipated income after a fire or similar event until operations resume. You need records of past sales.
- **Peak season endorsement:** raises coverage while inventory is temporarily high.
- **Ordinance and law:** pays the extra rebuild cost of meeting current code. Typically adds 10% of the dwelling value.

## Relevance to the Virginia plan [inferred]

- The cooler-spoilage coverage the plan needs corresponds to a commercial "spoilage / equipment breakdown" endorsement. On a farm policy it is the temperature-sensitive endorsement described here. Coverage depends on stated inventory value, so seasonal fall peaks argue for a peak-season endorsement.
- Under a custom-exempt model the carcasses belong to customers. A spoilage endorsement on the business's own property may not respond to them. That needs bailee / care-custody-control coverage, which these guides do not address.
- Livestock carried on the kill truck or trailer is excluded unless listed. Customers' animals arriving dead or dying would need cargo or animal coverage confirmed.
