# 9 CFR Part 416 - Sanitation (416.1-416.6)

Authority: 21 U.S.C. 451-470, 601-695. Source: 61 FR 38868 (July 25, 1996). The Sanitation Performance Standards text in 416.1-416.6 dates from 64 FR 56417 (Oct. 20, 1999). Fetched from the eCFR renderer API (current text, 2026-10-02).

**Why it applies to a custom-exempt cooler:** 9 CFR 303.1(a)(2)(i), covered in round 1, conditions the custom exemption on the operation being maintained per 416.1-416.6. The text below says "official establishment" and "FSIS program employee", but for custom-exempt plants FSIS (or VDACS OMPS in Virginia, which runs the state program) applies the same standards in periodic reviews [inferred from 303.1 cross-reference].

## § 416.1 General rules
"Each official establishment must be operated and maintained in a manner sufficient to prevent the creation of insanitary conditions and to ensure that product is not adulterated."

## § 416.2 Establishment grounds and facilities
- **(a) Grounds and pest control:** grounds maintained to prevent insanitary conditions; a pest management program is required. Pesticides must be safe and must not be applied or stored in a way that adulterates product.
- **(b) Construction:**
  - (1) Sound construction, kept in good repair, and "of sufficient size" for processing, handling and storage.
  - (2) "Walls, floors, and ceilings ... must be built of durable materials impervious to moisture" and be cleaned and sanitized.
  - (3) Openings constructed to keep out vermin.
  - (4) Edible product rooms "separate and distinct" from inedible product rooms, "to the extent necessary" to prevent adulteration.
- **(c) Light:** "of good quality and sufficient intensity" in processing, storage, examination, equipment-cleaning, hand-washing, dressing/locker and toilet areas. No lux or foot-candle number is given. (Older prescriptive FSIS guidance cited numbers, but they are not in the rule.)
- **(d) Ventilation:** adequate to control "odors, vapors, and condensation". For a cooler and cut room, condensation drip is the main concern [inferred].
- **(e) Plumbing:** must carry enough water; convey sewage and liquid waste; provide "adequate floor drainage in all areas where floors are subject to flooding-type cleaning"; prevent backflow and cross-connections between wastewater and product-water piping; and prevent sewer-gas backup.
- **(f) Sewage disposal:** must go into a sewage system separate from all other drainage lines, or be handled by other means that prevent backup. "When the sewage disposal system is a private system requiring approval by a State or local health authority, the establishment must furnish FSIS with the letter of approval from that authority upon request." For a rural Frederick County site this means a VDH onsite sewage/septic approval letter [inferred].
- **(g) Water:**
  - (1) Running water complying with the National Primary Drinking Water regulations (40 CFR part 141), at suitable temperature and pressure, for processing, cleaning and employee sanitary facilities. A municipal supply needs a water report from the state or local health agency. "If an establishment uses a private well for its water supply, it must make available to FSIS, upon request, documentation certifying the potability of the water supply that has been renewed at least semi-annually."
  - (2)-(6) Rules on reusing water, ice and solutions. Reconditioned water may be used on raw product (not formulation) with a separate potable final rinse. Non-potable water may not contact edible product.
- **(h) Dressing rooms, lavatories, toilets:** "sufficient in number, ample in size, conveniently located". They must be separate from product rooms. Lavatories need running hot and cold water, soap and towels, and must be placed near toilets and "at such other places ... as necessary". Refuse receptacles must be sanitary.

## § 416.3 Equipment and utensils
Equipment must be made of materials and construction that allow thorough cleaning, kept sanitary, and not placed where it prevents inspection. Inedible receptacles must be "conspicuous[ly] and distinctive[ly]" marked and never used for edible product.

## § 416.4 Sanitary operations
Food-contact and non-food-contact surfaces must be cleaned and sanitized "as frequently as necessary". Chemicals must be safe and effective, and documentation substantiating safety in a food environment must be available for review. Product must be protected during processing, storage, loading, unloading and transport.

## § 416.5 Employee hygiene
Hygienic practices are required. Outer clothing must be disposable or readily cleaned, with clean garments at the start of each day. Workers with infectious disease or open lesions must be excluded.

## § 416.6 Tagging
FSIS attaches a "U.S. Rejected" tag to insanitary equipment or rooms, which may not be used until made acceptable.

## §§ 416.11-416.17 (context)
Written Sanitation SOPs are required of "each official establishment". These sections are outside the 416.1-416.6 range that 303.1 imposes on custom-exempt operations, so a custom-exempt cooler is not federally required to keep a written SSOP. It is still good practice and VDACS may expect one [inferred].

## Implications for a cooler-only hanging/aging site (mobile unit kills, cuts and packages) [inferred]
- 416.2(b) still applies to the cooler: impervious, cleanable walls, floor and ceiling (insulated metal panel, sealed or coated concrete floor), vermin-tight doors, and rails and hooks that can be cleaned (416.3).
- 416.2(d): ventilation and refrigeration sized to control condensation. Drip onto hanging carcasses is the typical cooler finding.
- 416.2(b)(4): if inedibles (heads, hides, offal barrels) are staged on site, keep them out of the carcass cooler.
- 416.2(e)-(h): if no product is handled outside the cooler, plumbing needs are mainly a floor drain and a hand-wash point for workers moving carcasses. If the mobile unit docks there to cut, the site has to supply potable water (40 CFR 141; for a private well, potability documentation renewed at least semi-annually) and approved disposal for wash water (a VDH approval letter for a private system on request), plus toilet and lavatory access for crew (416.2(h)).
- 416.4(d): carcasses must be protected while they are loaded and unloaded between the mobile unit and the cooler, so a covered dock or enclosed transfer is advisable.
