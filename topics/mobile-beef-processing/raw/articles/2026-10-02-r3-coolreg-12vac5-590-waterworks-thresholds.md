# 12VAC5-590-10: Virginia Waterworks Regulations definitions

Statutory authority: Va. Code §§ 32.1-12, 32.1-170, 32.1-174.5. The historical notes list the latest amendments as 42:5 VA.R. (eff. 2025-11-19) and Vol. 43 Issue 1 (eff. 2026-09-23). Fetched from law.lis.virginia.gov on 2026-10-02.

## Key definitions (verbatim)
- **"Waterworks"** means "a system that serves piped water for human consumption to at least 15 service connections or 25 or more individuals for at least 60 days out of the year." It includes storage, collection, treatment and distribution, "except the piping and fixtures inside the building where such water is delivered."
- **"Community waterworks"** means one that "serves at least 15 service connections used by year-round residents or regularly serves at least 25 year-round residents."
- **"Noncommunity waterworks"** means "a waterworks that is not a community waterworks, but operates at least 60 days out of the year."
- **"Nontransient noncommunity waterworks" or "NTNC"** means one "that is not a community waterworks and that regularly serves at least 25 of the same persons over six months out of the year. When used in the context of an NTNC, 'regularly serves' means four or more hours per day, for four or more days per week, for 26 or more weeks per year."
- **"Transient noncommunity waterworks" or "TNC"** means "a noncommunity waterworks that is not a nontransient noncommunity waterworks (NTNC). A TNC serves at least 25 persons daily for at least 60 days out of the year."
- **"Owner"** means anyone "that supplies or proposes to supply water to any person ... by means of any waterworks." **"Permit"** means "an authorization granted by the commissioner to construct or operate a waterworks."
- **"Service connection"**: when one owner owns both the waterworks and the building, it is "the point of entry into the building."
- **"Containment"** means backflow protection between a waterworks and a consumer's system via a backflow prevention assembly or device.

## Application to a cooler-only site, with or without mobile-unit docking [inferred]
- **Headcount test:**
  - NTNC needs 25 of the same people, regularly (4 h/day, 4 days/wk, 26 wk/yr).
  - TNC needs 25 people daily for 60+ days.
  - A cooler with 2-6 workers, plus a 2-4 person mobile crew and occasional customer pickups, is far below either threshold. The well is therefore a private well under the VDH Private Well Regulations (12VAC5-630; not fetched this round), not a permitted waterworks. No waterworks operating permit, licensed operator or ODW monitoring schedule applies.
  - Threshold to watch: if retail pickup days bring 25+ people per day for 60+ days per year, the system could become a TNC.
- **Federal sanitation still applies:** 9 CFR 416.2(g)(1) requires water meeting 40 CFR 141 and, for private wells, potability documentation "renewed at least semi-annually". In practice this means a bacteriological (total coliform/E. coli) test every 6 months, kept on file for VDACS.
- **Wash water when the mobile unit docks to cut:** the trailer's potable supply would be filled from the site well. A backflow preventer on the fill point is needed under 416.2(e)(5) and the containment concept above.
- **Wastewater (not resolved this round):** wash-down water from cutting is process wastewater. Whether VDH (Sewage Handling and Disposal Regulations, 12VAC5-610/613) or DEQ (VPA permit for industrial wastewater) governs disposal on site, and whether a holding tank and hauling is acceptable, needs a separate source. Frederick County's ban on pump-and-haul for RA restaurants (165-401.03 E) suggests the county may resist hauling.
