# FSA Farm Loan Programs: Is a Slaughter Service or MSU an Eligible Purpose?

The regulation text below was pulled from the eCFR versioner (point-in-time 2026-09-01). The latest amendment cited is 89 FR 65039–65041, Aug. 8, 2024.

## Operating loan uses: 7 CFR 764.251(a)

Operating loans (OL), and microloans (ML) used for OL purposes, "may only be used for":
1. (1) Reorganizing a farm to improve its profitability.
2. (2) "Purchase of livestock, including poultry, farm equipment or fixtures, quotas and bases, and cooperative stock for credit, production, processing or marketing purposes".
3. (3) Farm operating expenses.
4. (4) Scheduled payments on FO and OL term debt.
5. (5) "Other farm needs".
6. (6) Land and water development.
7. (7) Closing costs.
8. (8) OSHA compliance.
9. (9) Borrower training.
10. (10) Limited refinancing.
11. (11) Minor real-estate repairs, "provided the loan is made primarily for agricultural purposes and can be repaid within 7 years".

## The bar: 7 CFR 764.102(f)

> "Loan funds will not be used to establish or support a non-eligible enterprise, even if the non-eligible enterprise contributes to the farm."

## Definitions: 7 CFR 761.2

The **non-eligible enterprise** definition is met by any one of four categories. The two that apply here are:

> "(iii) Primarily markets goods or provides services which might be agriculturally related, but are not produced by the farming operation; or
> (iv) Processes or markets farm products when the majority of the commodities processed or marketed are not produced by the farming operation."

- **Family farm** means a business that "produces agricultural commodities ... for sale in sufficient quantities so that it is recognized as a farm rather than a rural residence or non-eligible enterprise".
- **Farm** "may also include land and improvements and facilities used in a non-eligible enterprise". In other words, owning such a facility does not disqualify the farm itself, but FSA money cannot go into it.

## Who can borrow

- **7 CFR 764.101(e), credit elsewhere:** the applicant must be "unable to obtain sufficient credit elsewhere" at reasonable rates and terms. This test applies to all programs except Conservation Loans (CL).
- **764.101(i), managerial ability:**
  - The general routes are a farm-related degree, on-the-job training, or at least one full production cycle as a farm operator.
  - Microloan applicants have extra routes: a 4-H/FFA or beginning-farmer program record, or a self-directed apprenticeship combined with farm or "significant small business management experience".
- **764.101(k), family-farm operator:** the applicant must operate a family farm after the loan closes.
- **764.252(d), entities:** an entity must be "controlled by farmers engaged primarily and directly in farming".
- **764.252(e), term limit:** a borrower may close direct OLs in no more than 7 calendar years. Beginning farmers are exempt. Microloans to beginning or veteran farmers do not count toward the limit.
- **761.2, beginning farmer:** has not operated a farm for more than 10 years. Other criteria are in the full definition.

## Dollar limits: 7 CFR 761.8(a)

| Loan type | Limit |
|---|---|
| Direct OL | $400,000 |
| Guaranteed OL | $1,750,000 (FY2019 base, indexed each year by the Prices Paid by Farmers Index) |
| Direct FO | $600,000 |
| Emergency | $500,000 |

Combined caps also apply. The microloan cap is in a separate section and was not pulled here. The commonly cited figure is $50,000 [not verified in this fetch].

## Application to a Shenandoah Valley MSU [inferred]

- **Fee-for-service mobile slaughter LLC:** most animals it kills belong to other producers, so it falls squarely under 761.2 categories (iii) and (iv). It is **ineligible** for FSA OL, ML or FO money.
- **Cattle operator who buys a trailer mainly for their own herd:** this could arguably be financed as "farm equipment ... for processing or marketing purposes" under 764.251(a)(2). Two conditions:
  - Custom work for neighbours must stay a minority of throughput.
  - The farm, not the slaughter service, must be the business being financed.
- **Cooperative:** 764.251(a)(2) also lets individual farmer-members borrow OL funds to buy **cooperative stock** for processing purposes. Producers could therefore use FSA money to capitalize their equity in a producer-owned MSU co-op, as in the Island Grown Farmers Cooperative model from round 1. The co-op itself would need a different lender.
- **Bottom line:** the business entity needs commercial, SBA, B&I, MPILP or Farm Credit financing instead.
