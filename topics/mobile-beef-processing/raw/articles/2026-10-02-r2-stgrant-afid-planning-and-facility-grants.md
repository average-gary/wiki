# AFID Planning Grants and AFID Facility Grants (VDACS)

Sources read:
- Planning page: https://www.vdacs.virginia.gov/agriculture-afid-planning-grants.shtml
- Planning guidelines PDF: https://www.vdacs.virginia.gov/pdf/afid-grantsum.pdf
- Planning master list of awardees: https://www.vdacs.virginia.gov/pdf/afid-master-list-planning-grants.pdf
- Facility page: https://www.vdacs.virginia.gov/agriculture-afid-facility-grants.shtml
- Facility guidelines PDF: https://www.vdacs.virginia.gov/pdf/afid-guidelines.pdf

The legal basis is Code of Virginia Title 3.2, Chapter 3.1 (Governor's Agriculture and Forestry Industries Development Fund). The AFID Infrastructure grant was covered in round 1 and is not repeated here.

## AFID Planning Grant Program

- **Purpose:** "encourage local governments to think strategically" about agriculture and forestry economic development.
- **Maximums:** $20,000 for a single-locality applicant. $35,000 for a multi-locality applicant.
- **Timing:** "Applications will be accepted on a rolling basis." Awards come from current appropriations only.
- **Eligible activities:** strategic plans; "feasibility studies, business plans, or other predevelopment work for projects that will have a significant and lasting positive impact on the local agriculture or forestry sector"; local food system initiatives; marketing programs; ordinances; entrepreneurship and business development efforts. Capital expenditures are allowed up to $10,000.
- **Exclusions:** "Applications primarily benefiting an individual, for-profit business are not allowed." Education and youth training are also excluded.
- **Who can apply:** political subdivisions only. Grants are reimbursable. A locality may hold only one active planning grant at a time, not counting multi-locality projects.
- **Match:** 1:1. Economically Distressed Localities get a reduced 2:1 match. At least 50% of the match must be new cash. Up to 50% may be in-kind or "Other Match" (federal grants, private foundations, nonprofits, Tobacco Commission funds).
- **Governance requirement:** an agricultural board, committee, or working group must actively take part, for example an Agricultural Advisory Board or an Agricultural Development Board.
- **Term:** 2 years, with a possible 1-year extension. An interim report is due at 1 year. Up to 75% can be reimbursed before the grant period ends.
- **Contact:** the guidelines PDF lists Rachel Meyers (804-786-6911). The web page lists Joyce Blankenship (804-786-1906).

### Meat-processing precedents in the planning master list
- **Fauquier County, Oct 2022, $20,000:** "Feasibility Study for Abattoir at Fauquier Livestock Exchange", which paid a consultant to judge whether the exchange site suits an abattoir, citing nearness to Northern Virginia and DC markets.
- **Halifax County, May 2013, $12,500:** third-party feasibility study for a USDA-inspected meat processing facility for Southside.
- **Russell County, Aug 2019, $8,750:** market analysis, feasibility study, and business plan for a meat processing facility.
- **Valley planning grants:** Shenandoah County Agriculture and Forestry Economic Development Plan; Clarke County Cost of Community Services Study; Central Shenandoah PDC "Fields of Gold" agritourism ($35,000 follow-on). None found for Frederick County or Winchester.
- **Same document, infrastructure-grant meat projects:** Floyd County (a new custom-exempt facility), City of Harrisonburg (T&E Meats, USDA-inspected red-meat slaughter), Franklin County (KC Farms), and Orange County (Piedmont Processing cooler space for aging beef).

## AFID Facility Grant Program

- **Nature:** "a discretionary, performance-based economic development incentive specifically for agriculture and forestry value-added or processing projects". The program explicitly includes "meat and poultry processors."
- **Recipient:** "awarded to a political subdivision for the benefit of the company." Businesses should "contact their local economic development office or VDACS." Applying takes two letters: one from the locality's chief official and one from the business, both addressed to the Secretary.
- **Cap:** $500,000 per project. The cap can be exceeded for projects of "statewide or regional importance."
- **Not front-end money:** grants "are not intended to serve as front-end funding or financing" and are "generally paid out on a performance basis."
- **Virginia-grown requirement:** at least 30% of the inputs must be Virginia-produced in normal years. The definition of "agricultural products" includes cattle.
- **Match:** the locality matches dollar-for-dollar, in cash or in-kind (infrastructure, fee waivers, land or buildings). Funds from other government sources and private contributions do not count. Tobacco Region Opportunity Fund money can cover up to half of the match. The Governor can waive the match for projects of statewide or regional importance.
- **Scoring criteria:** new jobs; private capital investment; new state and local tax revenue; volume of Virginia-grown product used; impact on producers; ROI analysis; impact on competing local businesses.
- **New job definition:** at least 35 hours a week for 48 or more weeks, or 1,680 hours a year. Seasonal and temporary jobs do not count, though they are converted to FTEs and listed separately.
- **Allowed uses:** utilities, broadband, roads, site acquisition and preparation, building construction or build-out, and training. Rent and lease payments are not allowed.
- **Performance terms:** the performance date is generally 36 months after award. The grant is divided 33% Virginia purchases, 33% jobs, and 33% capital investment, with proportional clawback for shortfalls. A 100% clawback applies if the business will miss 50% of any one target.
- **Contact:** Michele Bridges, 804-774-6106.

## Fit for a startup mobile beef unit [inferred]
- **Planning Grant:** a for-profit startup cannot apply and cannot be the main beneficiary. Frederick County, or a multi-county group through the Northern Shenandoah Valley Regional Commission, could apply for up to $35,000 to study regional livestock processing capacity, with the mobile unit as one option. The Fauquier abattoir study is the closest precedent.
- **Facility Grant:** a startup could benefit, but only through a locality application, and only after it has invested its own capital and created full-time jobs. A 2-4 job mobile unit would likely score low on jobs and tax revenue. The 30% Virginia-grown requirement is easy to meet with local cattle.
