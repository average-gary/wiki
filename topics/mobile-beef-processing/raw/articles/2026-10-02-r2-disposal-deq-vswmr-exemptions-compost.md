# VSWMR (9VAC20-81): exemptions relevant to slaughter waste

Fetched from law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode on 2026-10-02.

## 9VAC20-81-95 C: materials that are not solid waste

"the following materials are not solid wastes for the purpose of this chapter:
1. Materials generated by any of the following, which are returned to the soil as fertilizers:
a. The growing and harvesting of agricultural crops.
b. **The raising and husbanding of animals, including animal manures and used animal bedding.**"

Paunch and rumen contents are manure-like. Whether they fall under C.1.b when land-applied, or count as Category IV "paunch manure" (see below), is ambiguous [inferred]. The two provisions point in different directions.

95 C.5.c also excludes "Compost that satisfies the applicable requirements of the Virginia Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (2VAC5-400 and 2VAC5-410)" (VDACS fertilizer and soil amendment rules).

## 9VAC20-81-95 D: conditionally exempt activities

Lead-in: "The following activities are conditionally exempt from this chapter **provided no open dump, hazard, or public nuisance is created**:"

Composting items, verbatim:
- "1. Composting of sewage sludge at the sewage treatment plant of generation ..."
- "2. Composting of household waste generated at a residence and composted at the site of generation."
- "3. Composting activities performed for educational purposes as long as no more than 100 cubic yards of materials are on site at any time ..."
- "**4. Composting of animal carcasses on site at the farm of generation.**"
- "5. Composting of vegetative waste or yard waste generated on site by owners or operators of agricultural operations ... provided: a. All decomposed vegetative waste and compost produced is utilized on that property; ... c. All applicable standards of local ordinances ... are satisfied ..."
- "7. Composting of preconsumer food waste and kitchen culls generated on site ..."
- "8. Vermicomposting ... no more than 100 cubic yards ..."

Container storage (D.10): "Management of solid waste in appropriate containers at the site of its generation, provided that: a. **Putrescible waste is not stored more than seven days** between time of collection and time of removal for disposal; b. Nonputrescible wastes are not stored more than 90 days ..."

**What this means for on-farm custom slaughter [inferred]:**
- If the mobile unit kills the farmer's animal on the farmer's land, the offal, head, and bones are arguably carcass material "generated" at that farm. Composting them on that farm fits D.4 without a DEQ permit, subject to the no-nuisance and no-open-dump condition. D.4 says "animal carcasses," not "slaughter waste," so this reading is likely but not certain. Confirm with the DEQ Valley Regional Office (Harrisonburg).
- If the operator **collects offal from several farms and composts it at a central site**, the material is no longer "at the farm of generation." D.4 then does not apply, and the compost site falls under Part IV (9VAC20-81-300 et seq.) compost facility rules for Category IV feedstock.
- Offal held in barrels or totes for a renderer pickup must leave within 7 days (D.10.a).
- Section 95 contains no burial exemption. Searches for "bur", "burial", and "bury" found nothing. On-farm burial of carcasses is not addressed here. It falls back to § 18.2-510 and DEQ/VDH guidance on water protection (open question).
- 95 E.1 exempts "Management of wastes regulated by the State Board of Health, the State Water Control Board, ... Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services, or any other state or federal agency with such authority." This covers wash water and blood handled under a VPA/VPDES or VDH permit.

## 9VAC20-81-310: compost feedstock categories (Part IV)

Part IV applies to facilities "producing compost from municipal solid waste/refuse or combinations of municipal solid waste/refuse with animal manures." Categories:
- **Category I**: plant or plant-derived preconsumer materials.
- **Category II**: animal-derived waste material.
- **Category III**: "Animal and postconsumer food wastes with pathogen potential," including "(2) Animal manures including ... livestock feedlot, holding pen and cage scrapings, dairy manure semi-solids, poultry litter and manure; and (3) **Rendered animals**."
- **Category IV**: "Other wastes such as: (1) **Nonrendered animal meat waste including but not limited to animal carcasses, slaughterhouse waste, paunch manure**; ..."

A permitted commercial composter that accepts slaughter offal must therefore be approved for Category IV, the most heavily regulated class [inferred from the category ladder; the Part IV permit tiers were not read in detail].

## 9VAC20-170 (correction)

Chapter 170 is titled "Transportation of Solid and Medical Wastes on State Waters." It is not a composting regulation. Virginia's composting rules are in Part IV of 9VAC20-81 (and 9VAC20-81-397 for yard waste).
