# A Guide to Selling Meat and Poultry Products in Virginia

The PDF is 4 pages from VDACS Office of Meat and Poultry Services, PO Box 1163, Richmond, VA 23218, tel. 804.786.4569, fax 804.371.7792. Its creation date is May 20, 2025.

## What must be inspected

- "The production of meat or poultry products is required to be conducted under inspection unless an exemption allows otherwise. Exemptions still have requirements that must be met; it is not a complete exclusion from all inspection."
- "All 'amenable' species i.e., Cattle, swine, sheep, goats, and poultry (including ratites) slaughtered and offered for sale must be inspected." "Products offered for wholesale must be manufactured from inspected and passed carcasses."
- Nonamenable species such as rabbits and bison do not require OMPS or USDA inspection, but "regulatory oversite is still required". Producers may request fee-based **voluntary inspection**. Rabbits can instead go through the VA Rabbit Program at the Office of Food & Dairy, 804.786.3520.

## Who can sell at farmers' markets, to restaurants, to retail and online

"Anyone who has had their products processed at a USDA/FSIS or VDACS/OMPS inspected facility or has applied and received a grant of inspection from USDA/FSIS or VDACS/OMPS." In addition:
- "People selling red meat may be required to apply for a **Meat Handlers Permit**. Please contact OMPS 804.786.4569 for more information."
- "Exempt poultry may not be sold across state lines." Exempt poultry labels should carry "Exempt P.L. 90-492."
- Businesses inspected by the Virginia Department of Health and/or the VDACS Office of Dairy and Foods may sell their products.

## Inspection legends and approved sources

- "Only meat and poultry products produced under a Federal or State Grant of Inspection will have labels with inspection legends." "Products inspected under State Inspection cannot be sold across state lines." The legend number identifies the facility that produced the product.
- **Restaurants and caterers** are inspected by the local health department. "Only meat and poultry coming from an approved source (inspected) can be used in meals prepared by these businesses."
- **Retail stores** are inspected by the local health department and/or the Office of Dairy and Foods. All meat sold there "must come from an approved source (inspected)". Stores "may further process the inspected meat and poultry."

## Getting inspection

- The facility operator must apply for a grant of inspection, and applying "agrees to follow all regulatory guidelines". The guide links to `vdacs.virginia.gov/pdf/inspectionguide.pdf`, an older URL. See the separate applying-for-grant-inspection.pdf extraction.
- Facility requirements are in 9 CFR. They "are generalized and may not address specifics for your type of operation. OMPS can provide basic guidance for the construction of processing and slaughter plants."
- The facility must be physically separate from living quarters: "Inspectors, products, equipment, and supplies cannot move through any portion of the area used as living quarters."

## Routing questions (which agency to contact)

1. Buying inspected carcasses or meat and selling at your own retail store: Office of Food and Dairy plus the local health department.
2. Ready-to-eat entrees sold direct to consumers: local health department if not packaged, labeled and chilled; if packaged, also the Office of Food and Dairy.
3. Catering: local health department.
4. Central kitchen or vending: same as item 2.

Footnote: product "Must start with inspected and passe[d] product or raw product prepared at a retail exempt store."

## Advice to would-be sellers

- "Check with your local city or county for zoning requirements."
- "Consider writing a business plan ... before investing money on equipment and facilities."
- "Contact OMPS to receive a directory of existing facilities. Some of these offer slaughter and processing services."
- Links: Virginia Business One Stop; NMPAN (nichemeatprocessing.org/get-started).

## Notable omission

The guide does **not** discuss custom-exempt slaughter, animal-share sales or "sold-on-the-hoof" beef. [inferred] Custom-exempt meat is not a retail channel under this guide. Its only legal outlet is the owner's household, through the 9 CFR 303.1(a)(2) permit of exemption in 2VAC5-210-30 and the round-1 VDACS custom-exemption guide. Meat sold at farmers' markets, to restaurants or to stores must carry a VA or USDA legend.
