# 21 CFR 589.2001 and 9 CFR 310.22

Both fetched from the eCFR (current) on 2026-10-02.

## 21 CFR 589.2001: Cattle materials prohibited in animal food or feed (CMPAF)

History: [73 FR 22756, Apr. 25, 2008, as amended at 81 FR 5596, Feb. 3, 2016]

**(b)(1) CMPAF includes:**
- (i) "The entire carcass of BSE-positive cattle;"
- (ii) "**The brains and spinal cords of cattle 30 months of age and older;**"
- (iii) "The entire carcass of cattle not inspected and passed for human consumption ... that are 30 months of age or older from which brains and spinal cords were not effectively removed or otherwise effectively excluded from animal feed;"
- (iv) mechanically separated beef derived from (i)-(iii);
- (v) tallow derived from (i)-(iii).
- (vi) Excluded from CMPAF: tallow derivatives, and tallow from (ii)/(iii) with **no more than 0.15 percent insoluble impurities** (AOCS Ca 3a-46).

**(b)(2)** "Cattle not inspected and passed for human consumption means cattle that did not pass antemortem inspection by the appropriate regulatory authority. This term includes nonambulatory disabled cattle."

[inferred] A custom-exempt kill has no antemortem inspection by a regulatory authority. A renderer may therefore treat a whole custom-exempt 30-month-and-older carcass or its parts as (b)(1)(iii) material unless the brain and spinal cord were removed or excluded. Removing the head and spinal cord in the field and keeping them separate lets the rest of the offal stay feed-eligible. This reading is not confirmed by FDA guidance. See the open questions below.

**(b)(4) Renderer** "means any firm or individual that processes slaughter byproducts, animals unfit for human consumption, or meat scraps. The term includes persons who collect such materials and subject them to minimal processing, or distribute them to firms other than renderers ..." [inferred: a mobile operator that only hauls its own offal to a renderer is a supplier, not a renderer; one that collects and redistributes material could fall within "persons who collect such materials".]

**(c)(1)** "No animal feed or feed ingredient shall be manufactured from, processed with, or otherwise contain, cattle materials prohibited in animal feed."

**(c)(2) Renderers handling CMPAF must:** exclude 30-month-and-older non-inspected carcasses unless brain and spinal cord are removed or excluded; keep written procedures for removal and age-sorting; prevent cross-contamination with separate equipment or containers; label "**Do not feed to animals**"; mark with "an agent that can be readily detected on visual inspection"; and keep tracking records.

**(c)(3) Renderers receiving any cattle materials** must keep records showing that feed-use material contains no CMPAF. Where suppliers segregate, the records may be "(A) Certification or other documentation from the supplier ... provided that it includes a description of the segregation procedures used ..." with periodic review by the renderer, or "(B) ... third-party certification." Renderers must also comply with § 589.2000 (the ruminant feed ban).

**(d)** Failure makes the feed adulterated (FD&C Act 402(a)(2)-(5)) or misbranded (403(a)(1), 403(f)).
**(e)** Records must be kept **at least 1 year**.

**Business implication [inferred]:** a renderer will likely require a written supplier certification from the mobile slaughter operator describing how it separates heads and spinal cords from cattle 30 months and older. The operator needs a dentition or age-records SOP and separate, marked containers for CMPAF.

## 9 CFR 310.22: Specified risk materials (FSIS, human food)

(a) SRMs are:
- "(1) The brain, skull, eyes, trigeminal ganglia, spinal cord, vertebral column (excluding the vertebrae of the tail, the transverse processes of the thoracic and lumbar vertebrae, and the wings of the sacrum), and dorsal root ganglia from cattle 30 months of age and older and
- (2) The distal ileum of the small intestine and the tonsils from all cattle."
- (b) "Specified risk materials are inedible and prohibited for use as human food."
- (c) SRMs "must be removed from the carcasses of cattle, segregated from edible materials, and disposed of in accordance with § 314.1 or § 314.3 ... The spinal cord from cattle 30 months of age and older must be removed from the carcass at the establishment where the animal was slaughtered."
- (d) The small intestine may be used for food only if the animal was inspected and passed and at least **80 inches** of distal ileum is removed.

Note: 310.22 is written for official (inspected) establishments. Whether and how SRM removal applies at custom-exempt slaughter (via 9 CFR 303.1) was not confirmed. A grep of the current 303.1 text for "specified risk" and "310.22" found no match. Keep as an open question. The FDA feed rule (589.2001) still applies at the renderer no matter who slaughtered the animal.

The FDA rule's age cut (brain and spinal cord, 30 months and older) is narrower than the FSIS human-food SRM list (which adds skull, eyes, vertebral column, DRG, tonsils, and distal ileum). In practice, renderers often handle the whole head and backbone of older cattle as CMPAF [inferred; not verified].
