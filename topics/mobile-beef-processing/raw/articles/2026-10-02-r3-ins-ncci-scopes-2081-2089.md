# NCCI Scopes Classifications 2081 and 2089 (via NMPAN)

Companion page: https://www.nichemeatprocessing.org/scopes-classification-2089-packing-house-all-operations/

NMPAN's framing: "The following is the technical classification for worker's compensation classification codes as written by the National Council on Compensation Insurance (NCCI). Last updated December 2008. All codes are subject to change. Contact your state worker's compensation bureau for the most up to date information."

## Code 2081 - "BUTCHERING"

- **Phraseology:** "Includes the handling of livestock, preparation of dressed meat, rendering, washing of casings, salting of hides or cooking of offal."
- **Cross-references:** Slaughtering and Stockyard-and-Butchering take the same footnote as Butchering.
- **Scope:** "Code 2081 covers insureds engaged in the butchering of livestock and the preparation of fresh meat. The class contemplates the handling of livestock including pen maintenance and killing room operations. The dressing, boning and chilling of beef, pork, sheep or calves for sale either in halves, quarters or individual fresh cuts are also contemplated within Code 2081 as well as rendering, washing of casings, salting of hides or cooking of offal."
- **Sausage:** "Incidental sausage manufacturing is also contemplated under this class when performed in connection with a butchering establishment that performs no other packing house operations."
- **Retail and wholesale meat stores:** Basic Manual Rule 1-D-3 (Rule IV-D-4 in the 1996 edition) decides whether a retail or wholesale meat store run by a 2081 insured can be rated separately as 8021 (wholesale) or 8031 (retail). Meat dealers that do no livestock handling or slaughter go to 8021 or 8031.
- **State variations:** 2081 does not apply in Oregon, which uses a combined code. California, Illinois, Missouri, Montana, New Jersey, and South Dakota have state-special wording. **Virginia is not listed among the variations** [inferred: Virginia follows the standard NCCI scope].

## Code 2089 - "PACKING HOUSE - ALL OPERATIONS"

- **Phraseology:** "Includes butchering or the handling of livestock. Shall not be assigned to a risk engaged in operations described by another classification unless the operations subject to 2089 are conducted as a separate and distinct business."
- **Scope:** covers fresh meat and meat products, including livestock handling, slaughter, pen maintenance, killing room, dressing, boning, chilling, "and the processing of some cuts into meat products". Examples given: cured hams, smoked tongues, bacon (including slicing), pickled or preserved cuts, frankfurters, bologna, rendering, lard refining, sausage manufacturing, viscera processing, and fertilizer manufacturing. Some products are canned or jarred.
- 2089 is not used in California or Montana.

## The 2081 vs. 2089 test (identical text on both pages)

"Although both classifications contemplate butchering or slaughtering and the handling of livestock, the packing house classification, Code 2089, is applied to those insureds whose operations involve meat processing beyond the point of cutting, boning and dressing for distribution to customers such as wholesalers, retailers, restaurants and hotels. In order to qualify for Code 2089, the risk's principal operations must be that of processing meat. Processing meat includes but is not limited to processes such as smoking or curing hams, bacon manufacturing, pickling or otherwise preserving beef or pork cuts and, in many cases, the packaging of meat products in the form that they will be subsequently sold to the consumer. Insureds that do not process meat as described above but only engage in sausage manufacturing and butchering operations that involve cutting of steaks, chops, roasts and incidental grinding of meat are assigned to Code 2081."

"Codes 2081 and 2089 - Packing House shall not be assigned to the same risk unless the operations described by the classifications are conducted as separate and distinct businesses."

## Relevance to the Virginia mobile custom-exempt plan [inferred]

- A mobile kill unit plus a fixed cooler and cut-and-wrap shop that only does carcass breakdown, steaks, roasts, grind, and possibly sausage fits squarely in **2081**. Adding smoking or curing as a principal line could move the whole risk to 2089.
- Because both codes cannot sit on one risk, a smokehouse would have to be a "separate and distinct business" to be rated separately.
- Drivers and office staff may fall under standard exception codes (for example 8810 clerical), but this page does not address that.
- The page contains no rates or loss costs.
