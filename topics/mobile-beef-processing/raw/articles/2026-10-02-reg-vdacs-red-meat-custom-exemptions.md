# A Guide to Red Meat Custom Exemptions (VDACS OMPS)

The PDF is linked from https://www.vdacs.virginia.gov/animals-meat-and-poultry.shtml. It carries no publication date. Some URLs in it point to the old FSIS "wps/portal" layout, which suggests it was written around 2019-2022 [inferred].

## The Custom Permit
- "The Office of Meat & Poultry Services (OMPS) issues Custom Permits to qualified operators. Permits are valid from the date of issue until June 30 of each year. **There is no fee for the permit.**"
- "All Custom exempt operators are required to have a valid permit." The exception is operators that already hold a Virginia State or Federal Grant of Inspection.
- A custom operator that also buys or sells inspected meat must contact the Office of Dairy and Foods (804-786-3520).
- OMPS phone: 804-786-4569 (the guide's closing lists "804.768.4569", which is likely a typo). Email: omps@vdacs.virginia.gov. Program Manager, OMPS, 102 Governor Street, Suite 133, Richmond.

## Mobile units are allowed
- Custom Slaughter Note 7: **"A custom exempt operation may use a mobile slaughter and processing unit."**
- The owner can split the work: one custom operator slaughters and a second one processes. The owner can also slaughter the animal and bring the carcass to a custom processor. Field-dressed cattle, sheep, swine and goats may be accepted, and the operator may ask for a written statement that the animal was ambulatory.

## The eight custom criteria
1. Product is only for the owner, the owner's household, nonpaying guests, and employees.
2. Records of owner names and addresses are kept, and 9 CFR 320.1 recordkeeping applies.
3. No animal unfit for human food is slaughtered.
   - Cattle that become non-ambulatory *after delivery* to the custom facility may still be slaughtered if no other unfit condition is seen (74 FR 11463).
   - Inedible material, SRMs included, must be controlled so it cannot be diverted into food (9 CFR 303.1(a)(2)(i), 303.1(b)(4), 416.2(b)(4), 416.3(c)).
   - **SRMs:** for cattle 30 months and older, "the skull, brain, trigeminal ganglia, eyes, vertebral column, spinal cord, and dorsal root ganglia." For all cattle, "the distal ileum of the small intestine and tonsils" (9 CFR 310.22).
4. Humane Methods of Livestock Slaughter Act compliance.
5. Sanitation under 9 CFR 416.1-416.6, except 416.2(g)(2)-(6). Sections 416.12-416.16 (SSOP and records) apply only when custom work is done in an official establishment.
6. No sale or donation of product. Edible articles not delivered to the owner must be denatured under 9 CFR 325.13.
7. Mark product "Not for Sale" (9 CFR 316.16).
8. In a dual-use facility, keep custom and inspected product separate by time or space. Separate rails or shelves in a shared cooler are enough.

## Notes on business models (key for a freezer-beef plan)
- No limit on how many animals an owner may have custom slaughtered.
- Note 4: "Selling livestock to a customer does not disqualify a business from the custom exemption." The operator may sell the animal before slaughter and then custom slaughter it for the new owner, with records showing the ownership transfer.
- Note 15: "There may be more than one owner of the live animal. Sharing a live animal is acceptable provided proof of ownership ... is available." Note 16: a list of the individual owners must exist **before slaughter**.
- Note 17: the sale must be priced "on live weight, price-per-head, or other quantity pertaining to the live animal." The custom operator "can only charge the owner a service fee ... not for the meat food product itself."
- Note 18: the operator may act as the owner's agent by arranging the animal purchase, slaughter, and delivery of "Not for Sale" product.
- Note 14: fat and meat trimmings from different owners may be commingled with each owner's written consent.
- Note 13: an owner may move custom product between custom facilities if it is marked "Not for Sale."
- Note 3: in a dual facility, an owner may choose to have the animal slaughtered under inspection instead.

## Personal-use exemption (owner does the work)
- No permit is needed when the owner slaughters animals of his own raising for his household.
- A buyer may use a farm's facilities to slaughter a purchased animal and it stays personal use. **"If the seller participates in the slaughter or processing activity, then the facility owner is subject to the custom exempt criteria and must have a Custom Permit."**
- "Personal use products, although uninspected, may move across State lines." They may not be sold or donated.

## Implications [inferred]
- In Virginia, a mobile custom-exempt beef operation that slaughters on the owner's farm is expressly allowed and needs only a free annual OMPS Custom Permit. The guide does not mention any state-specific mobile-unit construction standard.
- For a mobile operator, the open regulatory questions are not about OMPS. They are about wastewater, blood and offal disposal on each farm (VDH, DEQ, local health or zoning) and about SRM handling for cattle 30 months and older.
