# NMPAN: Product Costing for Meat Processors and Marketers (webinar transcript)

Presenters: **Pam Saunders** (Organic Prairie, Organic Valley's meat brand) and **Arion Thiboumery** (NMPAN co-coordinator, Iowa State University Extension Associate, VP of Lorentz Meats in Minnesota). The session was held Oct 21, 2010. The page also hosts slides and an .xls costing workbook (Spreadsheets_from_Costing_Webinar_Oct2010.xls) with a cutting test, quarter/half costing, per-piece costing and value-added costing. That workbook was not ingested.

## Break-even framing (Thiboumery)
- "the weight of the product you process times the price that you charge for it has to be greater than or equal to your cost of goods plus your overhead." To find required throughput, solve that equation for weight.
- In small plants, labor "is not a flexible price". You pay full-time wages whether or not the work is there, so treat labor as close to fixed. Packaging and spices are variable.
- Overhead covers the building, maintenance, building and equipment loans, the owner's salary, indirect salaries, trash and utilities. His preferred way to allocate it is by **labor hours** per area, not square footage.
- Increasing throughput beats cutting costs. Iowa State CIRAS example (Mike Willett): a 10% cost cut raised profit from $10 to $20, while 10% more sales raised it to $32.50.
- "if you're too busy, you're too cheap." Processors use seasonal pricing (fall costs more than spring), seasonal surcharges, and "take a spring slot, guaranteed a fall slot" rules.

## Cutter and kill productivity (quoted rules of thumb)
- **Custom beef fabrication:** "one person can do one beef from start to finish fabrication in an 8 hour period". This means about **8 man-hours per custom beef**, where custom means cut to an individual customer's spec, by quarters or sides.
- Worked small-plant day: 4 custom beef, 5 people cutting, starting at 7 am, one beef done about every 2 hours, 1-hour lunch, all 4 done by 4 pm.
- **Lorentz Meats (USDA-inspected, standardized specs):** "14 people on the cutting floor, which includes packaging and cutting", working 7 am to about 3-3:30 pm. That day they cut **27 beef**. One customer's spec "typically requires 4.5 man hours per beef". Each customer has its own measured man-hours per beef.
- Oversized carcasses (for example 1,100 lb) and small calves need different time standards.
- **Kill:** "one person can kill a beef every hour ... with clean up and start up ... 7 beef in an 8 hour day."
- Labor practices: Lorentz guarantees employees a minimum of 35 hours a week, which removes the incentive to slow down on light days. They post daily production goals and give incentives (meat or bonuses).

## Packaging cost
- "when we do packaging tests around here, depending if we're packing a lot of primals, we get somewhere around **two cents of plastic per carcass pound**, if we're packing a lot of individual retail cuts it's in that **5-6 cent neighborhood**, but we use expensive cryovac bags. Everything is vacuum-packed ... Paper wrapping would be a lot cheaper." (2010 dollars)

## The cooler is the bottleneck (key for a cooler-only fixed site)
- Nick McCann's graduate study found that small processors "stumble with capacity in their cooler". The most common cause is one or two kill days a week ("like a snake trying to eat an elephant").
- Example: a **20-beef cooler with one-week aging** can take a kill of **4 a day, 5 days a week**, with 4 cut a day. Killing 10 beef twice a week in the same cooler leaves spaces empty.
- Worked case: kill capacity 7 a day, cooler 4 a day, fabrication 5 a day (5 people at 8 man-hours each). "Clearly, the bottleneck right there is in the cooler."
- Ways to relieve the cooler without building:
  - Cut more hogs.
  - Do cull cows, which need only "about 48 hours" because the whole carcass is ground.
  - Wet-age primals in bags instead of hanging carcasses.
  - Re-rail the cooler for denser hanging.

## Saunders (marketer side), briefly
- Account for shrink: condemnations (for example 1 in 100 = 1%), the hot-to-cold carcass shrink if you are charged on hot weight, and fabrication "oops" losses. Run a cutting test to learn yields. USDA AMS daily boxed beef reports give a pricing index for primals and subprimals.

## Implications for a mobile kill-and-cut unit with a fixed hanging cooler [inferred]
- At **8 man-hours per custom beef**, a 2-person cutting crew can break about 2 head per 8-hour day. Matching a kill rate of 4-6 head a day would take roughly 4-6 cutters. Standardized specs (about 4.5 man-hours per beef) roughly double throughput.
- At about $20/hr Virginia butcher wages (BLS file), 8 man-hours is about **$160 of direct wages per custom beef**, before loading. At 4.5 man-hours it is about $90.
- Cooler sizing drives the business. Hanging capacity in head equals daily kill times aging days. A 14-day hang at 4 head a day needs about 56 rail positions. A 7-day hang needs about 28.
- With aging at the fixed cooler and cutting on the mobile unit, carcasses must go back out to the cutting unit, or the unit must park at the cooler on cut days. That second leg is not covered in this source.
