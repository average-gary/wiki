# VDH onsite sewage rules as applied to slaughter and cooler wash water

Text taken from the Virginia LIS Administrative Code on 2026-10-02. 12VAC5-613 is the AOSS regulation; its Part II applies to applications filed on or after December 7, 2011.

## 12VAC5-610: Sewage Handling and Disposal Regulations

- **Scope (12VAC5-610-30 A).** "This chapter addresses the handling and disposal of those portions of sewage flows not regulated by a [VPDES] Permit or a [VPA] Permit issued in accordance with 9VAC25-31 or 9VAC25-32, respectively." [inferred] VDH and DEQ jurisdiction are mutually exclusive by permit type. A VDH permit in turn triggers the VPA exclusion at 9VAC25-32-40(5).
- **Definition of "sewage" (12VAC5-610-120).** "water-carried and nonwater-carried human excrement, kitchen, laundry, shower, bath, or lavatory wastes separately or together with such underground, surface, storm, or other water and liquid industrial wastes as may be present from residences, buildings, vehicles, industrial establishments, or other places." [inferred] Industrial waste is in scope only as a component mixed with human or domestic wastes. A stream of blood and wash water alone does not clearly fit, and the department decides case by case.
- **Permit required (12VAC5-610-80).**
  - A: "The discharge of untreated sewage onto the land or into the waters of the Commonwealth is prohibited."
  - B: No one may discharge "treated or untreated sewage onto the land, into the soil or into the waters of the Commonwealth without a valid permit from the commissioner or ... a certificate issued by the Department of Environmental Quality."
  - C: Every building "designed for human occupancy, employment or habitation" must be served by an approved sewerage system or treatment works. [inferred] A staffed cooler building therefore needs at least a domestic system for its restroom and handwash, separate from any decision on process water.
- **Design flows (12VAC5-610-670, Table 5.1).** Values per unit:

  | Category | Flow | BOD | SS |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Dwelling, per person | 75 gpd | 0.2 lb/day | 0.2 lb/day |
  | Factories and offices, per person per 8-hr shift | 15-35 gpd | 0.03-0.07 lb | 0.03-0.07 lb |
  | Restaurants, per seat | 50 gpd | 0.2 lb | 0.2 lb |

  The table has no meat-processing, slaughter, or food-plant category. [inferred] Flow and strength for a wash-down operation must be estimated by the designer, so the work becomes engineered design.
- **Pretreatment (12VAC5-610-780 to 820).** "Pretreatment" means treatment works designed to prepare sewage for disposal in soil. The options are septic tanks (section 815), aerobic and anaerobic biological units (800, 810), and physical or chemical systems. The last must meet the Sewage Collection and Treatment Regulations, 12VAC5-580-930 through 960 (section 820 B). Septic tanks may be in series, with the first tank holding 1/2 to 2/3 of the total volume (820 A). The rule text has no grease-interceptor requirement for onsite systems.
- **Variances and deviations (12VAC5-610-660).** The design criteria apply to all onsite systems, but "deviations from these criteria may be considered by the district or local health department on a case-by-case basis."
- **Pump and haul (12VAC5-610-410 to 440, 598, 599 to 599.3).** Special permits cover emergency, temporary, and permanent pump-and-haul with permitted storage facilities. [inferred] A holding tank emptied by a licensed sewage handler is a VDH-recognized option for a low-volume cooler site. Its conditions should be confirmed with the Lord Fairfax Health District.

## 12VAC5-613: Regulations for Alternative Onsite Sewage Systems

- **Definition.** An AOSS is "a treatment works that is not a conventional onsite sewage system and does not result in a point source discharge." A conventional system is septic tank(s) to a gravity-distributed drainfield.
- **Size classes.**
  - Small AOSS: a structure with average daily sewage flow of 1,000 gpd or less.
  - Large AOSS: a structure with flow over 1,000 gpd.
- **Engineering (12VAC5-613-40 E).** "All plans and specifications for AOSSs shall be properly sealed by a professional engineer licensed in the Commonwealth ... unless such plans are prepared pursuant to an exemption." Applications under § 32.1-163.6 must include a site and soil characterization report (40 G).
- **Strength-aware design (12VAC5-613-80).**
  - Item 5: "All treatment units and treatment systems shall be designed for the anticipated receiving wastewater characteristics and peak flow."
  - Item 9: designs must "minimize noise, odor, or other nuisances at the property boundary."
  - Items 1-2 prohibit raw or partially treated sewage on the ground surface, and its exposure to insects, animals, or humans.
- **Effluent levels.**
  - TL-2: BOD5 and TSS of 30 mg/l or less.
  - TL-3: 10 mg/l or less.
  - If the designed effluent BOD5 is above 60 mg/l, septic-tank-effluent loading rates apply (80 item 10 d).
- **Vertical separation (Table 2).**
  - 18 inches or more of natural soil: septic effluent is acceptable.
  - 12 to under 18 inches: TL-2 required.
  - Under 12 inches: TL-3 plus disinfection.
- **Large AOSS extras (80 item 12).** Large systems must demonstrate wet-season non-flooding, a hydraulic gradient, and mounding control. VDH may require saturation monitoring.
- **Exclusions (12VAC5-613-30 J-K).** Dispersal to DEQ-permitted wetlands is excluded, and "Spray irrigation systems are subject to permitting by the Virginia Department of Environmental Quality and are specifically excluded from this chapter."
- **Chesapeake Bay nutrient requirements (12VAC5-613-90 D)** took effect December 7, 2013. Frederick County drains to the Potomac, part of the Bay watershed. [inferred]
- **Operation and maintenance.** Part III requires a licensed operator, visit schedules, and sludge removal for all AOSSs. [inferred] For a cooler site this is an ongoing operating cost.

## Supporting note: a vendor design manual hosted on VDH's site

A manufacturer design manual (Ecological Tanks, Inc., rev. 10/8/2005, hosted at vdh.virginia.gov/content/uploads/sites/20/2019/06/) defines "residential strength wastewater" as a "30 day average strength of 100-300 mg/l CBOD and 100-300 mg/l TSS". It says capacity must be derated as BOD rises: at 600 mg/l, a 1,000 gpd plant "will only be able to treat 500 gallons". It also says "All food services require some type of grease removal." This is vendor material, not VDH policy.

## Implications [inferred]

- **(a) On-farm custom slaughter.** Neither VDH chapter addresses outdoor kill-floor runoff from a mobile unit. Discharging blood or wash water into the farm's house septic system would put a high-strength, non-domestic load on a system permitted for domestic flow. Nothing in 12VAC5-610 authorizes that.
- **(b) Cooler with a docked mobile unit washing down.**
  - Process wash water likely means either an engineered AOSS designed for the measured strength, or a pump-and-haul holding tank.
  - Restroom and handwash flows need their own approved system.
  - Spraying wash water on land would be a DEQ matter, not VDH.
