# Central Coast (CA) Mobile Harvest Unit (NMPAN case study)

## Relevance to a "mobile kill + cut, fixed hanging cooler" model
This unit is **slaughter-only**. Hanging, cutting and packaging are done at a fixed butcher shop. It is a useful counter-case because the unit **sat unused for about 2 years mainly because it had no good hanging and cut-and-wrap partner**. In NMPAN's words, the fabricator is "an essential partner for a mobile slaughter unit." [inferred] Any model that splits the work across sites has to solve where carcasses hang, and for how long, before day one.

## Basic information (Fall 2012 update)
- Back in operation since Sept 2011 under USDA inspection, at least 2 days a week. Owned by the co-op, **leased to and operated by J & R Meats**, a small Paso Robles butcher shop "which also does the hanging, cutting, and packaging."
- Volume in Fall 2012: "about 5 to 7 beef/week at 2 different ranch sites." Site requirements (concrete pad, solid wall as a visual and sound barrier, all-metal stun chute) limited the unit to a few ranches. Other ranchers haul their animals to those sites.
- Capacity per day: 5 to 6 beef (or 10 lambs, goats or hogs).
- Hours per day: up to 12, including travel and off-loading. Days per week: 2 to 3. Weeks per year: on demand, year-round.
- Services: slaughter only. Floor area: 208 sq ft.
- Employees: 3 (managing butcher, assistant butcher, truck driver).
- Annual revenue: "projecting $100,000". Price of services: "prices vary".
- **Operating costs: "$800+ per day without overtime, more for locations >50 mi. distant."**
- Custom work: all fee-for-service. "Can do custom-exempt slaughter any day, 3 beef minimum."
- Source verification on the label is done by the fabricator. All meat is labeled with the ranch name.

## Funding history
- The unit was built in 2002 by Bruce Dunlop, who also built the Washington unit. Congressman Sam Farr redirected $138,750 in unspent federal Economic Development funds to pay for it. The Monterey County Ag & Historical Land Conservancy owned it, and it was leased to ranchers.
- A Sept 2007 USDA Rural Business Enterprise Grant paid for market research, forming the co-op (incorporated Aug 2008, 7 ranchers by summer 2009, members within 100 miles), and regulatory compliance research. HACCP, SSOPs and the site plan were written Sept 2007 to Sept 2008. The co-op then ran on a bank loan.

## Operating cycle (2009 plan)
- The fabricator's shop was "closed by the time the slaughter day ends." Carcasses were therefore "stored overnight in coolers at the ranches" and off-loaded at the fabricator the next day. This gave a two-day cycle (slaughter, then offload and park), run twice a week.
- "Carcasses are hung for 14-21 days, then cut and packaged. The producer pays the fabricator directly."
- The co-op stored packaged meat in freezers at its Oceana facility for just-in-time sales. It never owned the meat, and kept 15% of the sale price for marketing. Minimum delivery order was $150.

## Regulatory note
This was the first inspected MSU in California, which has no state inspection program. Finding the right forms took about 6 months. Front-line inspectors "didn't have the form number right." The breakthrough was FSIS's small/very small plant guide to applying for a federal grant of inspection.

## Derived numbers [inferred]
- At 5-7 beef a week, $800 a day, and 2 days a week, kill-day operating cost is about $230-320 per head before overhead and capital.
- At $100k a year and about 300 head a year (6 a week), revenue is about $330 per head.
