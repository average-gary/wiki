# WSDA Custom Farm Slaughterer (mobile) rules (2026)

Sources: the WSDA Custom Meat page (fetched 2026-10-02) and application form AGR-3324 "Custom Farm Slaughterer - Mobile (CFS) - New License Application" (rev. 6/26), https://cms.agr.wa.gov/WSDAKentico/Documents/Forms/3324-CustomFarmSlaughtererLicenseApplication.pdf. The statute is Chapter 16.49 RCW (Custom Slaughtering). The rules are Chapter 16-19 WAC and 21 CFR 117.10-117.80 (CGMP). This is a state custom-exempt framework layered on the federal 9 CFR 303.1 exemption. Virginia instead uses a VDACS custom permit, so the Washington rules are a comparison model for rig design, not Virginia law.

## Three licence categories (WSDA wording)
- **Custom Farm Slaughterers** "are mobile slaughter units licensed by WSDA to slaughter meat food animals for the owner. The slaughtering is done on the owner's farm or at an approved site."
- **Custom Slaughter Establishment**: a fixed slaughter facility.
- **Custom Meat Facilities**: cut, process and wrap uninspected meat "for the sole consumption of the owner".
A mobile kill business that also cuts needs both the CFS and CMF endorsements (via an amendment form). "There can only be one Custom Farm Slaughterer (mobile/truck) endorsement per license. If you wish to license additional vehicles you will need to complete a new application for each new vehicle."

## Minimum rig specification (WAC 16-19-100, as summarized by WSDA)
"Mobile custom farm slaughtering units must have:
- A van body completely covering the unit, but which may exclude the driver's cab and the hoist. The van body must be made of material that is nonporous and impervious to moisture.
- A hoist of 2,000 pound capacity, capable of lifting a beef carcass to a height that enables the carcass to clear the ground for bleeding and evisceration.
- A sterilizing tank ... large enough to allow complete sanitizing of tools used in the slaughtering operation.
- A water tank built into the vehicle ... with a minimum capacity of forty gallons."
The application also asks whether the tank(s) "can maintain adequate hot water temperatures (WAC 16-19-100(3))". It asks how hands are washed, how aprons, boots and gloves are cleaned, and for the brands of cleaners and sanitizers used.

## Operations required by the application (Attachment B, C)
- Applicants submit a process flow covering: verifying proof of ownership; slaughter; dressing (washing) and identifying carcasses; carcass and offal storage; offal disposal; and "Carcass delivery to a WSDA licensed custom meat facility for processing".
- "Will each carcass be hung during transport, so it does not touch the floor?" Edible offal (hearts, livers, kidneys) must travel in identified containers, kept separate from inedible material (WAC 16-19-100(9),(14)).
- Allowed species: cattle, swine, sheep, goats, emu, ostrich and other ratites (RCW 16.49.015).
- Proof of ownership: branded cattle need a WSDA livestock inspection certificate. Unbranded cattle need a "Certificate of Permit (Haul Slip)" (AGR-7020). If the operator sells the animal to the customer before slaughter, sales records must show the customer owned it first (RCW 16.49.015(3)).
- Records: dates, species, quantity, carcass weights and owner.

## Carcass identification and transport
- RCW 16.57.277: a custom farm slaughterer "shall complete and attach a custom slaughter beef tag to each of the four quarters of all slaughtered cattle". Only the director provides tags. The fee per set of four is set in WAC 16-607-100; the dollar figure was not on the page.
- RCW 16.57.275: an uninspected cattle carcass or primal part in transit must carry a certificate of permit signed by the owner. The receiving custom facility keeps it for one year.

## Costs and timing (2026)
- Licence fee: **$25.00** (AGR-3324 rev. 6/26). There are no refunds after 10 business days, and the licence expires every June 30. A returned check costs $25.
- From receipt of the application to inspection "can take 4 - 6 weeks". Applications not licensed within 6 months are dismissed.
- The licence is non-transferable, and a new owner or new location needs a new inspection.
- Other permits are the applicant's job: business licence, zoning, water and waste water.

## Related WSDA guidance (Tips for Aspiring Custom Butchers page)
"The average Custom-Exempt processor creates 6,000 lbs or more of waste each week". Rendering and landfill access are limited, and composting may need Ecology or county permits. This waste figure is for fixed custom shops. [inferred] It still matters to a mobile operator's offal plan.
