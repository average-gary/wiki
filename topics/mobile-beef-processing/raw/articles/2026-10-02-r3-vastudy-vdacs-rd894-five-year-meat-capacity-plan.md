# VDACS Five-Year Strategic Plan for Meat Processing Throughput Capacity (RD894)

**Mandate:** Chapters 310 and 311 of the 2022 Acts of Assembly (SB 726 / HB 830). Patrons were Sens. Pillion and Hackworth and Dels. Wilt, Webert and Rasoul. Both bills passed unanimously. They direct VDACS to deliver a five-year plan by Jan 1, 2023, and authorize "a program of financial incentives and technical assistance ... to the extent that public or private funds become available." The report is listed on RGA as RD894, publication year 2022.

**Note on the Virginia Tech/VSU assessment:** This report does not cite a Virginia Tech or VSU statewide processor survey. Its only two "essential resources" for the situation analysis are (1) Matson Consulting / VA FAIRS, *A Study of Small-Volume Red Meat Processing in Virginia* (Sept 2020), and (2) Fuller Consulting / PEC, *Review of Piedmont Regional Processing Plants* (Spring 2021). The section on land-grant universities describes only Master Cattlemen, VQA, the VT Meat Center and the VSU small-ruminant MPU. No VT/VSU survey results are mentioned. [inferred: as of Jan 2023 no VT/VSU statewide survey had been published that VDACS relied on.]

## Data collection
- Virginia Farm Bureau hosted four listening sessions in June 2022:
  - Buckingham (6/15): limited processing options within driving distance, and federal grants too hard to apply for. Participants asked for a VDACS "meat specialist."
  - Culpeper/Rapidan (6/20): local wastewater rules, with localities requiring plants to build their own systems, and NIMBY opposition. Participants asked for model ordinances and alternative wastewater models.
  - **Harrisonburg (6/21)**: T&E Meats, Gore's and Redbanks Custom Cuts attended. Issues raised: by-product disposal ("the entire slaughter industry in Virginia is reliant on a single rendering operation"), aging infrastructure, labor, and inspection experiences.
  - Wytheville (6/27): input costs and direct-marketing upside.
- VDACS also interviewed stakeholders directly, including T&E, Gore's, Washington County Meat Packing, Seven Hills, Blue Ridge Meats of Front Royal, the Tobacco Commission, First Bank & Trust (Harrisonburg) and NCDA&CS.

## Facility inventory (as of late 2022)
- 46 slaughter or processing facilities operate under state inspection. **10 are Talmadge-Aiken all-species slaughter+processing plants.** All are Very Small except Seven Hills (Small):
  - Crabill's (Toms Brook)
  - Donald's (Lexington)
  - Double L (Jonesville)
  - EcoFriendly Foods (Moneta)
  - Gore's (Edinburg)
  - Salatin & Cloud/T&E (Harrisonburg)
  - Schrock's (Gladys)
  - Seven Hills (Lynchburg)
  - Smith Valley (Rich Creek)
  - Washington County Meat Packing (Bristol)
- 4 FSIS-direct plants offer custom services:
  - Blue Ridge Meats of Front Royal (Middletown)
  - **Fauquier's Finest Custom Meat Processing (Fauquier, Small)**
  - **Gentle Harvest Custom Processing (Winchester)**
  - Safa Halal (Fredericksburg)
- Inspection: 40 hours/week at no cost, with overtime for a fee. Processors report sometimes not getting all the hours they want.

## Livestock base (NASS, Jan 2022)
- 599,000 beef cattle, 265,000 hogs, 72,000 sheep, 42,000 meat goats.
- Beef inventory is down about 18% since 2006.
- Only 17,000 cattle are on feed, under 3% of beef cows. Most steers and heifers ship to Western feedlots.

## Economics quoted from Matson (2020)
- A small to medium multispecies plant (1,000-2,500 head) would net only **$17,000-$42,000/yr**.
- Construction costs $300-$400 per sq ft.
- The pro forma does not return worthwhile income below **5,000+ head/yr**.
- A 5,000-head plant costs over **$2.5 million** to build, with loan payments "well over $100,000" a year. These are pre-inflation figures.

## Constraints identified
- Labor is the top constraint, per PEC. Plants cannot staff the kill floor and processing at the same time.
- Aging equipment, and too little cooler space and finished-goods storage.
- A single renderer.
- Hides: the China export market dried up, and processors now pay for disposal, often by landfill.
- Of several prospective entrepreneurs who contacted the VDACS Office of Agriculture and Forestry Development (OAFD), "only one" followed through. That project was still seeking financing.
- Most processors would rather optimize than expand.

## AFID usage (since 2012)
- Facility grants: 4 meat processors (and 5 poultry).
- Infrastructure grants (created 2021): 3 meat processors in the first two rounds.
- **Planning grants: "four localities have received grants to explore the feasibility of new meat processing operations"** since 2013. [Fauquier's Oct 2022 grant would be one of these; the report does not name the localities.]

## Other states (ARPA-funded)
- Maryland: equipment grants up to $100k at 75% of cost.
- Pennsylvania: grants up to $100k.
- West Virginia: Mountain State Ground Beef.
- North Carolina IMPEC: $26M across 93 grants ($13k-$2M), with a phase 4 of up to $15M.
- "Virginia chose not to use ARPA funds" for meat processing. The SWOT lists this as a threat.

## Goals (five)
1. Optimize existing inspected plants: AFID Infrastructure grants, OMPS inspection capacity, by-product disposal alternatives, a revolving working-capital loan fund backed by MPILP/FSCGLP, and NMPAN/AAMP participation.
2. Strategic expansions: help custom-exempt plants convert to inspection, AFID for value-added processing, and SBDC/VSBFA/Tobacco Commission/VCEDA financing.
3. New facilities: AFID Facility grants for "processing deserts," **stand-alone cut-and-wrap facilities** so slaughter plants can raise head throughput, model ordinances and site-selection toolkits, and producer capital pooling.
4. Workforce: AFID Planning grants for training, matching funds for certification, a CTE curriculum, DOLI apprenticeships, and VT training capacity.
5. Finished cattle market: food-waste feed studies, feed-out workshops, a Virginia Beef ground-beef brand, and year-round timing of fed cattle.

Implementation runs in three phases over five years. Phase 1 is the specialist position plus AFID Planning and Infrastructure grants.
