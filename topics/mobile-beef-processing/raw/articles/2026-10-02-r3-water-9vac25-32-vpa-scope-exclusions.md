# 9VAC25-32 VPA Permit Regulation: scope as it applies to slaughter blood and wash water

Text taken from the Virginia LIS Administrative Code on 2026-10-02. The most recent amendment to section 30 is Virginia Register Vol. 39, Iss. 5, effective November 23, 2022. Statutory authority: Va. Code § 62.1-44.15.

## Key definitions (9VAC25-32-10)

- **"Industrial wastes"**: "liquid or other wastes resulting from any process of industry, manufacture, trade, or business, or from the development of any natural resources." [inferred] Slaughter blood and wash water from a custom-slaughter business fit this definition. The regulation does not exempt small or agricultural meat processing by name.
- **"Industrial residuals"**: "solid or semisolid industrial waste including solids, residues, and precipitates separated or created by the unit processes of a device or system used to treat industrial wastes." [inferred] Solids screened or settled out of wash water could fall here.
- **"Land application"** (for sewage, biosolids, and industrial residuals): spreading, spraying, injecting, or incorporating treated effluent, biosolids, or industrial residuals "with a uniform application rate for the purpose of fertilizing crops or vegetation or conditioning the soil."
- **"Pollutant management activity"**: "a treatment works with a potential or actual discharge to state waters, but which does not have a point source discharge to surface waters."
- **"Treatment works"** includes septic tanks and "any works, including land," used for treatment or ultimate disposal.
- **"State waters"**: "all water on the surface or under the ground wholly or partially within or bordering the state." This includes groundwater, so a soak-away pit can trigger the regulation.
- **"Point source"**: "any discernible, defined and discrete conveyance, including ... pipe, ditch, channel ... container, rolling stock". It excludes return flows from irrigated agricultural land.

## Requirements and prohibitions (9VAC25-32-30)

- A. Activities under a VPA permit must have "no point source discharge of pollutants to surface waters except in the case of a storm event greater than the 25-year, 24-hour storm."
- B. "Except in compliance with a VPA permit, or another permit issued by the department or a general permit issued as a regulation adopted by the board, it shall be unlawful for any person to: 1. Discharge into, or adjacent to, state waters sewage, industrial wastes, other wastes, or any noxious or deleterious substances; or 2. Otherwise alter the physical, chemical or biological properties of such state waters..."
- C. An unpermitted discharge must be reported to DEQ "immediately upon discovery ... and, in any event, no later than 24 hours after the discovery." A written report is due within five days. It must give the nature, cause, date, duration, volume, and planned steps to prevent a recurrence.
- D. VPA permits "may be utilized to authorize pollutant management activities including ... storage or land application of sewage, sludge, biosolids, industrial waste or other waste; or the complete reuse or recycle of wastewater. Point source discharges of pollutants to surface waters may be authorized by a VPDES permit (See 9VAC25-31...)."

## Exclusions: no VPA permit needed (9VAC25-32-40)

1. Introduction of sewage or industrial waste "into publicly owned treatment works by indirect dischargers." This is the sewer route, for example to FCSA.
2. "Any introduction of pollutants from nonpoint source agricultural or silvicultural activities, including runoff from orchards, cultivated crops, pastures..." This does not apply to concentrated confined animal feeding operations.
3. Return flows from irrigated agricultural land.
4. Land disposal activity, including onsite waste treatment, "when this activity is otherwise authorized by the department."
5. "Land disposal activity, including onsite waste treatment, when this activity is authorized by a Virginia Department of Health permit."
6. EPA-authorized UIC (Safe Drinking Water Act) discharges approved in writing by DEQ.

[inferred] For the two sites:
- **(a) On-farm slaughter.** The regulation has no carve-out for blood or wash water from a commercial mobile unit. Exclusion 2 covers runoff from farming. Whether it covers waste generated by a third-party business on the farm is not settled by the text, so ask the DEQ Valley Regional Office.
- **(b) Hanging and aging cooler where the mobile unit docks and washes down.** The three permit-free routes are: (i) sewer, under exclusion 1; (ii) a VDH-permitted onsite system, under exclusion 5; or (iii) hauling to a permitted facility. Spreading the wash water on land otherwise needs a VPA permit.

## General VPA permits (9VAC25-32-260)

The board may issue general VPA permits for a category of pollutant management activities with "the same or similar types of operations" and wastes. DEQ may require an individual permit where an activity "is a significant contributor of pollution". The regulation text itself lists no small-food-processor or slaughter general permit. The general permits named in Part V are for animal feeding operations (section 250) and animal-waste end-users (section 255). [inferred] No general permit for small meat-processing wastewater was found in 9VAC25-32. Its land application would need an individual VPA permit.

## Part IX scope note

Part IX (sections 303-850) is the biosolids program: sewage sludge, applicator certification, and liability insurance. It does not set standards for industrial wastewater from food processing, beyond the definitions above.
