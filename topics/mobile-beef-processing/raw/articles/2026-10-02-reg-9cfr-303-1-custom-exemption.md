# 9 CFR 303.1 Exemptions

Text as it stood in eCFR on 2026-09-01 (fetched through the eCFR versioner API). This is the federal basis for the "custom-exempt" model.

## (a)(1) Owner-raised, owner-slaughtered

Inspection does not apply to "the slaughtering by any individual of livestock of his own raising, and the preparation by him and transportation in commerce of the carcasses ... exclusively for use by him and members of his household and his nonpaying guests and employees."

## (a)(2) Custom slaughter, which is the custom-exempt mobile model

Inspection does not apply to "the custom slaughter by any person of cattle, sheep, swine, or goats delivered by the owner thereof for such slaughter ... exclusively for use, in the household of such owner, by him and members of his household and his nonpaying guests and employees." Custom *preparation* (cutting and wrapping) of owner-raised animals or game is exempt on the same terms.

The custom operator must meet these conditions:

- **(i) Sanitation.** Custom establishments "must be maintained and operated in accordance with the provisions of §§ 416.1 through 416.6", except 416.2(g)(2)-(6) on water reuse and any provisions that depend on a Program employee. Custom work done inside an official (inspected) establishment falls under all of part 416.
- **(ii) Separation.** Any product handled for sale is kept "separate and apart from the custom prepared products at all times."
- **(iii) Marking.** Custom product must be "plainly marked 'Not for Sale' as provided in § 316.16 ... immediately after being prepared" and stay identified until it is delivered to the owner.
- **(iv) Shared facilities.** In an official establishment, equipment used for custom work must be cleaned and sanitized before it is used for products for sale.

## (b) Further conditions

- (b)(1) Products must not be adulterated and must follow 318.5, 318.6, 318.300-318.311 and 424.21. The provisions of those sections that depend on an inspector do not apply.
- (b)(2) Products must comply with 316.16 (marking) and 317.16 (labeling).
- (b)(3) **Records.** Custom operators must keep records "showing the numbers and kinds of livestock slaughtered on a custom basis, the quantities and types of products prepared on a custom basis, and the names and addresses of the owners of the livestock and products," on top of the part 320 records.
- (b)(4) Edible articles from custom slaughter must be denatured or identified under 325.13 before they leave the establishment, unless they are delivered to the owner.

## (d) Retail store and restaurant exemption

This exemption does not cover slaughter. It covers cutting carcasses into retail cuts, grinding, curing, cooking, smoking, rendering, breaking bulk, and wrapping. The listed operations explicitly exclude "slaughtering or the retort processing of canned products."

- A "normal retail quantity" is no more than one-half carcass per purchase. For **cattle that is 300 lb**. The other species: calves 37.5, sheep 27.5, swine 100, goats 25.
- A retail store must make at least 75% of product sales by dollar value to household consumers. Sales to non-household consumers are capped by a CPI-adjusted dollar limit that is published in the Federal Register.
- A retail store may use only federally or State inspected and passed product for sale. Custom product may be handled under (a)(2), but not for sale. Doing custom work does not disqualify a store from the retail exemption.

## Implications for a mobile custom operator [inferred]

- The exemption depends on **who owns the animal at slaughter**. The person who receives the meat must own the live animal. That is why "freezer beef" is sold by the share (live-animal shares) before slaughter, and why custom meat cannot be resold.
- 303.1 says nothing about where slaughter takes place. On-farm slaughter by a mobile custom unit is therefore not barred federally. The 416 sanitation standard still applies to the "establishment", and state law decides how a mobile unit is licensed.
