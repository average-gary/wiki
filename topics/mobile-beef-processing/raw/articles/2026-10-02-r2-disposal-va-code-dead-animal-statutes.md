# Virginia Code dead-animal disposal statutes

Fetched from law.lis.virginia.gov on 2026-10-02 (Code as of that date).

## § 18.2-510. Burial or cremation of animals or fowls which have died

Full text:

> "When the owner of any animal or grown fowl which has died knows of such death, such owner shall forthwith have its body cremated or buried or request such service from an officer or other person designated for the purpose. If the owner fails to do so, any judge of a general district court, after notice to the owner if he can be ascertained, shall cause any such dead animal or fowl to be cremated or buried by an officer or other person designated for the purpose. Such officer or other person shall be entitled to recover of the owner of every such animal or fowl that is cremated or buried the actual cost of the cremation or burial and a reasonable fee ... Any person violating the provisions of this section shall be guilty of a Class 4 misdemeanor.
>
> Nothing in this section shall be deemed to require the burial or cremation of the whole or portions of any animal or fowl which is to be used for food or in any commercial manner.
>
> This section shall not apply to any county until the governing body thereof shall adopt the same."

History: Code 1950, § 32-70; 1979, c. 716; 1981, c. 578; 2008, c. 345.

Points for a custom-exempt mobile slaughter business:
- The duty falls on the **owner** of the animal that "has died". For custom-exempt slaughter the animal's owner is the customer (farmer), not the slaughter operator [inferred: whether slaughter offal from a deliberately slaughtered animal counts as an animal that "has died" is not addressed in the text].
- Burial or cremation is required only for the parts not "used for food or in any commercial manner." Offal sent to a renderer is a commercial use [inferred], so it is outside the burial duty.
- **Local-option:** the section applies in a county only after the county board adopts it. Whether Frederick, Clarke, Shenandoah, Warren, or Page counties have adopted it was not checked (open question; look in the county code, usually the animal-control chapter).
- No burial depth, setback, or distance-to-water rule appears in the statute. Those come from DEQ/VDH guidance, not this section.

## § 3.2-6554. Disposal of dead companion animals

> "The owner of any companion animal shall forthwith cremate, bury, or sanitarily dispose of the animal upon its death. If, after notice, any owner fails to do so, the animal control officer or other officer shall bury or cremate the companion animal, and he may recover on behalf of the local jurisdiction from the owner his cost for this service."

This sits in Ch. 65 (Comprehensive Animal Care), Art. 6 (Authority of Local Governing Bodies). It applies to **companion animals**, not livestock. It does not govern cattle slaughter waste.

## Title 3.2 Chapter 60 (Livestock and Poultry): what it contains

From the chapter table of contents:
- Art. 1, Contagious and Infectious Diseases (§§ 3.2-6000 to -6023). Includes § 3.2-6010, "Euthanasia or slaughter of livestock or poultry by owner" (disease-control context) and § 3.2-6013, "Duty of operators of stockyards and poultry slaughter facilities." Disposal of diseased or condemned animals follows State Veterinarian orders under this article.
- Art. 2, **Disposal of Dead Poultry** (§§ 3.2-6024 to -6030). Covers proper disposal by persons raising poultry for profit, transportation of dead poultry, exemptions, and regulations. Poultry only; there is no matching article for cattle.
- Art. 3, Prohibitions on Feeding Garbage to Swine (§§ 3.2-6031 to -6034). This is relevant if anyone proposes feeding raw offal to hogs [inferred: whether raw slaughter offal counts as "garbage" under § 3.2-6031 was not checked].
- Arts. 4-5: shooting enclosures; penalties.

Chapter 59 (General Provisions, §§ 3.2-5900 to -5905) covers the State Veterinarian, the diagnostic laboratory, and indemnity. It has no carcass-disposal or rendering-license provision. Title 3.2's chapter list (Ch. 53 eggs, 54 slaughterhouses/meat, 55 vinegar, 56-58 weights and measures, 59 general, 60 livestock and poultry, 61 branding) has **no rendering or dead-animal-hauler chapter**. The "Chapter 57 renderers" premise in the research brief is wrong: Ch. 57 is weights-and-measures service agencies.

## Takeaways

1. Virginia's statutes set no cattle-specific rule for how slaughter offal must be disposed of. The only general livestock rule is the local-option § 18.2-510, which exempts parts put to commercial use.
2. Environmental controls on burial and composting therefore come from DEQ (solid waste) and VDH, and federal BSE feed rules (21 CFR 589.2001) govern any material sent to rendering.
