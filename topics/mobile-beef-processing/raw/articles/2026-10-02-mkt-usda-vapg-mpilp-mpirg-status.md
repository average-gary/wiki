# VAPG FY2026, plus MPILP and MPIRG status

All rd.usda.gov pages were read through Wayback snapshots because rd.usda.gov returns 403 to scripted clients. The AMS MPIRG page was fetched live.

## Value-Added Producer Grant (VAPG), FY2026
- "Applications are accepted for processing from Agricultural Producers from February 17, 2026, through 1:00 p.m. Eastern Time (ET), April 22, 2026." Status: **Closed**.
- Total available funding: "Approximately $25 million."
- Maximum grants: **Planning up to $50,000** (feasibility studies, business and marketing plans). **Working capital up to $200,000** (processing, packaging, advertising, inventory, salaries).
- Match: "1:1 match (100% of the grant amount), which may include cash or eligible in-kind contributions."
- Regulation: 7 CFR Part 4284, Subpart J. VAPG is part of LAMP under the 2018 Farm Bill.
- Eligible applicants: agricultural producers, producer groups, farmer or rancher cooperatives, and majority-controlled producer-based business ventures. Applicants must "Own and produce more than 50% of the raw commodity" and "retain greater revenue from the value-added product than from the raw commodity alone."
- Priority categories: beginning, veteran, or socially disadvantaged farmers and ranchers; small or medium family farms; cooperatives; and Mid-Tier Value Chain projects. 10% of funds are reserved for beginning and veteran producers, and 10% for mid-tier value chains. Food safety projects are also mentioned.
- FY2026 Administrator priority points: "Eligible applicants who have never previously received a VAPG grant", "Applicants requesting a grant amount of less than $125,000", and "Applicants who broaden the geographic reach of the awards."
- Third-party preparer rule: applicants must disclose the name and amount paid. If the fee is over 15% of the requested grant, the application is not eligible for Administrator priority points. Awards can be rescinded if the contract does not support what was disclosed.
- [inferred] For a mobile beef business, VAPG fits best when a cattle-producer group or cooperative owns the unit and sells its own branded beef. A processor-only company that slaughters other people's cattle would not meet the more-than-50%-ownership-of-the-raw-commodity test.

## Meat and Poultry Intermediary Lending Program (MPILP)
Source: https://www.rd.usda.gov/programs-services/business-programs/meat-and-poultry-intermediary-lending-program (snapshot 2025-02-05).
- Gives grants to intermediary lenders "who finance – or plan to finance – the start-up, expansion, or operation of slaughter, or other processing of meat and poultry."
- Ultimate recipients must "Be a business engaged, or proposing to engage, in commercial meat or poultry processing" and "Comply with USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service requirements, or be custom exempt". This means MPILP loans can reach **startups and custom-exempt operators**, unlike MPPEP.
- Eligible uses include constructing a new facility, equipment, working capital, feasibility studies, waste management, and "Offsetting start-up costs, working capital, fees and other expenses related to federal inspection."
- Intermediary awards range from $500,000 minimum to $15 million aggregate maximum.
- **2026 status:** the same URL shows "Page Not Found" in the 2026 Wayback snapshot. The program page appears to have been removed. Existing intermediary loan funds may still be lending [unverified]. Which Virginia-area intermediaries (if any) hold MPILP funds was not found.

## Meat and Poultry Inspection Readiness Grant (MPIRG), AMS
Source: https://www.ams.usda.gov/services/grants/mpirg (fetched live 2026-10-02).
- Helps "currently operational meat and poultry slaughter and processing facilities" obtain a federal grant of inspection or operate under a CIS program.
- The page lists only the **2021 and 2022** RFAs and webinars. No FY2025 or FY2026 round is posted, so the program is effectively dormant as of October 2026 [inferred from the page contents].
- [inferred] Virginia has no state meat inspection program that is "equal to" federal inspection and runs a CIS agreement for red meat, so the federal grant-of-inspection route would be the only path [needs verification against VDACS].
