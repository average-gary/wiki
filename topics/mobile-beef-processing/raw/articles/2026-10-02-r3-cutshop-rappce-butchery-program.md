# RappCE Butchery Program, and PEC's 2021 announcement

## RappCE program page (fetched 2026-10-02)
- Overview: "designed to give you the tools and confidence to provide your customer with a great experience at the meat case. We include courses on providing exemplary customer service and understanding basic meat knowledge, including characteristics of over 240 meat cuts."
- Skills listed:
  - "a solid foundation similar to a seasoned supermarket butcher"
  - customer service and meat basics
  - primals and subprimals
  - recognizing cuts in the meat case
  - meat as an ingredient (slices, cubed, ground)
  - identifying and merchandising cuts
  - cut attributes
  - recommending substitutes and cooking methods
- **Tuition: $900, including all classroom materials.** Need-based scholarships are available to eligible applicants.
- Background on the page: AFT surveys found "expanding small processing operations is challenging because trained labor is scarce despite the relatively high pay and regular hours." RappCE built the program with PEC. Producers featured in the video: Belle Ridge Farm and Bean Hollow Grassfed. Foodshed Capital funded the video through a USDA AMS grant/cooperative agreement.
- Institution: RappCE is a 501(c)(3) at PO Box 35, Sperryville, VA 22740, phone 540-321-0926. It is "Certified by SCHEV to Operate a Vocational Postsecondary Institution". It does not grant college credit or degrees.
- The course calendar is linked from the page, but no dates appeared in the fetched HTML.

## PEC press release, Aug 3, 2021 ("New skilled meat cutter training program...")
Source: https://www.pecva.org/region/albemarle-charlottesville-region/new-skilled-meat-cutter-training-program-will-help-regions-meat-processors-serve-virginia-cattle-farmers/
- The program came out of a four-month PEC study done with American Farmland Trust, funded by Prince Charitable Trusts, and run with Fuller Consulting. That study was ingested in round 2.
- John McCarthy (PEC): during the pandemic, Piedmont-area processors ran "at 125 to 150 percent capacity," and "local cattle farmers faced 12- to 18-month waits."
- The team visited all seven operating USDA-certified facilities in the region: **Winchester, Stephens City, Middletown, Harrisonburg, Lexington, Bealeton and Lynchburg**.
- "One challenge that each and every processor reported was access to trained labor. Trained meat cutters are in high demand and short supply, and bringing untrained people in to fill these roles forces slowdowns in operations and safety concerns."
- The program uses "a national training model". The first class was planned for late fall 2021 with "an anticipated enrollment of six to ten students" (RappCE CEO Doug Schiffman).
- Partners named: PEC, AFT, RappCE, Virginia Tech. AFT's Jacob Gilley is quoted.
- Another outcome of the study was PEC's processor asset map at https://processing.pecva.org/.

## Implications for a mobile kill-and-cut crew [inferred]
- RappCE's current curriculum trains **retail counter butchers**, not carcass breakers, kill-floor workers or band-saw fabricators. A mobile unit that cuts onboard would still need on-the-job training, or a hire who already has experience breaking carcasses.
- At $900 per trainee, sponsoring hires through the program costs little. It is a minor line item compared with wages of about $20/hr (see the BLS OEWS file).
- No Virginia community college butchery or meat-cutting credential was found in this pass. Laurel Ridge CC site search returned nothing relevant. This is an open question.
