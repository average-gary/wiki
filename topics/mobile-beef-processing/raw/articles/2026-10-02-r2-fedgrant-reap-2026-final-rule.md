# REAP final rule with comment, 91 FR (Oct 1, 2026), FR Doc 2026-20178

PDF: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-01/pdf/2026-20178.pdf. The rule amends 7 CFR 4280 Subpart B.

## What changed

- **DATES.** "This final rule is effective October 16, 2026." An associated information-collection notice takes comments until Nov 30, 2026.
- **Post-completion model.** "Projects must already be fully built and operational before the applicant ever applies. Applicants then apply, submitting actual energy production or savings data for the prior 12 months, alongside 12 months of pre-installation data." All RES/EEI awards are "based on actual documented output, energy savings, costs, and system performance."
- **One national competition.** The rule proposes to eliminate state-level competitions "in favor of a single national scoring and selection process." Funding is subject to "criteria provided in an annual notice." On submission periods: "The Agency will publish the dates for the application period on its website." Requests of $20,000 or less have their own submission track.
- **One application per funding cycle.**

## Award terms (new § 4280.122)

- **Minimum request:** "$1,500."
- **Maximum request:** "$500,000 for a RES grant ... $250,000 for an EEI grant."
- **Cost sharing:** "required for at least 75 percent of the Project Cost. For example, if the Project Cost is $1,000,000, Cost Sharing must be at least $750,000." Under the rule, the grant therefore covers at most 25% of cost. The pre-rule rd.usda.gov page (2026 Wayback) still said "Grants for up to 50 percent of total eligible project costs" and "Combined grant and loan guarantee funding up to 75%".
- **Loan guarantees** (pre-rule page): up to 75% of costs, 80% guarantee for FY2025 loans, 1% guarantee fee plus 0.25% annual retention, term up to 40 years.

## Eligibility points that hit a start-up mobile slaughter unit

- **Applicant type** (pre-rule page, unchanged in substance): either
  - an agricultural producer with "at least 50 percent of their gross income coming from agricultural operations", or
  - a rural small business (for-profit, or a cooperative including 501(c)(12)) in an area of 50,000 or fewer people that meets SBA size standards.
- **Existing Business:** "The Applicant must be an Existing Business at least 12 months prior to the start of the Project Period." The revised definition "no longer treats a new business and Existing Business applying as co-Applicants as an existing business."
- **Ownership/control:** the applicant must own the RES/EEI and "own or control the site for the Project. This ownership must be in place at least 12 months prior to the Project Period."
- **Mobile systems** (§ 4280.222(b)(14) and preamble xix): "The Project involves an RES not directly mounted on a vehicle that is used to carry out the Applicant's full-time business operations, or the Project involves EEI to any vehicle" is ineligible.
  - Eligible example: "installing a solar panel on a food truck used by the applicant to conduct business operations—such as powering the truck's refrigeration system."
  - Ineligible example: "solar panels and battery storage systems mounted on a trailer."
- **Multilocation:** "applications to install a RES or an EEI at more than one location are no longer eligible."
- **Storage:** batteries are limited to 120% of the prior-12-month average energy use. Stand-alone storage is ineligible.
- The pre-rule list of efficiency uses included "Cooling or refrigeration units." Under the new rule, though, EEI "to any vehicle" is excluded.

## Implications [inferred]

1. **A brand-new entity cannot use REAP in year 1.** It must be an existing business for 12 months, own or control the site for 12 months, build the system with its own money, and then document 12 months of energy data before and after installation. Realistically, REAP becomes a partial reimbursement in year 2-3 or later.
2. **Refrigeration on the mobile unit is likely ineligible as EEI**, because EEI to any vehicle is excluded.
   - Solar mounted directly on a self-propelled truck used full-time might fit the food-truck example.
   - Solar on a towed slaughter trailer matches the cited ineligible example ("mounted on a trailer").
   - A fixed base, such as a cooler or carcass-hanging building at the home farm, is the cleaner REAP target. EEI there would be refrigeration; RES would be roof or ground solar.
3. Because the grant covers at most 25%, REAP is a minor funding source in a start-up capital stack.
