# Code of Virginia Title 3.2, Chapter 54 (Meat and Poultry Products Inspection)

Fetched section by section from law.lis.virginia.gov. The chapter runs §§ 3.2-5400 to 3.2-5421, including 3.2-5402.1, which was added in 2026 (cc. 107, 108). Article 2 (§§ 3.2-5419 to 5421) covers only genuine Smithfield hams.

## Key sections

- **§ 3.2-5400 Definitions.** "Livestock" is "any cattle, sheep, swine, goats, horses, mules, or other equines, whether live or dead." An article is "capable of use as human food" unless it has been denatured or identified under Board regulations, or is naturally inedible. An "inspector" can be a state employee or a local government employee working under an agreement with the Commissioner.
- **§ 3.2-5403 Cooperation with USDA.** Virginia's programs must "impose and enforce requirements with respect to intrastate operations and commerce that are at least equal to those imposed and enforced under the Federal Meat Inspection Act and the Federal Poultry Products Inspection Act." This "equal to" standard is the condition FSIS uses for state MPI programs.
- **§ 3.2-5404 Duties of the Board.**
  - (1) Requires antemortem and postmortem inspection "at all establishments in the Commonwealth, except those exempted by the Commissioner pursuant to subdivision 9 of § 3.2-5405," where product is prepared "solely for distribution in intrastate commerce."
  - (2) Passed product is marked **"Virginia Inspected and Passed"**. Adulterated product is marked "Virginia Inspected and Condemned" and destroyed under supervision.
  - (3) Only federally or Virginia-inspected product may enter official establishments.
  - (5)-(6) The Board sets sanitation rules and can withdraw inspection from insanitary establishments.
  - (7) Sets recordkeeping duties for slaughterers, processors, brokers, renderers, and dead-stock haulers.
- **§ 3.2-5405 Powers of Commissioner.**
  - A 6: cooperate with USDA, accept federal funds, and "pay 50 percent of the estimated total cost of the cooperative program." This is the Talmadge-Aiken / state MPI 50:50 funding split [inferred link to the federal program name, which the statute does not use].
  - **A 9:** the Commissioner may "exempt the operations of any person from inspection or other requirements of this article if and to the extent such operations would be exempt from the corresponding requirements under the federal acts if they were conducted in or for interstate commerce." This is the only statutory hook for custom-exempt operation in Virginia.
- **§ 3.2-5407 Prohibitions.** No one may slaughter or prepare articles capable of use as human food at an intrastate establishment "except in compliance with the requirements of this chapter." No one may sell articles that must be inspected "unless they have been so inspected and passed."
- **§ 3.2-5409 B / § 3.2-5411 Inedible material.** Livestock products not intended as human food must be "denatured or otherwise identified" before they are bought, sold, or transported, unless naturally inedible. § 3.2-5409 C limits dealing in dead, dying, disabled, or diseased animals, which affects how offal and dead stock are hauled.
- **§ 3.2-5413** allows administrative detention of violative or exempted product.
- **§ 3.2-5415 Penalties.** A Class 1 misdemeanor. A Class 6 felony if the violation involves intent to defraud or knowing distribution of adulterated product. The Commissioner may issue a warning letter for minor violations.
- **§ 3.2-5417** authorizes injunctions.
- **§ 3.2-5402.1 (2026).** A civil penalty of up to $500 for each mislabeled manufactured-protein product. This does not bear on beef slaughter.

## What the statute does NOT contain (as fetched)

- No custom-slaughter license, permit, or registration fee schedule. A search of all 23 sections for "custom", "mobile", "license" and "registration" found only the federal-parity exemption in § 3.2-5405 A 9. Any licensing or registration of custom operators would sit in the Board's regulations (2VAC5) or in VDACS administrative practice, not in Chapter 54 [inferred; still to be verified].
- No mention of mobile units and no fixed-location requirement. A mobile custom unit operating on-farm is therefore neither expressly permitted nor barred by the statute. Its status depends on the 9 CFR 303.1 parity exemption and on VDACS regulations and policy [inferred].
- No Cooperative Interstate Shipment (CIS) language. CIS participation would come from a USDA agreement, not this chapter.
