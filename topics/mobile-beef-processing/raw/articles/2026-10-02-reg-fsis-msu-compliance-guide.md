# Mobile Slaughter Unit Compliance Guide (FSIS)

The live fsis.usda.gov site returned 403. The PDF was fetched from a Wayback snapshot dated 2023-08-06. The guide's comment docket is FSIS-2010-0004, which suggests it dates from about 2010 [inferred]; the FSIS landing page is /guidelines/2010-0002. The guide calls itself recommendations, not regulatory requirements. It also contains outdated URLs and a typo ("40 CFR Part 1410", which should be part 141).

## Definition and baseline
- An MSU is "a self-contained slaughter facility that can travel from site to site."
- "MSU operators are subject to the same regulatory requirements that apply to a fixed ('brick and mortar') facility."
- Stated advantages: "lower processing costs, reduced stress on animals, lower capital investment, and less resistance from municipalities and neighbors."

## Grant of inspection (IV.A)
- Contact the District Office (DO) for the main operating area and file **FSIS Form 5200-2**.
- There are seven steps: (1) application; (2) facilities meet regulatory performance standards; (3) approved labels or brands; (4) approved water source letter; (5) approved sewage system letter; (6) written Sanitation SOP; (7) written hazard analysis and HACCP plan.
- Only the application goes to the DO. Everything else stays on file at the facility for inspection program personnel (IPP).
- After a successful review, FSIS issues a **conditional grant that allows 90 days to validate the HACCP program**.
- **Operating in several districts:** the home district assigns the establishment number (for example "Est. 00"). Each additional district uses that number with a suffix ("00 A", "00 B").
- IPP must have "access at all times, day or night" (FMIA Sec. 606).
- **Scheduling:** "Every time the MSU moves to a different location, and before conducting any slaughter operations," the operator must notify the DO for that location. The operator files days and hours under 9 CFR 307.4(d)(1), and FSIS recommends **2-4 weeks advance notice**. The DO must approve any schedule change. A seasonal operation can request a voluntary suspension of up to **120 calendar days**.
- FSIS cautions: "The staffing of mobile slaughter units may present a challenge to FSIS. It may be difficult for the Agency to find personnel to provide inspection at the location."

## Sanitation (IV.B), 9 CFR part 416, all provisions
- **Water (416.2(g)(1)):** potability must be documented under the National Primary Drinking Water regs. The water can be municipal, private well, or trucked in a tank with a potability report. The documentation has to be available at every site before slaughter. For a private well at a recurring site, it must be renewed **semi-annually**.
- **Sewage and wastewater:** the operator must give FSIS "a letter of approval from the local health authority for any specific slaughter site." Options are on-site septic, a holding tank hauled to a docking station, or dispersing blood and wastewater on the producer's land "well away from any stream or drainage, provided the local health authority permits this." This letter is "required to obtain and uphold terms of the grant."
- **Facilities:** surfaces must be impervious and cleanable (416.2(b)), with ventilation (416.2(d)) and pest control using EPA/FIFRA-approved products. A concrete or gravel pad is recommended, and bleeding on a sloped pad draining to a drain field.
- **Toilets and handwashing (416.2(h)):** must be available within a "reasonable" distance, which the DO decides. Portable toilets plus a hand sink inside the unit qualify. Farm toilets may suffice.
- **Inspector office:** not required if the MSU is on a patrol assignment or the inspector can use an office at an associated official fabrication plant. FSIS recommends a desk, light, chair, sample cooler, and a lockable cabinet for brands.
- **SSOPs:** 416.11-416.12, with the records required by 416.16 kept in the MSU.

## HACCP (IV.C)
- A written hazard analysis and slaughter HACCP plan must be prepared by an HACCP-trained individual *before* the grant (9 CFR 304.3(b)). All 417.5 record requirements apply.
- Hazards named: feces, ingesta and milk contamination; E. coli O157:H7 and Salmonella; and residues.
- "Chilling and cold storage of product may occur within the MSU or in an associated fabrication facility." Further processing must be covered in the HACCP plan.
- States assign HACCP Coordinators to help establishments.

## Slaughter rules (IV.D)
- Parts 307-314 apply, including generic E. coli testing (310.25(a)) and humane slaughter (part 313). "If a firearm is used then the head cannot be saved for edible product, except for the tongue."
- FSIS humane-handling authority begins as soon as animals are staged for slaughter.
- **Each site** must provide an ante-mortem pen, a holding pen for U.S. Suspects, and a covered pen for diseased or disabled animals.
- Custom carcasses and carcasses of different species carried in the MSU need separation or wraps.
- Inedible material must be denatured and handled under **9 CFR 325.11 and 325.13**.
- **Offal disposal:** "denatured meat or poultry offal might be allowed to remain on the farm to be composted for use as a soil amendment when permitted by local regulations," which avoids "the higher cost of rendering."
- Moving retained product to an official facility requires FSIS-approved securing (307.2(h), 310.3).
- Exotic species such as bison and elk fall under voluntary inspection (part 352).

## Implications [inferred]
- An inspected MSU in practice has to be paired with a fixed inspected cut-and-wrap plant (for chilling and the inspector office), or it needs its own cooler.
- Because a health-authority wastewater letter is needed for each site, every new farm stop means paperwork with the county or VDH.
- The guide predates the 2017 consolidation of FSIS districts and makes no mention of bovine SRM rules (9 CFR 310.22). Check those separately.
