# Custom-exempt limits and PRIME Act status (as of 2026-10-02)

Sources compiled:

## 1. What custom-exempt allows (9 CFR 303.1(a)(2))

The exemption covers "custom slaughter by any person of cattle, sheep, swine, or goats delivered by the owner thereof for such slaughter ... exclusively for use, in the household of such owner, by him and members of his household and his nonpaying guests and employees." Conditions on the custom operator:

- (i) Sanitation per 9 CFR 416.1-416.6, with listed exceptions.
- (ii) Any product for sale is kept "separate and apart" from custom product.
- (iii) Custom product is "plainly marked 'Not for Sale' as provided in § 316.16 ... immediately after being prepared and are kept so identified until delivered to the owner."
- (iv) Equipment shared with an official establishment is cleaned and sanitized before inspected work.
- (b)(3) Records of "numbers and kinds of livestock slaughtered on a custom basis ... and the names and addresses of the owners."
- (b)(4) Edible articles not delivered to the owner must be denatured per § 325.13.

**Contrarian reading [inferred]:**
- The exemption is about who owns the animal and who eats the meat, not about the type of facility. A custom-exempt mobile unit cannot generate retail, restaurant, or farmers-market product.
- The producer's whole market is limited to buyers who own the live animal (whole, half, or quarter) before slaughter.
- "Cow-share" or "beef share" arrangements are legally sound only if the buyer truly owns part of the live animal before slaughter. If the arrangement is really a sale of packaged meat, it becomes uninspected meat in commerce under the FMIA.
- The operator's record of owner names and addresses is the paper trail an enforcement review would examine.
- Not verified in this source: FSIS's specific guidance on animal-share ownership tests, and Virginia/VDACS enforcement history. Both need a follow-up source.

## 2. PRIME Act (119th Congress): introduced, not advanced

- H.R. 4700, "Processing Revival and Intrastate Meat Exemption Act," introduced 2025-07-23 by Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY). About 45 original cosponsors are listed on the bill text, bipartisan (including Pingree, Leger Fernandez, Golden, Huffman, Tokuda, and Virginia's Cline). Referred to House Agriculture. GovTrack status as of 2026-10-02: **introduced**.
- S. 2409 companion, Sen. Angus King (I-ME), introduced 2025-07-23. GovTrack status: **introduced**.
- Substance (Sec. 2, new FMIA §23(b)): inspection requirements "shall not apply" to slaughter and preparation at a custom slaughter facility if it is done under state law and the meat goes "exclusively" to (i) "household consumers within the State" and (ii) in-state restaurants, hotels, boarding houses, grocery stores or other establishments that serve meals to consumers or sell meat directly to consumers.
- Sec. 3: no preemption of state law. Each state would still have to opt in or regulate.
- [inferred] Even if enacted, PRIME does nothing in Virginia until the General Assembly or VDACS sets up a state framework. A business plan should not depend on it.

## 3. The farm-bill substitute: Sec. 12114 custom-exempt sales pilot

H.R. 7567, "Farm, Food, and National Security Act of 2026," passed the House 2026-04-30 and was pending in the Senate per GovTrack as of 2026-10-02. Sec. 12114, "Pilot program to support custom slaughter establishments":

- A state department of agriculture may run a pilot letting a custom-exempt facility "sell slaughtered meat and meat food products ... directly to consumers within the State." If the state declines, USDA may run one on request.
- **Caps:** no more than 5 facilities per state (state-run) and no more than 10 facilities nationally (USDA-run). After at least 2 years, and only if there have been no emergency actions, another 5 per state or 10 nationally may be added.
- **Requirements:** sales direct to consumers in-state, only by the animal owner or the custom facility. Product is "not eligible for re-sale." The label must show facility name and address, owner name and address, where the animal was raised, slaughter date and raising period, that the product was "not subject to Federal inspection," and that it shall not be resold.
- Facilities must comply with the Humane Methods of Slaughter Act, state and local law, FMIA §23(d), custom sanitation and record rules, and SRM handling.
- Facilities face onsite inspection by USDA, and at least annually by the local restaurant-inspection authority or the state department of agriculture.
- **Ineligible:** any establishment under federal or state inspection.
- **Emergency action:** the Secretary may terminate a facility on "credible evidence" of adulteration.
- State reports are due each FY through 2031, with a Secretary's report to Congress within 2 years.
- Compared with PRIME, the pilot drops restaurant and grocery sales and caps how many facilities can take part.
- Sec. 12112 of the same bill would direct FSIS to publish scale-appropriate model HACCP plans for slaughter-only small/very small establishments within 18 months. That is relevant to an inspected MSU.

## Risk implications for a Valley mobile beef operation [inferred]

1. Plan revenue on today's law: custom-exempt means live-animal shares only, every package marked "Not for Sale," and no retail.
2. PRIME has stalled at introduction across several Congresses (this bill was reintroduced in 2025). Treat it as optionality only.
3. The pilot, if enacted, allows at most 5 Virginia facilities. Admission is competitive and revocable, and it adds labeling and inspection overhead.
4. Being a pilot participant excludes being state- or USDA-inspected, so an operator cannot run both models under one establishment.
