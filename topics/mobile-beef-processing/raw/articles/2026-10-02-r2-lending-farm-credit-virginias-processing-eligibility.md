## Federal eligibility rules

These rules bind every Farm Credit association.

### 12 CFR 613.3020: farm-related service businesses

- **Who qualifies:** "(a) An individual or legal entity that furnishes farm-related services to farmers and ranchers that are directly related to their agricultural production is eligible".
- **What can be financed:** if more than 50% of the business's income comes from such services, all of its business activities can be financed. Otherwise only the service activities can.
- **Limit, (c):** financing is limited to "necessary capital structures, equipment, and initial working capital."

### 12 CFR 613.3010: processing or marketing operations

A borrower is eligible if any one of these applies:
- **(a)(1)** It is a bona fide farmer who "regularly produces some portion of the throughput".
- **(a)(2)** Farmer-borrowers own more than 50% of the voting stock or equity and supply some throughput.
- **(a)(3)** Farmer-borrowers own 50% or less but exercise majority voting or management control.
- **(a)(4)** Farmer-borrowers own at least 25%, supply at least 20% of throughput, and have board representation.
- **(a)(5)** It is an outgrowth entity that is functionally integrated with an eligible farmer and gets at least 20% of its throughput, or at least 50% of the farmer's output, from that farmer.

**Portfolio cap, (b):** loans to processors whose farmer-owners supply less than 20% of throughput are capped at 15% of the association's retail loans. Such loans are therefore available but rationed.

**(d)(2):** boards must adopt procedures that "encourage financing under paragraph (a)(4) ... of credit-worthy entities whose operations directly benefit producers, have local community investment support and provide accessible ownership opportunities for local farmers and ranchers."

## Implications for the MSU [inferred]

**Path A: farm-related service business (613.3020).** On-farm custom slaughter for producers is arguably a service "directly related to their agricultural production", like custom harvesting. That would make even a non-farmer-owned MSU eligible for:
- the trailer (equipment)
- a cooler or cut-room building (capital structure)
- initial working capital

Whether slaughter counts as "production" or as "processing" is a judgment for the association. Ask the loan officer directly.

**Path B: farmer-owned processor (613.3010).** If the founder raises cattle and supplies some of the throughput, or farmer-members own at least 25% and supply at least 20%, eligibility is clear under (a)(1), (a)(2) or (a)(4).

**Combined approach.** FarmLaunch plus an FSA guarantee could cover a beginning-farmer founder. Leasing could take the trailer off the balance sheet. Farm Credit is the strongest-fit commercial lender because, unlike FSA (see the r2 FSA file), it can lend to a service or processing business.
