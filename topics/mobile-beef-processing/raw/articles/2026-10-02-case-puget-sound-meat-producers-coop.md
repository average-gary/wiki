# Puget Sound Meat Producers Cooperative (PSMPC)

**Status note at the top of the page (January 2021):** "PSMPC is no longer in business. The unit is being operated by Heritage Meats and is parked in one location. All information below is from 2013." In the 2022 Rusted Gate plan, Heritage Meats (Stanwood, WA) appears as a "fixed mobile slaughter unit" charging $216/hd.

## Basics (2011-2013)
- A nonprofit cooperative of ranchers, farmers, butchers and restaurant owners. Its USDA-inspected red-meat MSU served King, Kitsap, Lewis, Mason, Pierce and Thurston counties. Inspected operations began in **fall 2009**.
- **Ownership:** "The MSU is owned by the Pierce Conservation District, leased by the Puget Sound Meat Producers Cooperative, and operated by a PSMPC member on a contract basis." The lease is **$1/year**. The District "took out a loan for part of the necessary capital" and covers the payments.
- Services: slaughter, plus delivery of carcasses to USDA cut-and-wrap plants (Minder Meats, Bremerton; Heritage Meats, Rochester). Custom-exempt and retail-exempt butchers also use the unit, either to get inspected carcasses to sell from their own counters or to do custom-exempt slaughter.
- Capacity: **8-10 animal units/day** (1 cow = 2 pigs = 3 sheep/goats). Up to 8 hours under inspection, including a 30-minute pre-inspection. About 90 processing days were estimated for 2012. The trailer is 45 ft, built by TriVan. It is self-contained (diesel generator, water, hot water, refrigeration) and also has a backup hot water and filtration system.
- Volume: **516 animals in 2010**, **850 in 2011**. As of September 2013 (year to date): 266 beef, 156 pigs, 129 sheep, 17 goats, more than 219,000 lb of meat worth over $750k to members.
- As of spring 2012 the unit traveled between **two fixed slaughter sites**, one weekly and one every other week. It was not doing farm-by-farm visits.

## Fees and finances
- Members: beef **$110**, pigs $70, sheep/goats $50. Non-members: beef **$140**, pigs $90, sheep/goats $70.
- Surcharges: cattle over 30 months, pigs over 275 lb, sheep/goats over 100 lb. "Mileage, disposal, stall, toll, and other fees may also apply."
- Start-up was "$500,000 (est.)": trailer $250,000; equipment $12,000; design/initial management $35,000; outreach $30,000/yr.
- Operating costs were **$57,000 in 2011**. Net income in 2011 was about **$11,000**, after paying the butcher and covering operations and maintenance.
- Contract model: the butcher is paid "**a $300 daily rate (for set-up and transport) plus a fee per animal unit**". He pays his own assistants and buys all consumables. Producer fees include a PSMPC margin for maintenance, repairs, equipment replacement, insurance, lab fees, legal fees/permits and site improvements.
- Membership: 57 voting members and 25 associate members.

## History
- Southern Puget Sound had "lost all USDA-inspected slaughter facilities for large animals" over 20 years. A November 2007 agricultural summit in Enumclaw led to the co-op forming in February 2008.
- The Conservation District approved funding in June 2008, relying on a UW student feasibility study (Georgine Yorgey).
- The unit was delivered in June 2009. The grant of inspection and WSDA organic certification came two months later, and the first slaughter was in October 2009.

## Lessons (near-bankruptcy)
- "Two critical challenges which together nearly led to bankruptcy":
  1. "Co-op membership and numbers of livestock processed by the MSU have both been dramatically lower than originally anticipated, based on the original feasibility study." Only about **20% of members used the unit regularly** in 2011. Many members lacked established meat markets. Record-high cattle prices also made selling live at auction attractive.
  2. Management cost. The co-op originally had "nearly a half-dozen paid staff" (manager, site coordinator, lead butcher, assistants). "This did not prove sustainable." By 2010 it had moved to one contract butcher plus volunteer board scheduling and bookkeeping.
- The unit stayed viable because (a) the public owner absorbed capital cost ($1 lease), and (b) the contract butcher "can incorporate the MSU into his existing processing business."
- Implication [inferred]: an MSU survives when it is a capacity add-on to an existing butcher business, not a standalone employer. It eventually ended up parked as a fixed unit at a plant.
