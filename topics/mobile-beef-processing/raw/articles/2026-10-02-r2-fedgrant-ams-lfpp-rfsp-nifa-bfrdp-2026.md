# AMS LAMP (LFPP, RFSP) and NIFA BFRDP: FY2026

Sources:
- ams.usda.gov/services/grants/lfpp and /rfsp (live, fetched 2026-10-02);
- the FY26 LFPP NOFO PDF (ams.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media/AMS_LFPP_NOFO.pdf);
- Grants.gov records 361995 (LFPP), 361992 (RFSP) and 362376 (BFRDP).

## Local Food Promotion Program (LFPP), part of LAMP

- **Status.** "The FY26 grant application period is now closed. Applications were due June 5, 2026." The Grants.gov notice was posted Apr 21, 2026, with $13,840,000 estimated, about 55 awards, a $25,000 floor and a $500,000 ceiling. FY2025 ran May 9 to Jun 27, 2025. [inferred] On that cadence, the next round would be around spring 2027.
- **Purpose.** To support "local and regional food business enterprises that engage as intermediaries in indirect producer-to-consumer marketing". Eligible applicants must "support local and regional food business enterprises that process, distribute, aggregate, or store locally or regionally produced food products."
- **Eligible applicants.**
  - Agricultural businesses and cooperatives;
  - CSA networks;
  - food councils;
  - economic development corporations;
  - local governments;
  - nonprofit and public benefit corporations;
  - producer networks or associations;
  - regional farmers' market authorities;
  - tribal governments.

  The NOFO defines a Producer Network or Association as "A producer group or member-owned organizations or businesses that provide, offer, or sell agricultural products or services through a common distribution system for the mutual member benefit."
- **Project types and sizes** (NOFO §3.3):
  - Planning: $25,000-$100,000 over 24 months. Example activities include "Hiring experts for training on managing a local/regional food storage or processing facility" and "Devising a business development plan associated with the processing/marketing of local/regional agricultural products."
  - Implementation: $100,000-$500,000 over 36 months, "to establish a new food enterprise or market channel or to improve or expand an existing" one.
  - Two Turnkey types: $50,000-$100,000 each.
- **Cost share.** "25 percent of the total Federal portion of the grant."
- **Not eligible** (NOFO §3.3.5). Projects may not:
  - "Benefit only one agricultural producer, vendor, entity, or individual. Applicants must collaborate with others";
  - "buy land, or for construction of a building or structure";
  - "Include legal fees and other costs associated with establishing a business or organization";
  - "Provide capital for revolving loan funds or seed equity funds";
  - pay agricultural production expenses, including farm equipment;
  - "Depend upon the completion of another project or the receipt of another grant".
- **Award limit.** One LFPP award per funding cycle. An open Planning award must be closed out before an Implementation award.
- **Priority.** Projects in a low-income/low-access census tract (ERS Food Access Research Atlas), or projects carried out under an RFSP partnership agreement.

## Regional Food System Partnerships (RFSP), part of LAMP

- **Status.** Closed June 5, 2026 (posted Apr 21, 2026). Estimated funding was $4,710,000, with about 10 awards of $100,000-$1,000,000. "In FY2025, AMS funded 7 (6.5%) of the eligible applications received an awarded a total of $4,712,934."
- **Purpose.** "support partnerships that connect public and private resources to plan and develop local and regional food systems."
- **Eligible entities.**
  - producers;
  - farmer or rancher cooperatives;
  - producer networks or associations;
  - "majority-controlled producer-based business venture";
  - food councils, local governments, nonprofits, economic development corporations and tribes.

  Eligible partners include state agencies, universities, private corporations and Farm Credit lenders.
- **Cost share.** "cash-only cost share contributions in an amount equal to 25 percent of the total Federal portion."

## Beginning Farmer and Rancher Development Program (BFRDP), NIFA

- **Status.** USDA-NIFA-BFR-011336 was posted May 13, 2026 and closed **June 16, 2026**. Estimated funding was $44,443,140, with awards of $49,999-$750,000 and cost sharing required.
- **Eligibility.** "Applications may only be submitted by a collaborative state, tribal, local, or regionally based network or partnership of" qualifying organizations. The goal is "education, outreach, and technical assistance" for beginning farmers and ranchers.
- [inferred] BFRDP cannot fund a processing business directly. At most, it could pay for a Virginia extension or nonprofit partnership to run beginning-producer training that includes on-farm harvest.

## Fit for a start-up mobile beef slaughter unit [inferred]

- **No existing-operation test.** Unlike MPPEP Phase 4 (one year of inspected operation) and the new REAP rule (12 months as an existing business), LFPP and RFSP state no prior-operation requirement. A newly formed producer cooperative or network is a listed eligible applicant.
- **The single-beneficiary bar.** A single-owner custom-exempt business would need to frame the project around multiple producers, for example a producer network using the unit, to clear "Benefit only one agricultural producer."
- **Custom-exempt fit is weak.** LFPP is about marketing local food through intermediaries. Under custom exemption, the meat belongs to the animal owner and cannot be sold. A pure custom-exempt service is therefore a weak fit. An inspected or producer-marketing model (cooperative brand, aggregation, cold storage, distribution) fits better.
- **What can be funded.** LFPP does not fund building construction or land. Whether the slaughter trailer or equipment is an allowable Implementation cost falls to the AMS GT&C. Equipment is not listed as unallowable except for agricultural-production equipment.
- **Best near-term use.** An LFPP Planning grant ($25K-$100K; 25% match) for a producer group's feasibility study and business plan is the cleanest fit, targeting the spring 2027 round.
