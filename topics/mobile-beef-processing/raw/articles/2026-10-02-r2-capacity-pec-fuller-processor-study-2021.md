# PEC Study Seeks to Aid Local Meat Processors and Farmers (2021)

Published in *The Piedmont View*, PEC's member newsletter. Posted 2021-09-20, updated 2024-02-15. By John McCarthy. The underlying Fuller Consulting report is not linked from the article; no PDF was found on the page.

## Study design

- Partners were the Piedmont Environmental Council (PEC) and American Farmland Trust (AFT). The Prince Charitable Trusts funded it, and Fuller Consulting, "a nationally recognized expert on animal processing operations", did the work.
- "Visiting the region's **seven USDA-certified animal processing facilities—in Winchester, Stephens City, Middletown, Harrisonburg, Lexington, Bealeton, and Lynchburg**—we documented operations, capacities, staffing, challenges and opportunities."

## Capacity and wait-time findings

- "Local farmers turned increasingly to small, local processors, who found themselves **operating at 125-150 percent of their normal capacity** and stressed by storage limitations and labor needs."
- Producer Jacob Gilley (Heaven's Hollow Farm, Madison County): "small producers are accustomed to dealing with **processing slots six to nine months away**, but suddenly, **wait times for processing cattle were 12-18 months** with little flexibility."
- Gilley: "processing could be a bottleneck for expansion and a real challenge for our farm."

## Outcomes

1. **Expansion possibilities**: "Fuller identified expansion potential at two facilities during our visits and subsequently provided detailed plans for equipment acquisition and process modifications that would **increase production from 15 percent to as much as 50 percent**." The plans were positioned for USDA expansion grant applications.
2. **Processor asset map**: an interactive online map built by Lightbulb Machine (Charlottesville), with processor locations, contacts, and operations.
3. **Labor**: "The high demand and short supply of trained meat cutters was a challenge for **every processor interviewed**. Bringing untrained people in... forces slowdowns in operations and compromises safety." AFT, PEC, and the Rappahannock Center for Education built a butcher training program for late fall 2021 (rappce.org/course/butcher_program/). Virginia Tech and Virginia State expressed interest in offering it too.
4. **Value-added niches**: one opportunity raised is a facility specializing in beef sticks and jerky for cull cattle.
5. Guidance for "non-USDA-certified processors interested in certifying", tied to a USDA grant program announced at the time.
6. "**Virginia Tech and Virginia State are embarking on a similar assessment of meat processors statewide.**" (The statewide study's results were not located in this round.)

## Relevance [inferred]

- This is independent confirmation of T&E's own account (16 months out in Sept 2020). Pre-COVID baseline waits in the region were already 6-9 months. That gap is the market a mobile or custom unit would fill.
- Every plant reported cutter labor as the binding constraint, more than kill-floor capacity. A mobile slaughter unit relieves the kill floor, but it still needs a cut-and-wrap partner or its own cutting room.
