# VDACS AFID Infrastructure Grant Program

Sources:
- Program page: Wayback snapshot (2026) of https://www.vdacs.virginia.gov/agriculture-afid-infrastructure-grants.shtml
- Guidelines PDF, fetched live: https://www.vdacs.virginia.gov/pdf/AFID-infrastructure-grant-guidelines.pdf

## Current status (program page)
- "Fall 2026 AFID Infrastructure Grant Status | Open September 14 - October 30."
- The program funds "capital projects at new and existing food hubs, farmers' markets, commercial kitchens and other value-added facilities such as those used for processing and packaging meats, dairy products, produce, or other Virginia agricultural products."
- The program page lists Joyce Blankenship, Project Manager (804-786-1906). The guidelines PDF lists Rachel Meyers, Program Manager (804-786-6911). The PDF contact may be outdated.

## Guidelines (key rules)
- Grants are "up to $50,000 per grant to political subdivisions for community infrastructure development projects that support local food production and sustainable agriculture." They are competitive, paid as reimbursements, and require a match.
- "Community infrastructure" means a facility that serves "(i) smaller, newer or limited-resource farmers, ranchers or food entrepreneurs; (ii) farmers or ranchers using sustainable agriculture practices; or (iii) startup farms and food enterprises."
- Priority project types include "Processing facilities that are primarily locally owned, including commercial kitchens, packaging and labeling facilities, **animal slaughtering facilities**, or other facilities... primarily utilized for the processing of meats." Extra consideration goes to projects that benefit multiple small producers or are located in economically distressed communities.
- **Eligibility:** applications "must be made by a political subdivision of the Commonwealth, typically Counties and Cities, but also Towns, Industrial and Economic Development Authorities, or Planning District Commissions." Multi-locality applications are allowed, with one lead locality. A locality can receive only one award per round.
- **Match:** generally 1:1. Economically distressed localities or projects benefiting multiple small-scale producers qualify for a reduced 2:1 match. Up to 50% of the match may be in-kind or "Other Match", which includes federal grants, nonprofits, and the Tobacco Region Revitalization Commission.
- **Rounds:** two per fiscal year. Fall awards are announced by December 31 and Spring awards by June 30.
- **Grant period:** two years, with up to one more year by extension (requested at least 90 days before the end). An interim report is due at one year. Up to 75% of funds can be reimbursed before the end of the grant period.
- **Allowable costs (Appendix A):** construction or renovation of existing facilities, including wastewater structures. Special-purpose equipment is allowed, including "processing equipment, packing and labeling equipment, or delivery vehicles." **Unallowable:** general-purpose equipment such as general-use motor vehicles, lease-to-own arrangements, marketing costs, and purchases of land or buildings.

## Award history (awardees PDF, updated January 9, 2023)
- "Twenty-nine grants totaling $742,772 have been awarded to twenty-four unique localities through three grant rounds" (2021-2022).
- **Meat processing awards:**
  - Floyd County EDA / Firehouse Farms, December 2021, **$23,124**. A new facility that "will open as a custom-exempt facility later this winter and will become a USDA-inspected facility in two to three years". The grant paid for saws, a grinder, and a vacuum sealer.
  - City of Harrisonburg / T&E Meats, December 2021, **$25,000**. A "small, USDA-inspected red meat slaughterhouse... in operation since 1940 and serves roughly 300 producers each year". The grant replaced the packing-room cooling system and bought a patty machine. This is the nearest inspected slaughter competitor or partner to the target area [inferred].
  - Orange County / Piedmont Processing, June 2022, **$25,000**. A walk-in freezer added 40% cooler space. The facility serves 75 to 100 local producers, and its growth "is currently limited by insufficient cooler space for aging beef."
  - Franklin County / KC Farms Meats, December 2022, **$33,333**. A custom processor serving "39 livestock producers in the region who sell directly to their customers". The grant bought a smoker and a walk-in freezer.
  - Albemarle County / Kelly Turkeys, December 2022, $24,000 (poultry).
- **Mobile precedent:** Northampton County / Ruby Salts Oyster Cooperative, June 2022, $25,000, for a "pontoon barge to act as a mobile processing facility."
- **Shenandoah County precedent:** Cool Connections / Jon Henry General Store (New Market), December 2021, $25,000, flash-freezing for a produce aggregator.

## Relevance [inferred]
- A mobile beef unit could apply through Frederick County, the Winchester EDA, or the Northern Shenandoah Valley Regional Commission (a PDC). The "multiple small-scale producers" criterion fits a shared mobile unit and could cut the match to 2:1. The $50,000 cap makes AFID a gap-filler, for example for a refrigerated trailer or cooler, not a primary funder.
- Whether a custom-built mobile slaughter trailer counts as "special purpose equipment" or as an unallowable "general use motor vehicle" should be confirmed with VDACS. The oyster barge precedent suggests it would be treated as special purpose.
- The awardee list is dated January 2023. Rounds from 2023 to 2026 were not captured here.
