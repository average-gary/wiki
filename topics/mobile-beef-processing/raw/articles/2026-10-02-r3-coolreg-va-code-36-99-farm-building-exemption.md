# Va. Code §§ 36-97 and 36-99: USBC and the farm building exemption

Both sections were fetched from law.lis.virginia.gov on 2026-10-02 (current Code of Virginia text).

## § 36-99. Provisions of Code; modifications

**(A)** The Uniform Statewide Building Code (USBC) prescribes regulations for construction, rehabilitation and maintenance of buildings and their equipment. It also sets procedures by which the local building department grants modifications, "provided the spirit and functional intent of the Building Code are observed". Buildings "should be permitted to be constructed ... at the least possible cost consistent with recognized standards of health, safety, energy conservation and water conservation, including provisions necessary to prevent overcrowding, rodent or insect infestation, and garbage accumulation".

**(B)** The Board follows national standards (ICC, NFPA). Then:

> "Notwithstanding the provisions of this section, farm buildings and structures shall be exempt from the provisions of the Building Code, except for a building or a portion of a building located on a farm that is operated as a restaurant as defined in § 35.1-1 and licensed as such by the Board of Health ... However, farm buildings and structures lying within a flood plain or in a mudslide-prone area shall be subject to flood-proofing regulations or mudslide regulations, as applicable."

A farm building where (i) the public is invited in for agritourism and (ii) farm products are displayed, sampled or sold must have:
1. portable fire extinguishers;
2. a simple written emergency plan;
3. a sign at the entrance reading "This building is EXEMPT from the Uniform Statewide Building Code. Be alert to exits in the event of a fire or other emergencies." The sign must be at least 24 x 36 inches, with black letters at least 1 inch high.

**(C)** Code provisions should be performance-based where practical.

## § 36-97. Definitions: "Farm building or structure"

> "a building or structure not used for residential purposes, located on property where farming operations take place, and used primarily for any of the following uses or combination thereof:
> 1. Storage, handling, production, display, sampling, or sale of agricultural, horticultural, floricultural, or silvicultural products produced in the farm;
> 2. Sheltering, raising, handling, processing, or sale of agricultural animals or agricultural animal products;
> 3. Business or office uses relating to the farm operations;
> 4. Use of farm machinery or equipment or maintenance or storage of vehicles, machinery, or equipment on the farm;
> 5. Storage or use of supplies and materials used on the farm; or
> 6. Implementation of best management practices associated with farm operations."

"Building" means "a combination of any materials, whether portable or fixed, having a roof to form a structure for the use or occupancy by persons or property."

## Application to a hanging/aging cooler [inferred]
- **The textual argument for exemption:** a cooler on land "where farming operations take place", used primarily for "handling, processing ... of ... agricultural animal products", fits use 2. Use 2 does not carry the "produced in the farm" qualifier that use 1 has. On that reading, a carcass cooler on a working cattle farm, even one holding other owners' beef, could be a farm building exempt from the USBC (no building permit, no plan review, no certificate of occupancy).
- **The limits:**
  - The parcel must actually host "farming operations", and the cooler must be used "primarily" for that purpose.
  - A cooler on a non-farm commercial lot is not exempt.
  - The building official makes the call. Most Virginia localities ask for a farm-building exemption form or affidavit; Frederick County's form was not checked.
  - Flood-plain rules still apply.
- **What the exemption does not remove:**
  - zoning (see the Frederick County RA file, where the use may still need a CUP);
  - VDACS sanitation standards (9 CFR 416.2 construction);
  - VDH water and sewage permits;
  - electrical utility service requirements.
  - Whether the Statewide Fire Prevention Code reaches a farm building was not checked.
- **Insurers and lenders** (Farm Credit, FSA) may still require code-equivalent construction. Inspected walk-in cooler panels and a licensed refrigeration contractor are prudent either way.
- **If the mobile unit docks to cut,** the building itself still only houses storage and handling, so the exemption analysis is unchanged. The zoning analysis does change.
