# 1. Executive Summary

**The business.** A custom-exempt mobile beef service for Virginia's northern Shenandoah Valley. A
mobile unit **harvests cattle on the owner's farm**. The carcass hangs and ages for 7–21 days in a
**company-owned cooler**. The same mobile unit then **cuts, vacuum-packs and labels** the beef while
docked at the cooler. Customers are cattle owners (freezer-beef producers) and the households that
buy their animals in halves and quarters before slaughter.

**Why now.**

- Regional USDA plants ran at **125–150% of normal capacity** in 2021, with producer waits of 12–18 months.
- Virginia's own five-year plan records **18–24 month waits**, explicitly backs investment in custom-exempt capacity, and
  notes that the whole state depends on **a single renderer**.
- The nearest USDA plant to Winchester still books **3–5 months out** in 2026.

**Why this model.**

- **It's legal.** VDACS expressly allows a custom-exempt "mobile slaughter and processing unit" under a **free annual Custom
  Permit**. There is no inspector to schedule, no HACCP validation, and no per-site federal paperwork.
- **It avoids the usual failure.** The documented reason mobile units fail is the lack of a partner to hang and cut
  their carcasses: one California unit sat idle for about two years. Owning the cooler and the cutting removes that
  dependency.

**What it is not.** Custom-exempt beef is marked "Not for Sale", so it **cannot be sold at retail, to
restaurants, at farmers' markets or online**. Customers must own the live animal before slaughter. Moving
to Virginia state inspection or USDA inspection later is possible but is a separate, more expensive plan.

- **Capital required:** about **$470,000**, dominated by the mobile kill-and-cut unit (~$300,000 list).
- **Break-even with a $50,000 owner draw:** about **275 head a year**, about 5–6 a week.
- **Capacity:** two people top out near **300–400 head a year**, because cutting takes about 8 labor-hours per custom beef.

**The three things that decide go/no-go:**

1. **Zoning for the cooler site.** In Frederick County's Rural Areas (RA) district, any place where meat is
   "processed" is a *slaughterhouse* use, which needs a conditional use permit. A commercially zoned
   parcel with public sewer may be simpler.
2. **Wastewater.** Blood and wash water are regulated "industrial wastes". Blood capture plus public sewer at the cooler is
   the clean route.
3. **Equipment cost and labor.** Real quotes for the mobile unit, and a second skilled cutter.

**Financing approach.** No grant program funds a new for-profit processor directly. The realistic stack is:

- **Farm Credit of the Virginias** equipment debt;
- **Virginia Small Business Financing Authority cash collateral** to cover lenders' discount on specialized equipment;
- the **cooler as an AFID Infrastructure grant project** (≤$50,000, applied for by a county);
- a free or low-fee **VAFAIRS** business-plan review.

**Immediate next step:** a 90-day diligence sprint (Section 11), starting with a written zoning use
determination and equipment quotes.

# 2. The Business

## 2.1 Service

| Step | Where | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Booking | Phone / online | Owner books a date and signs the cut sheet. For shared animals, the list of owners is recorded **before slaughter** (VDACS requirement) |
| 2. Harvest | Owner's farm | Mobile unit kills, skins and eviscerates. Offal is composted on that farm where allowed, or tubbed for the renderer. **Blood is captured**, not spread. Carcass chills in the unit's onboard cooler |
| 3. Hang and age | Company cooler | Sides or quarters hang 7–21 days at about 34°F |
| 4. Cut and pack | Mobile unit docked at the cooler | Cut to the owner's sheet, ground, vacuum-packed, labelled "Not for Sale", frozen |
| 5. Pickup | Company cooler | Owner collects. Optional delivery |

## 2.2 Customers

- **Primary:** cattle producers selling freezer beef by the half or quarter, who currently wait months
  for a slot or haul 30–100+ miles.
- **Secondary:** homesteaders and small herds finishing a few head for their own households.
- **Not customers:** retailers, restaurants, or anyone buying packaged meat. That requires inspection.

## 2.3 Value proposition

The service is priced at or above fixed-plant rates. It sells **time and convenience**, not a discount:

- no months-long queue
- no hauling live cattle, and no stress on the animals
- a harvest date the producer chooses, on their own farm
- one provider from field to freezer

## 2.4 Ownership structure (decision pending)

| Structure | Opens | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| Sole operator LLC | Simplest; Farm Credit (as a farm-related service business), VSBFA | No co-op grants; FSA operating loans barred |
| **Farmer-majority LLC or cooperative** | Farm Credit eligibility on the cleanest basis; FSA loans for members buying co-op stock; USDA Value-Added Producer Grants | Governance overhead. Co-op governance problems are a documented cause of mobile-unit failure |

# 4. Regulatory Path

## 4.1 Why custom-exempt

| | Custom-exempt *(this plan)* | Virginia state inspection | USDA inspection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who may buy the meat | Only the owner of the live animal | Anyone, **in Virginia only** (Virginia is not in the CIS interstate program) | Anyone, any state |
| Permit | Free VDACS Custom Permit (2VAC5-210-30), renewed each June 30 | Grant of inspection (form VDACS-03090); HACCP plan; 90-day validation | FSIS grant; HACCP; inspector at every kill |
| Mobile units | Expressly allowed, slaughter **and processing** | Not addressed | Same rules as a fixed plant, plus per-site paperwork |
| Product marking | "Not for Sale" | Virginia inspected mark | USDA mark |

**Rules the business must follow** (9 CFR 303.1; VDACS custom guide):

- Records of every owner's name and address.
- Sanitation under 9 CFR 416.1–416.6.
- Humane slaughter.
- Removal and segregation of specified risk materials (SRMs). For cattle 30 months or older: skull, brain, eyes, spinal
  cord and vertebral column. For all cattle: tonsils and distal ileum.
- **Charging only a service fee**, never a price for the meat.
- Shares are allowed if the owners are listed before slaughter.

**Pending federal law** (PRIME Act; the farm-bill custom-exempt pilot, capped at 5 facilities per state) could
later allow limited in-state direct sales. **The plan assumes neither.**

## 4.3 Zoning: the main risk

In Frederick County's Rural Areas (RA) district:

- **Slaughterhouses are a conditional use**, not allowed by right. The county defines a slaughterhouse as an
  establishment "primarily engaged in the slaughtering **or processing** of meats".
- The RA district has **no cold-storage use**.
- Storage of farm products is allowed by right only if the farm produced more than half of what it stores.

So a cooler where the mobile unit docks to cut is almost certainly a conditional "slaughterhouse" use. The permit
would require:

- buildings at least 100 ft from property lines
- a 20,000 sq ft building cap
- **all operations under roof**, meaning a canopy or bay over the docked unit
- screening, plus public hearings

The alternative is a **commercially or industrially zoned parcel with public sewer**.

## 5.3 Waste

| Stream | Plan | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Offal, head, bones | Compost on the farm where the animal was killed, or tubbed for the renderer within 7 days | 9VAC20-81-95 D.4 conditionally exempts carcass composting at the farm of generation; confirm DEQ's reading |
| SRMs from cattle ≥30 months | Segregated and dyed for the renderer; surcharge to the owner | 21 CFR 589.2001 feed ban |
| Blood and first rinse | **Captured** and hauled with the offal | Captured blood cuts wastewater strength from 1,000–2,500 mg/l BOD to about 127 mg/l |
| Wash water at farm kills | Onboard holding tank *[inferred]* | DEQ "industrial waste"; whether the agricultural exclusion applies is unsettled |
| Wash water at the cooler dock | **Public sewer**, with a grease and solids interceptor | Frederick Water specifications; septic would need an engineered system |
| Renderer | Darling Ingredients (Winchester or Linville) | The only renderer statewide (RD894). Fees and minimums unknown |

## 5.4 Scheduling and season

- **Booking:** committed slaughter dates booked ahead, with a deposit and a no-show fee. This is the documented fix for
  no-shows.
- **Peak:** demand peaks September–December. February–April is slow, so use it for maintenance, training and
  winter-finished cattle.
- **Cooler space:** cooler rail space is the scheduling constraint, at about 2–3 weeks of kill volume on the rail.

# 10. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood / impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Zoning denies or delays the cooler and dock | High / high | Get a written use determination first. Compare Shenandoah, Clarke and Page counties. Consider a commercial parcel with sewer |
| Wastewater: no sewer, or DEQ objects to farm wash water | Medium / high | Capture blood and first rinse, carry an onboard holding tank, choose a sewered site, get DEQ's position in writing |
| Can't hire or keep a second cutter | High / high | Owner cuts. Simplified cut menu. Seasonal cutter. RappCE pipeline. Pay above the $20/hr mean |
| Volume below plan, or uneven across the year | Medium / high | Pre-sold harvest dates with deposits. Off-season pricing for winter-finished cattle. 100-mile service radius |
| Equipment cost above list, or long lead time | Medium / medium | Three quotes. Consider kill-only + cut-room trailers. Used units |
| Single renderer statewide | Medium / medium | On-farm composting where allowed. Confirm Darling's terms early |
| Food safety or customer illness | Low / high | 9 CFR 416 sanitation SOPs even though not required for custom-exempt. Product liability cover |
| Share-sale structure challenged as meat sales | Low / high | Owners listed before slaughter. Service-fee-only invoices. Records kept under 303.1(b)(3) |
| Competition: ShenValley co-op or new mobile entrants | Medium / medium | Position as feeder or partner; the co-op plans to outsource live receiving |
