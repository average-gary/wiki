# Valley Processor Landscape

| **T&E Meats / Honest Meats** (M7420) | Harrisonburg | city | Very Small | 1,000–10,000 | Y | **$75 kill + $1.05/lb** (2025) |

**Reconciling two sources (round 3).** VDACS's 2023 plan (RD894) lists Crabill's, Gore's, T&E (as "Salatin & Cloud/T&E"), Donald's and Seven Hills among **10 Talmadge-Aiken plants**. Talmadge-Aiken means **federal inspection performed by state inspectors**, which is why the same plants appear in the FSIS directory with federal grants. RD894 lists Blue Ridge Meats, Fauquier's Finest, **Gentle Harvest Custom Processing (Winchester)** and Safa Halal as FSIS-direct plants offering custom services. The two sources agree.

Virginia overall: 21 federal establishments with cattle slaughter, 20 Very Small and one Small (Seven Hills, Lynchburg).
Only one, Fauquier's Finest in Bealeton, is in volume band 4 (100,000+ head of all species).

**Virginia MSU precedent:** Virginia State University's **Small Ruminant Mobile Meat Processing
Unit** (M46728, Petersburg) holds a federal grant.

## Capacity and wait times

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| PEC/AFT/Fuller study (2021) | The region's 7 USDA plants (Winchester, Stephens City, Middletown, Harrisonburg, Lexington, Bealeton, Lynchburg) ran at **125–150% of normal capacity**. A producer reported pre-COVID waits of **6–9 months**, rising to **12–18 months**. Two plants could expand 15–50%. **"The high demand and short supply of trained meat cutters was a challenge for every processor interviewed"** ([PEC](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r2-capacity-pec-fuller-processor-study-2021.md)) |
| T&E Meats (2020) | Booked through end of 2021, about **16 months**. It suspended labor-heavy services (patties, links, specialty cuts, dry-aging). Busy May–Dec, peaking Labor Day to Thanksgiving ([T&E](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r2-capacity-te-meats-covid-backlog.md)) |
| Blue Ridge Meats (2026) | 3–5 months |
| Missouri survey (2023, peer-reviewed, n=26) | Top concern is shortage of qualified labor (3.98/5); financing is lowest (1.56/5). 61.5% at full capacity. Training a cutter takes 6–12 months ([Missouri](../../raw/papers/2026-10-02-r2-lit-syukron-su-2023-missouri-small-processors.md)) |
| **VDACS RD894 (Jan 2023)**, the state's five-year plan | Wait times **18–24 months**. Labor is the top constraint, and plants can't staff the kill floor and processing at once. Too little cooler and finished-goods space. **One renderer for the whole state.** No ARPA money spent on meat processing. Recommends standalone cut-and-wrap facilities and notes that investment in custom-exempt capacity "could free up capacity in inspected facilities" ([RD894](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r3-vastudy-vdacs-rd894-five-year-meat-capacity-plan.md)) |
| Matson / VAFAIRS (2020) literature review | Fixed beef plant pro forma at $466/head: **−$35.5k at 1,000 head, +$3.5k at 2,500, +$93k at 5,000**, on $840k–$1.68M buildings. Mobile-unit start-up "under $250,000" ([Matson](../../raw/papers/2026-10-02-r3-vastudy-matson-vafairs-small-volume-red-meat-2020.md)) |
| Fauquier Livestock Exchange (2022–24) | AFID planning grants of $20k (2022) + $9,800 (2024). A county survey found 92% of farmers want another abattoir. Farmers travel to Stephens City and Middletown; backlog about a year. Stalled on water testing as of Nov 2024 ([Fauquier](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r3-vastudy-fauquier-livestock-exchange-abattoir-afid.md)) |
| New England (2014, peer-reviewed) | Cold storage and cut-and-wrap were tighter than slaughter. Committed slaughter dates fixed no-shows ([Gwin & Thiboumery](../../raw/papers/2026-10-02-r2-lit-gwin-thiboumery-2014-institutional-entrepreneurship.md)) |

**Reading** [inferred]: four independent sources (PEC, T&E, the Missouri survey, New England) point the same
way. **The bottleneck is cutter labor and cooler space, not the kill floor.** A mobile kill unit
that doesn't bring its own cut-and-wrap capacity adds carcasses to the constrained step.
"Stephens City" in the 2021 PEC list has no cattle grant in the 2026 directory, so it may have closed.

## The coming entrant: ShenValley Meat Processing Cooperative

GO Virginia Region 8 granted Page County $87,450 (4/11/2025) to plan a cooperative facility that addresses
exactly the **aging, storage, and fabrication** bottleneck, aiming to "triple the region's cattle
processing capacity". It intends to partner out steps it won't handle, **"including live receiving"**
([GO Virginia](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r2-stgrant-go-virginia-region8-and-nsvrc.md)).
This makes it a potential cut-and-wrap partner for a mobile kill unit, not a competitor [inferred].
