# MSU Unit Economics

> **The pattern across sources.** An inspected mobile unit is a high-fixed-cost asset that sits
> idle most of the week. Labor and capital recovery are spread over too few head. The surviving
> units either had their capital donated, or sat inside an existing butcher business that
> absorbed the overhead.

Every figure below is for a **USDA-inspected** unit unless marked otherwise, and carries the year
its source reported. For the far cheaper **custom-exempt** rig, see [[custom-exempt-mobile-rig|Custom-Exempt Mobile Rig]] ([Custom-Exempt Mobile Rig](custom-exempt-mobile-rig.md)).

chill/age/cut plant was costed at $1.51M, later revised to about $0.8M for the building. That is
4–7× one MSU. See [[fixed-facility-dependency|The Fixed-Facility Dependency]] ([The Fixed-Facility Dependency](fixed-facility-dependency.md)).

## Throughput ceiling

| Source | Beef per kill day | Kill days per week | Crew |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idaho calculator (2009) | max **14** (2/hr × 7 hr) | max 4–5 | 3 butchers + 2 assistants |
| IGFC (2007–08, actual) | 9–10 | 3–4 | 2 butchers, 8 hr + 2 hr driving |
| PSMPC (2011–13, actual) | 8–10 animal units | ~90 days/yr (2012 est.) | 1 contract butcher + own assistants |
| Coast Grown (2009, actual) | ~3.6 (32 head in 9 days) | — | — |
| Rusted Gate (2022, plan) | — | **1–2** in years 1–2 | 2 butchers at 0.5 FTE |

A kill day is capped by the hours under inspection (about 8) plus set-up, driving and
clean-up. Days per week are capped by hauling carcasses back, washing and maintenance.
**Real units ran at a fraction of the theoretical ceiling.**

## Break-even: the Rusted Gate table (2022, USDA-inspected)

| Scenario | Y1 | Y2 | Y3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80% debt, $175/hd | −$57,099 | −$28,739 | −$79,227 |
| Grant-funded capital, $175/hd | −$40,328 | −$12,563 | −$62,456 |
| Grant capital + fee $200 → $240/hd | −$27,828 | +$17,310 | +$2,544 |

Volume was 500 / 700 / 1,000 head. Year 3 losses jump because both butchers go full-time. The
plan's own conclusion: "The MSU will likely not be profitable for many years, if ever". It needed a
subsidy of "$25-50K plus a year". Three agents extracted this plan independently and agree on
every figure.

## What drives cost

- **Labor.** About 75% of total cost at IGFC (2008). It is Rusted Gate's largest line, followed by
  depreciation, interest and **offal disposal**. Skilled cutters take about 2 years to train,
  and part-time butcher jobs are "difficult to find" people for.
- **Rough labor cost per head** [inferred from the 2009 Idaho figures]: a 5-person, 7-hour crew
  costs about $860 per kill day, or about $61/head at 14 head/day before capital. At 4 head/day it
  is about $215/head.
- **Capital recovery per head** [inferred, Idaho 2009]: about $43k/yr in loan payments ÷ 300
  head ≈ $143/head. Utilization is the lever that brings this down.
- **Distance.** IGFC caps its service area at 100 miles (1–2 hours), "the largest area the MPU
  can serve efficiently". Nevada budgeted fuel and repairs at $230/day for two units covering
  150 miles/day at 5 mpg.

## Why the optimistic projections differ

The NMPAN 2011 pro forma showed profit from year 1 ($36k in 2011 rising to $131k in 2014). It
assumes **260 full kill days a year from launch**, with marketing, rent, benefits and admin
carried by a partner cut-and-wrap. The Idaho calculator's $280k "return" is a
**retail margin on meat the operator owns**, not fee-for-service profit. **Never quote either
as evidence that a fee-for-service MSU pays.**

## A fixed-plant benchmark (ERS ERR-150, 2013)

The USDA research report modeled annual expenses at **$231k for a very small plant (4 FTE), breaking even at about 462 beef a year** (about 9 a week). A small plant (10 FTE) costs $565k and needs about 1,130 beef; a regional plant costs $4.44M and needs about 8,884. All three assume $500 processing revenue per beef. Its mobile-unit box warns that MSUs may cost **more** per head to run (fuel, maintenance, transit, limited capacity), and that kill plus a separate cut-and-wrap can cost more in total than one plant. Its success factors are a committed cut-and-wrap partner, one or two dedicated leaders, and the right scale ([ERR-150](../../raw/papers/2026-10-02-r2-lit-ers-err150-local-meat-processing-commitments.md)). Figures are in 2013 dollars.

## How the survivors made it work

- **IGFC.** Capital came from grants and donations, so there was no initial debt. 30 founding members paid $600 each.
  Members had **no other slaughter option**. Fees were raised repeatedly.
- **PSMPC.** A conservation district owned the unit and carried the loan, leasing it to the co-op for $1/yr. The co-op
  switched from about 6 paid staff to one contract butcher at **$300/day plus a per-animal fee**,
  who folded the unit into his existing business. It still closed by January 2021, and the unit now
  sits parked at one plant.
