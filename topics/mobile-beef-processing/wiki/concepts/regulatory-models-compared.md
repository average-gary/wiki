# Regulatory Models Compared

> The single decision that fixes the rest of the plan is **who is allowed to buy the meat**.
> Mobile vs. fixed is a secondary choice. Under custom-exempt, the buyer must own the live
> animal before slaughter. Under inspection, the meat can be sold to anyone the stamp covers.

## Side by side

| | **Custom-exempt** | **USDA-FSIS inspected MSU** | **Virginia state-inspected** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal basis | 9 CFR 303.1(a)(2); Va. Code § 3.2-5405 A 9 (state parity exemption) | FMIA; 9 CFR 304, 307–314, 416, 417 | Va. Code § 3.2-5403/5404 ("at least equal to" federal) |
| Who can buy the meat | Only the **owner of the live animal**, for household, nonpaying guests and employees | Anyone: retail, restaurant, wholesale, interstate | Anyone, but **intrastate only** (Virginia is not in CIS) |
| Marking | "Not for Sale" immediately after preparation (316.16) | USDA mark of inspection | "Virginia Inspected and Passed" |
| Permit | **Free annual VDACS OMPS Custom Permit** ("permit of exemption", 2VAC5-210-30), issue date to June 30 | Grant of inspection: Form 5200-2 plus a 7-step file, then a 90-day conditional grant to validate HACCP | Form VDACS-03090 + SSOP/HACCP/recall plan; 90-day conditional grant; inspection free for five 8-hr days per shift each week |
| Mobile unit | **Expressly allowed** ("A custom exempt operation may use a mobile slaughter and processing unit", VDACS Note 7) | Allowed, held to the "same regulatory requirements" as a fixed plant | Not addressed in statute |
| Inspector on site | No; periodic VDACS review | Yes, for every kill. Notify the District Office of each move; 2–4 weeks' notice recommended | Yes |
| HACCP / SSOP | Sanitation 416.1–416.6 only (water-reuse 416.2(g)(2)–(6) excepted) | Full HACCP plan (417) **before** the grant, plus SSOPs (416.11–16) | Equal to federal |
| Records | Owner names and addresses, head counts, product types (303.1(b)(3)) | All 417.5 and 416.16 records | Equal to federal |

## Custom-exempt in Virginia: what is actually allowed

From the VDACS OMPS guide (undated, probably 2019–2022):

- **Mobile units and split work.** A mobile unit can kill on-farm, and the owner can send the carcass to a *different* custom operator for hanging and cutting. That handoff makes a **kill-only mobile unit plus partner cut shop** legal in Virginia. Field-dressed carcasses may be accepted.
- **Selling the animal is fine.** The operator may sell the live animal to the customer, then custom-slaughter it as the new owner's agent (Notes 4, 18). The sale must be priced on the live animal (live weight or per head). The operator may charge only a **service fee**, never a price for the meat (Note 17).
- **Shares are fine.** A live animal can have several owners if proof of ownership exists and the owner list exists **before slaughter** (Notes 15–16). There is no limit on head per owner. With written consent, trim from different owners may be commingled (Note 14).
- **The seller-participation trap.** If a farm lets a buyer slaughter on-site, that is personal use and needs no permit. If the *seller* helps with slaughter or processing, the facility owner needs a Custom Permit.
- **Specified risk materials (SRMs).** For cattle **≥30 months**: skull, brain, trigeminal ganglia, eyes, vertebral column, spinal cord, dorsal root ganglia. For all cattle: distal ileum and tonsils. Inedible material must be controlled so it can't end up in food.

The statute itself contains **no custom license, no fee schedule and no mention of mobile units**. Custom-exempt operation rests entirely on the federal-parity exemption in § 3.2-5405 A 9 plus VDACS administrative practice. Any construction standards for mobile units would sit in 2VAC5 regulations, which have **not been checked**.

## USDA-inspected MSU: what changes

- **Same rules as a fixed plant**, plus overhead that is specific to being mobile:
  - **Per-site wastewater approval.** Each slaughter site needs a local health-authority letter covering wastewater, and that letter is "required to obtain and uphold terms of the grant". Every new farm stop means paperwork with VDH or the county.
  - **Water.** Potability must be documented at each site, and private wells at recurring sites renewed semi-annually.
  - **Pens.** Each site needs an ante-mortem pen, a holding pen for U.S. Suspects, and a covered pen for diseased or disabled animals.
- **Inspector availability.** FSIS itself warns that staffing MSUs "may present a challenge". Schedule changes need District Office approval. A seasonal operation can request a voluntary suspension of up to 120 days.
- **Inconsistent inspection review.** At Island Grown in 2009, a three-month FSIS Food Safety Assessment demanded more E. coli testing than the co-op's lab could run. NMPAN's lesson: "EIAOs are not all alike and may interpret the same regulation differently" ([NMPAN manual](../../raw/papers/2026-10-02-risk-nmpan-msu-manual-lessons.md)).
- **Firearm stunning** means the head cannot be saved for edible product, except the tongue.
- The FSIS guide dates from about **2010**, predates the 2017 consolidation of FSIS districts, and **does not cover SRMs**. Treat it as a checklist, not current law.

## Virginia state inspection

Va. Code § 3.2-5403 requires the state program to be "at least equal to" the FMIA. § 3.2-5404 marks product "Virginia Inspected and Passed", and § 3.2-5405 A 6 funds the program 50:50 with USDA. This is the Talmadge-Aiken / state MPI structure, although the statute never uses that name.

**Contradiction resolved (round 2).** The MPIRG source file inferred that Virginia has no equal-to state program. FSIS's own state-MPI list confirms that **Virginia does run one** (VDACS OMPS). It also confirms that **Virginia is not in CIS**. Only 10 states participate (OH, ND, WI, IN, ME, MO, IA, VT, SD, MT), and West Virginia isn't one of them. State-inspected product "can only be sold within that State". Maryland and Pennsylvania have **no** state programs. A Winchester operator selling into MD, WV or DC therefore needs **USDA** inspection ([FSIS CIS/MPI lists](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r2-vareg-fsis-cis-and-state-mpi-lists.md)).

**Getting a Virginia grant of inspection** (VDACS guide, Jan 2026):
- **Application package:** form VDACS-03090, a plot plan (hand-drawn is acceptable), a water potability letter and lab report, sewage approval, SSOP, HACCP plan, recall plan, and the beef humane-handling, SRM and STEC measures.
- **Cost:** inspection is free for five consecutive 8-hour days per shift each week. Overtime is billed in tenth-hour increments, with a 2-hour call-back minimum.
- **Conditional grant:** issued after an onsite review, with 90 days to validate HACCP.
- **Mobile units:** the guide links to the FSIS mobile slaughter compliance guide but has **no mobile-unit text of its own**. Fixed-site items (plot plan, well letter, sewage letter) would need answers for each docking site ([VDACS grant guide](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r2-vareg-vdacs-applying-state-grant-inspection.md)).

**Regulations: 2VAC5-210** (not 2VAC5-60, which is a repealed livestock-markets rule):
- It adopts 9 CFR Subchapters A and E **as of the Jan 1, 2021 edition**, reading "Federal" as "Virginia" and "interstate" as "intrastate". Later FSIS amendments may not apply in Virginia yet.
- 2VAC5-210-30 requires anyone using the 9 CFR 303.1(a)(2) custom exemption to "apply for and receive a permit of exemption". This is the regulatory basis for the VDACS Custom Permit.
- **No mobile-unit provisions** ([2VAC5-210](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r2-vareg-2vac5-210-meat-poultry-regs.md)).

**Where meat may be sold** (VDACS selling guide, May 2025): farmers' markets, restaurants, retail and online sales all need USDA- or OMPS-inspected product. Red-meat sellers "may be required to apply for a Meat Handlers Permit". The guide is silent on custom-exempt shares, which confirms that custom beef has **no retail channel** ([selling guide](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r2-vareg-vdacs-guide-selling-meat-poultry.md)).

## Pending law: do not plan on it

- **PRIME Act** (H.R. 4700 / S. 2409, introduced 2025-07-23): would let custom-exempt meat be sold in-state to households and restaurants, but only where state law opts in. Status as of 2026-10-02: **introduced**. Virginia's own Rep. Cline is a cosponsor.
- **Farm bill pilot, Sec. 12114** (H.R. 7567, House-passed 2026-04-30, now in the Senate): custom-exempt facilities could sell **direct to consumers in-state**, with no resale, an "uninspected" label, and at least annual inspection. The cap is **5 facilities per state**, and inspected establishments are ineligible. Sec. 12112 of the same bill would direct FSIS to publish scale-appropriate model HACCP plans for slaughter-only small plants.
- Confidence: high on the bill text; **medium on the House-passage date and current Senate status**, which come from a single GovTrack read.

## Open regulatory questions

1. **Waste disposal at each site.** Mostly answered in round 2: on-farm composting is likely exempt under 9VAC20-81-95 D.4. See [[slaughter-waste-disposal-virginia|Slaughter Waste Disposal in Virginia]] ([Slaughter Waste Disposal in Virginia](slaughter-waste-disposal-virginia.md)). **Blood and wash-water rules (VDH, DEQ VPA) are still unsourced.**
2. ~~2VAC5~~ Resolved: 2VAC5-210 requires a permit of exemption and has no mobile-unit provisions. Still open: **does OMPS have an unwritten policy or equipment checklist for mobile custom units?**
3. ~~CIS~~ Resolved: Virginia is **not** a CIS state.
4. **Meat Handlers Permit.** What it requires, and whether a cut-and-wrap partner selling inspected product needs one.
5. **Retail exemption.** The 9 CFR 303.1(d) retail exemption excludes slaughter, and its "normal retail quantity" for cattle is 300 lb (half a carcass). Is it relevant to a cut-and-wrap partner selling inspected product?
