# Custom-Exempt Share Sales

> **The rule.** Custom-exempt revenue is a service fee charged to someone who already owns
> the animal. Any business plan line that reads like selling meat (price per pound of
> packaged product, a retail case, a farmers-market table) needs inspection.

## What Virginia permits (VDACS OMPS guide)

| Practice | Allowed? | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Operator sells live animal, then custom-slaughters it | Yes (Note 4) | Records show the ownership transfer |
| Several owners of one animal (halves, quarters) | Yes (Notes 15–16) | Proof of ownership; owner list exists **before slaughter** |
| Operator arranges purchase, slaughter, delivery as owner's agent | Yes (Note 18) | Delivered product marked "Not for Sale" |
| Sale priced on live weight or per head | Yes (Note 17) | Not priced on the meat |
| Operator charges for the meat itself | **No** | Service fee only |
| Commingling trim from different owners | Yes (Note 14) | Each owner's written consent |
| Resale or donation of the meat by the owner | **No** | 303.1(a)(2), VDACS criterion 6 |

Source: [VDACS custom-exemptions guide](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-reg-vdacs-red-meat-custom-exemptions.md).

## Where the legal risk sits

- The exemption is defined by **who owns the animal at slaughter**, not by the facility
  ([9 CFR 303.1](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-reg-9cfr-303-1-custom-exemption.md)).
- A share arrangement that is really a sale of packaged meat (priced per pound of cut product,
  shares sold after slaughter, owners not recorded in advance) becomes uninspected meat in
  commerce under the FMIA. Under Va. Code § 3.2-5415, a violation is a Class 1 misdemeanor, or a
  Class 6 felony where there is intent to defraud
  ([Va. Code Ch. 54](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-reg-va-code-3-2-ch54-meat-inspection.md)).
- The operator's own owner records (303.1(b)(3)) are the paper trail an enforcement review
  would examine.
- **Not yet sourced:** FSIS guidance on what counts as genuine ownership in a share deal, and
  any VDACS enforcement history on beef shares. Confidence on how enforcement plays out in
  practice is **low** until those are found.

## Implications for the plan [inferred]

- A custom-exempt mobile operator's customer is usually the **producer**, who in turn
  pre-sells halves and quarters to households. The operator charges a kill fee per head, and
  the cut shop charges $/lb hanging weight.
- Market size is capped by how many households will buy a quarter or half at a time. Restaurant,
  grocery and farmers-market demand is out of reach without inspection.
- Pending law would widen this only modestly: PRIME is stalled, and the farm-bill pilot caps
  participation at 5 facilities per state. See
  [[regulatory-models-compared|Regulatory Models Compared]] ([Regulatory Models Compared](regulatory-models-compared.md)).
