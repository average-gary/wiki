# Custom-Exempt Mobile Rig

> Round 1's economics were all for **inspected** units. Custom-exempt mobile slaughter is the
> older model: ERS notes "custom-exempt mobile kill trucks ... have been around for decades"
> ([ERR-150](../../raw/papers/2026-10-02-r2-lit-ers-err150-local-meat-processing-commitments.md)).
> It needs no kill compartment, no inspector station and no HACCP validation. It costs a fraction
> as much.

## What the rig is

On Sentinel's custom-exempt models, cattle are killed, skinned and eviscerated **in the field,
outside the trailer**. The trailer provides:

- **Lifting:** a hydraulic swing crane and a roof crane with 8,000–10,000 lb winches.
- **Carcass handling:** an aluminum skinning cradle, a gambrel, and a gut-barrel hoist.
- **Water:** 100–200 gal, with on-demand hot water.
- **Power:** a 6.5–13 kW generator.
- **Cooling:** a refrigerated cooler with stainless rail, where quarters or halves ride
  **hanging** to the cut shop.

It is "compact enough for a 3/4 ton pickup"
([Sentinel](../../raw/data/2026-10-02-r2-rig-sentinel-slaughter-trailer-prices.md)).

## Prices (Sentinel Trailers, Idaho Falls, 2026 list, "starting at")

| Model | Price | Label | Beef per trip (cooler) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 ft bumper pull | **$72,000** | Not USDA | 3–6, as quarters |
| 16×8 ft gooseneck | **$92,000** | Not USDA | 5–8, as quarters |
| 16×10 ft gooseneck | **$115,000** | Not USDA | 8–10, as halves |
| 30 ft gooseneck | $192,000 | USDA compliant | 8–12 |
| 53 ft semi | $300,000 | USDA compliant | kill + cooler + cut floor |

For comparison, the Rusted Gate plan put an inspected Friesla unit at about $550k turnkey (2022). Mobile
Processing Trailers & Supply (Colorado) publishes size classes but no prices. Not included above:
options, delivery, the tow vehicle, and any 2025–26 quote other than Sentinel's.

**Minimum legal rig (Washington State comparison).** WAC 16-19-100 requires only a nonporous van
body, a **2,000 lb hoist**, a tool sterilizing tank, and a built-in **40 gal** water tank. The
licence costs $25/yr. A traditional boom-truck "farm kill" rig is therefore legal there for far less than a
refrigerated trailer ([WSDA](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r2-rig-wsda-custom-farm-slaughterer-mobile-rules.md)).
**Virginia sets no comparable equipment minimum** in 2VAC5-210 or the VDACS guide. Ask OMPS what it
inspects for. Washington also requires a tag on each quarter, an owner-signed transit certificate, and
carcasses hung during transport. These are useful SOP models even where Virginia doesn't require them.

## Throughput is set by the cooler

- **Head per trip** is capped by cooler rail: 3–10 beef depending on model. That capacity, not kill speed,
  likely limits a day unless the operator unloads mid-day [inferred].
- No source gives a custom-exempt crew size or head per day. For reference, the Idaho 2009 inspected model
  assumed 2 cattle/hour.

**The pricing problem** [inferred]: at a fixed plant, the kill fee is about $75 and most revenue is in cutting. A
**kill-only** mobile service can't live on a $75 kill fee. Round 3 found the fee shape real operators use:
**a flat kill fee plus mileage plus a daily minimum, with offal haul-off as an option.** Backyard Butchery's
$500/day minimum is what actually prices a single-head farm call. **No Mid-Atlantic farm-kill operator publishes
prices online**; a scraped directory (Butcher Bud) lists about 30 "mobile" entries in Virginia, but most are fixed plants.
Getting local prices means a phone survey ([farm-kill table](../../raw/data/2026-10-02-r3-farmkill-operator-price-table.md)). Rough capital recovery: a $92k rig over 7 years is about $13k/yr
before interest, or about $44/head at 300 head/yr and about $26/head at 500.

## Kill-and-cut units (round 3)

If the unit also cuts and packages (the user's chosen model), the options are Sentinel's **53 ft semi from $300,000** with an 18 ft butcher room, or **Mobile Processing Trailers & Supply turnkey goosenecks (38–44 ft, quote only)**. The 2020 VAFAIRS review put mobile-unit start-up "under $250,000" ([Matson](../../raw/papers/2026-10-02-r3-vastudy-matson-vafairs-small-volume-red-meat-2020.md)). ## Insurance

- **Workers' comp** is rated by job code, and a farm-kill crew fits **NCCI 2081 Butchering**, the most
  expensive meat code. Example rates (Iowa, 2009): $10.89 per $100 of payroll for 2081, against $3.37 for
  code 2089 Packing House ([NMPAN](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r2-rig-nmpan-workers-comp-butchering-codes.md)).
- Other cover (liability, auto, cargo, spoilage, bailee) and dated benchmarks are in [[insurance-benchmarks|Insurance Benchmarks]] ([Insurance Benchmarks](../references/insurance-benchmarks.md)).
