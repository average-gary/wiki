# Slaughter Waste Disposal in Virginia

> **Bottom line [inferred].** **Solids:** composting on the host farm is the likely default, and rendering at
> Darling Winchester is the fallback. **Liquids (round 3):** blood and wash water are the harder
> problem. They are DEQ-regulated "industrial wastes", so the practical answer is to **capture blood
> and the first rinse and haul them with the offal**, and put the cooler and cutting dock on public
> sewer. Get DEQ's reading of both the composting exemption (D.4) and the agricultural exclusion in writing.

## The rules that apply

| Material | Rule | What it means for an on-farm custom kill |
|---|---|---|
| Offal, head, bones composted **on the host farm** | 9VAC20-81-95 **D.4**: "Composting of animal carcasses on site at the farm of generation" is conditionally exempt, "provided no open dump, hazard, or public nuisance is created" | Likely exempt, with no permit. "Carcasses", not "slaughter waste", is the wording, so confirm with DEQ |
| Offal hauled to **one central compost site** | Leaves D.4. Category IV feedstock under 9VAC20-81-310 ("animal carcasses, slaughterhouse waste, paunch manure"), the strictest compost class | A permitted compost facility; not a startup option |
| Offal in barrels awaiting a renderer | 9VAC20-81-95 **D.10.a**: putrescible waste must leave within **7 days** | Pickup schedule ≤7 days |
| Paunch / rumen contents | Ambiguous: C.1.b excludes animal manures returned to soil, while Category IV names "paunch manure" | Ask DEQ |
| Dead animals generally | Va. Code § 18.2-510: the owner must "forthwith" bury or cremate; exempts parts "used for food or in any commercial manner"; applies only in counties that adopt it | Offal sent to rendering is outside it. Check which Valley counties have adopted it |
| Burial | No burial exemption in 9VAC20-81-95 | Falls back to § 18.2-510 and DEQ/VDH water-protection guidance (not sourced) |
| Wash water, blood | 9VAC20-81-95 **E.1** defers to the water boards; see the liquid-waste section below | **Industrial waste** under 9VAC25-32 |

Sources: [DEQ VSWMR](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r2-disposal-deq-vswmr-exemptions-compost.md),
[Va. Code statutes](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r2-disposal-va-code-dead-animal-statutes.md).

**Corrections to round 1's research brief:** § 3.2-6554 covers companion animals only. Title 3.2
has no renderer chapter (Ch. 57 is weights and measures). 9VAC20-170 covers waste
transport on state waters, not composting.

## Liquid waste: blood and wash water (round 3)

| Rule | What it says | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **9VAC25-32 (VPA)** | "Industrial wastes" are liquid wastes from "any process of industry, manufacture, trade, or business". Discharge into or near state waters, **groundwater included**, needs a VPA, VPDES or other DEQ permit. An unpermitted discharge must be reported within 24 h, with a written report within 5 days | Spreading blood or wash water on a pasture is, on the text, a permitted activity. **No general permit exists** for small food or meat processing (only animal feeding operations and animal-waste end users) ([VPA](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r3-water-9vac25-32-vpa-scope-exclusions.md)) |
| 9VAC25-32-40 exclusions | (1) discharge to a POTW; (2) **nonpoint agricultural** runoff; (5) onsite treatment under a VDH permit | Whether (2) covers a *third-party* mobile unit's blood and wash water on a farm is **unsettled**. Ask the DEQ Valley Regional Office |
| 12VAC5-610 / -613 (VDH) | "Sewage" includes "liquid industrial wastes as may be present". Any alternative system (AOSS) is PE-designed for the actual wastewater strength; there is no meat category in the flow table; over 1,000 gpd is a Large AOSS. Spray irrigation goes to DEQ | Septic at the cooler means a custom-engineered system ([VDH](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r3-water-vdh-12vac5-610-613-onsite-sewage.md)) |

**Volume and strength** ([NMPAN wastewater](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r3-water-nmpan-wastewater-pretreatment.md)):
- Beef slaughter uses about **50–200 gal of water per animal**. At 5 head/day that is 250–1,000 gpd [inferred], which sits right at the small/large AOSS line.
- BOD is **1,000–2,500 mg/l with blood down the drain**, against **127 mg/l** when blood and the first sump rinse are hauled off. Residential sewage is about 100–300 mg/l.
- NMPAN: septic "probably will not work for most meat processing plants"; municipal sewer is "often your best bet"; dry clean-up and blood capture come first.
- A small Indiana plant runs six 1,000-gal tanks (the first drawn from the bottom as a grease trap), an effluent filter and a constructed wetland, with restrooms on a separate septic tank.

**Mobile-unit practice:** Sentinel's 53 ft unit drains to a 60 gal holding tank, and the 2020 VAFAIRS review says mobile units typically carry 500 gal of potable water and 500 gal of wastewater. A custom-exempt field kill on bare ground has none of that, so a **blood tub plus a wastewater holding tank on the rig** is the conservative design [inferred].

## SRMs: cattle 30 months and older

- **FSIS SRM list (9 CFR 310.22):**
  - For cattle ≥30 months: skull, brain, trigeminal ganglia, eyes, vertebral column, spinal cord,
    dorsal root ganglia.
  - For all cattle: distal ileum and tonsils.
- **VDACS applies this to custom operators.** Its custom-exemption guide requires inedible material
  "including SRMs" to be controlled so it can't be diverted into food
  ([VDACS](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-reg-vdacs-red-meat-custom-exemptions.md)). This answers the
  disposal agent's open question of whether SRM rules reach custom-exempt kills at all.
- **FDA feed ban (21 CFR 589.2001).** Brains and spinal cords of cattle ≥30 months are banned from **all** animal feed.
  So is the whole carcass of non-inspected cattle of that age unless the brain and spinal cord are removed.
  Renderers must segregate this material, dye it, label it "Do not feed to animals", keep records for ≥1 year,
  and get **supplier certifications** of segregation procedures
  ([21 CFR 589.2001](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r2-disposal-21cfr589-2001-srm-feed-ban.md)).
- **What this means for the operator** [inferred]: an age-sorting SOP, determined by dentition or records,
  that keeps ≥30-month brain and spinal cord in separate containers, plus a renderer that accepts them.
  T&E Meats charges **$50/head for "mad cow" handling** of cattle over 30 months. That is a real price
  point for this extra work.

## Renderers

- **One renderer for the whole state.** VDACS's 2023 five-year plan (RD894) records industry testimony that "the entire slaughter industry in Virginia is reliant on a single rendering operation". Hides lost their China export market, and processors now often pay to landfill them ([RD894](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r3-vastudy-vdacs-rd894-five-year-meat-capacity-plan.md)). Single-supplier risk belongs in the plan.
- **Renderer cost benchmark:** the California Central Coast USDA unit paid about **$6/animal** for renderer collection of tubbed offal ([Central Coast](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r3-cutshop-nmpan-central-coast-mhu.md)).

- **Darling Ingredients**, which bought Valley Proteins in fiscal 2022, lists animal by-product rendering plants at
  **Winchester, VA** and **Linville, VA** in its FY2025 10-K, plus Linkwood, MD
  ([Darling](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r2-disposal-darling-renderers-shenandoah.md)).
- Suppliers on non-formula terms may be paid, not paid, or "charged a collection fee", depending on
  volume and transport. A small operator should expect to pay [inferred].
- Archived Valley Proteins pages show dead-stock collection only "in a limited service area" and sold hides
  to the leather trade.
- **Unknown:** Darling's minimum volumes and fees, whether it will take ≥30-month SRM material and hides from a
  mobile operator, and its pickup radius.

## How other states compare

New Mexico and Washington let ranchers compost offal on their own land. California bans on-ranch
composting of mammalian tissue and requires an inedibles permit and a renderer
([NMPAN manual](../../raw/papers/2026-10-02-risk-nmpan-msu-manual-lessons.md)). On this question
Virginia's D.4 exemption looks closer to New Mexico and Washington than to California [inferred].
