# Mobile Beef Processing Business Plan

> **Status.** Synthesis of three research rounds (71 sources), built on **the user's chosen model**.
> It is a decision frame and diligence list, **not a costed pro forma**. The next step is real
> quotes and a zoning determination.

## The model

**A custom-exempt mobile unit kills on the owner's farm and later cuts and packages. A company-owned
fixed cooler only hangs and ages carcasses.** The full workup, with flow, sizing, labor, equipment and
revenue, is in [[cooler-backed-mobile-model|Cooler-Backed Mobile Model]] ([Cooler-Backed Mobile Model](cooler-backed-mobile-model.md)).

| Element | Plan | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Legal basis | VDACS Custom Permit, free, renews June 30. Custom-exempt "mobile slaughter **and processing** unit" expressly allowed | VDACS guide; 2VAC5-210-30 |
| Customers | Owners of the live animal: freezer-beef producers and their quarter/half buyers. No retail | 9 CFR 303.1; VDACS selling guide |
| Kill | On the owner's farm; offal composted there (likely exempt, 9VAC20-81-95 D.4) or tubbed for Darling Winchester; **blood captured, not spread** | Disposal article |
| Hang and age | Company cooler, 7–21 days, about 20–30 beef on the rail, about 300–500 sq ft | Iowa State, Friesla, NMPAN |
| Cut and pack | Mobile unit docked at the cooler; "Not for Sale" labels; owner pickup at the cooler | VDACS; NMPAN productivity |
| Capacity | **Cutting is the ceiling:** about 8 man-hours per custom beef (4.5 standardized), so 2 people ≈ 6–10 head/week ≈ 300–400 head/yr | NMPAN/Lorentz [inferred] |
| Revenue | ≈ **$800–1,100/head**: kill $100–150 + mileage + daily minimum, cut and pack $0.90–1.05/lb, aging and over-30-month surcharges | Backyard Butchery 2025; T&E 2025 [inferred] |
| Ownership | Decide early. A farmer-majority LLC or co-op opens Farm Credit (613.3010), FSA loans for members buying co-op stock, and VAPG | Financing article |

**Why this model** (user decision, evidence-consistent): the documented MSU failure is a unit with no partner to hang
and cut. California's Central Coast unit sat idle about 2 years, and regional cut shops run at 125–150% of capacity.
Owning the cooler and the cutting removes that dependency. **It moves the region's binding constraint, cutter
labor, inside the business.**

## The three hard problems

1. **The cooler site is a processing site.**
   - Frederick County lists slaughterhouses ("slaughtering or processing of meats") only as a **conditional use** in RA,
     and has no cold-storage use there. A service cooler fails the by-right "more than half produced on the farm" test.
   - Docking to cut there almost certainly triggers the CUP: 100 ft setbacks, a 20,000 sq ft cap, everything under
     roof, and screening.
   - Alternative: a commercially or industrially zoned parcel with **public sewer**.
   - See [[cooler-site-permits|Cooler Site Permits]] ([Cooler Site Permits](../concepts/cooler-site-permits.md)).
2. **Wastewater, on farms and at the dock.**
   - Blood and wash water are DEQ "industrial wastes", and there is no small-processor general permit.
   - Strength drops about tenfold if blood and the first rinse are captured (1,000–2,500 → 127 mg/l BOD).
   - At the dock, public sewer is cleanest; septic needs an engineered system, and the county resists pump-and-haul.
   - See [[slaughter-waste-disposal-virginia|Slaughter Waste Disposal in Virginia]] ([Slaughter Waste Disposal in Virginia](../concepts/slaughter-waste-disposal-virginia.md)).
3. **Capex for a kill-and-cut unit.**
   - Sentinel's 53 ft semi lists **from $300k**. Mobile Processing Trailers & Supply turnkey goosenecks are quote-only.
   - A 53 ft semi is awkward on farm lanes and needs a Class A CDL.
   - Option noted, not recommended over the user's choice: since cutting only happens at the dock, a cheap
     kill-only trailer ($72k–$115k) plus a separate cut-room trailer parked at the cooler is a cost alternative worth quoting.
   - See [[custom-exempt-mobile-rig|Custom-Exempt Mobile Rig]] ([Custom-Exempt Mobile Rig](../concepts/custom-exempt-mobile-rig.md)).

## Market case

- **Demand:**
  - Regional USDA plants ran at 125–150% of capacity with waits of 12–18 months (PEC 2021).
  - The state plan records **18–24 month** waits (RD894, 2023).
  - Blue Ridge Meats books 3–5 months out (2026).
  - Fauquier farmers already haul to Stephens City and Middletown (2024).
- **Policy tailwind:** the state's own plan says investment in custom-exempt capacity "could free up capacity in
  inspected facilities".
- **Herd:** about 26,300 head in Frederick, Clarke and Warren combined; about 32,000 in Shenandoah; about 174,000 in Rockingham and
  Augusta (NASS 2022, dairy included). Basing the cooler farther south widens reach [inferred].
- **Price position:** a fixed USDA plant is about $860–975 all-in for a 750 lb carcass (2025–26). The mobile service is
  priced about the same or higher, so it **sells no wait, no hauling, and on-farm harvest**, not price.

## Money

- **Debt is the realistic route:** Farm Credit of the Virginias (equipment and initial working capital; leasing), with VSBFA
  cash collateral covering lenders' discount on custom equipment. FSA operating loans are **barred** for a standalone
  slaughter service.
- **The cooler is the natural grant project:** AFID Infrastructure (≤$50k, through a locality) funded Piedmont
  Processing's beef-aging freezer space. REAP can fund efficiency later, since the cooler is a fixed installation.
- **Planning money:** an AFID Planning grant (through a county) or GO Virginia (two or more localities); free or low-fee VAFAIRS business plan.
- **Next deadlines:** AFID Infrastructure **Oct 30, 2026**; Southern SARE **Nov 6, 2026** (research only).
- See [[grant-and-financing-opportunities|Grant and Financing Opportunities]] ([Grant and Financing Opportunities](grant-and-financing-opportunities.md)).

## What would make it a no-go

- Frederick County (and the alternatives in Shenandoah, Clarke or Page) won't permit the cooler and dock, or no affordable
  site has sewer.
- DEQ won't accept on-farm handling of captured blood and wash water without a permit, and the renderer won't take it.
- You can't hire or be the second cutter. At one cutter, capacity drops to about 3–5 head a week.
- Quotes for a kill-and-cut unit plus cooler push debt service above what about 300 head/yr at about $900/head can carry.
- Producers won't pay about $100–150 plus mileage plus a minimum when a fixed plant charges a $75 kill fee.

## Diligence: calls, in order

| # | Who | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Frederick County Zoning Administrator** (then Shenandoah, Clarke, Page) | Written use determination for a cooler where a mobile cut unit docks; CUP timeline and cost |
| 2 | **Frederick Water / FWSA** | Sewer at candidate parcels; discharge limits; high-strength surcharge |
| 3 | **DEQ Valley Regional Office** | D.4 covers offal composted on the farm of kill? Does the agricultural exclusion cover a mobile operator's wash water? |
| 4 | **VDACS OMPS** (804-786-4569) | Custom Permit for a kill-and-cut mobile unit plus cooler; site inspection or checklist; current Valley custom-permit holders (competitors) |
| 5 | **Mobile Processing Trailers & Supply, Sentinel, Friesla** | Quotes: turnkey gooseneck vs 53 ft semi vs kill trailer plus cut-room trailer; docking power and water spec |
| 6 | **Darling Winchester** | Offal, blood, over-30-month SRM, hides: fees, minimums, pickup |
| 7 | **5–10 freezer-beef producers** | Head per year, current wait and haul, willingness to pay a farm call plus cutting |
| 8 | **Insurance agent** (Farm Bureau / Nationwide Agribusiness) | NCCI 2081, general and product liability, auto plus cargo, inland marine, spoilage, bailee |
| 9 | **Farm Credit of the Virginias; VSBFA** | Territory, eligibility, cash-collateral support |

## Biggest remaining unknowns

1. Zoning outcome and site cost: CUP vs a commercial parcel with sewer.
2. Real quotes for the kill-and-cut unit and the cooler.
3. DEQ's position on blood and wash water at farm kills.
4. Local producers' willingness to pay, and existing Valley mobile competitors (the VDACS roster).
5. Virginia insurance premiums.
