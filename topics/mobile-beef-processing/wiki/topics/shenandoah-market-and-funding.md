# Shenandoah Valley Market and Funding

## Supply: cattle within reach

NASS 2022 Census of Agriculture, inventory as of December 31, 2022
([NASS](../../raw/data/2026-10-02-mkt-nass-2022-county-cattle-shenandoah-valley.md)):

| County | Cattle & calves | Cattle sales ($1,000) | Farms selling direct to consumers | Distance from Winchester [inferred] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frederick | 10,720 | 4,325 | 9% | home |
| Clarke | 10,686 | 3,485 | 12% | adjacent |
| Warren | 4,885 | 1,910 | 7% | adjacent |
| Shenandoah | 31,990 | 18,522 | 7% | ~30–50 mi |
| Rockingham | 87,565 | 45,285 | 8% | ~60–80 mi |
| Augusta | 86,078 | 46,441 | 7% | ~80–100 mi |
| **Total** | **231,924** | **119,968** | | |

- The inventory counts **include dairy**. Rockingham and Augusta carry large dairy herds, so the
  number of beef animals there is meaningfully lower. The beef-cow split (Census Table 11) has
  **not been fetched**.
- Direct-to-consumer percentages cover **all products**, not beef alone.
- IGFC treats 100 miles (1–2 hours) as the efficient service radius for a mobile unit
  ([[msu-unit-economics|MSU Unit Economics]] ([MSU Unit Economics](../concepts/msu-unit-economics.md))).
  From Winchester that radius barely reaches Augusta. **A base near Winchester serves the
  thinnest part of the Valley's herd.** Locating farther south means competing directly with
  T&E Meats (Harrisonburg) [inferred].

## Demand signal: processing backlog

- **Blue Ridge Meats (Middletown, USDA): "We book 3 to 5 months in advance"** (2026)
  ([Blue Ridge](../../raw/data/2026-10-02-econ-blue-ridge-meats-frederick-county-terms.md)).
  This is the only local wait-time data point so far.
- For comparison, Oregon processors averaged about 6 months during 2020–22, with some booking 12 months out
  ([Rusted Gate](../../raw/papers/2026-10-02-econ-rusted-gate-msu-business-plan-2022.md)).
- **Round 2:** the 2021 PEC/AFT/Fuller study found the region's 7 USDA plants at **125–150% of capacity**, with waits of 6–9 months before COVID and 12–18 months during. T&E was booked about 16 months out in 2020. Every plant named **meat-cutter labor** as its main constraint. The full plant list and prices are in [[valley-processor-landscape|Valley Processor Landscape]] ([Valley Processor Landscape](../references/valley-processor-landscape.md)).
- **Still missing:** the Virginia Tech/VSU statewide processor assessment (begun 2021; results not found), current lead times in weeks, and direct-to-consumer beef trend data.

## Funding, status as of 2026-10-02

> **Superseded in detail by [[grant-and-financing-opportunities|Grant and Financing Opportunities]] ([Grant and Financing Opportunities](grant-and-financing-opportunities.md))**, which adds AFID Planning and Facility, GO Virginia, SARE, REAP, LFPP, RMAP and RBDG, and debt (Farm Credit, VSBFA, FSA). The table below is the round-1 snapshot.

| Program | Status | Fits a startup mobile unit? | Terms |
|---|---|---|---|
| **USDA MPPEP Phase 4** | Closed Aug 7, 2026; no Phase 5 announced | **No.** Needs ≥1 year operating under FSIS, CIS, or equal-to state inspection, and must primarily process cattle | Expansion $50k–$2M at 50% match; Equipment-Only $10k–$250k at 25% match ([MPPEP](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-mkt-usda-mppep-phase-4.md)) |
| **USDA VAPG** FY2026 | Closed Apr 22, 2026 | **Only if a producer group owns the unit** and sells its own beef (applicant must own >50% of the raw commodity) | Planning up to $50k, working capital up to $200k, 1:1 match; ~$25M total ([VAPG](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-mkt-usda-vapg-mpilp-mpirg-status.md)) |
| **USDA MPILP** | Program page "Not Found" in the 2026 snapshot; likely retired | Was open to startups **and custom-exempt** operators, through intermediary lenders | Unknown which Virginia lenders, if any, still hold the funds |
| **USDA MPIRG** | Only the 2021 and 2022 rounds are listed; dormant | For moving to inspection | — |
| **VDACS AFID Infrastructure** | **Open Sept 14 – Oct 30, 2026**; awards by Dec 31 | **Yes, through a locality** (county, EDA or planning district commission) | ≤$50k, reimbursement; 1:1 match, or 2:1 if the locality is distressed or the project serves multiple small producers ([AFID](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-mkt-vdacs-afid-infrastructure-grants.md)) |

AFID details that matter here:

- "Animal slaughtering facilities" are a named priority.
- A mobile oyster-processing barge was funded in 2022 ($25k), so a mobile unit has precedent.
- Special-purpose equipment is allowed, but **general-use motor vehicles are not**. Whether a
  slaughter trailer qualifies needs confirming with VDACS.
- Plausible applicants: Frederick County, the Winchester EDA, or the Northern Shenandoah Valley
  Regional Commission [inferred].
- The awardee list stops at January 2023.

**What this means for financing** [inferred]: the first unit will most likely be financed with
debt (FSA, Farm Credit, equipment financing secured by the trailer) or owner equity, with AFID as
a ≤$50k gap-filler. Federal expansion money becomes available only after a year of
*inspected* operation, and **a custom-exempt year does not count toward MPPEP eligibility.**

**Round 2:** FSA operating loans are **barred** for a standalone slaughter service (non-eligible enterprise). Farm Credit of the Virginias is the best debt fit. No AFID awardee list newer than January 2023 exists online. See the financing article.
