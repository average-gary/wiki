# Wiki Configuration

## Scope

What it takes to start and run a mobile beef slaughter and processing business in
Virginia's Shenandoah Valley (Frederick County / Winchester area and nearby counties).
Covers:

- **Regulatory models, compared.** Custom-exempt on-farm slaughter (owner-of-animal, meat
  marked "Not For Sale"), USDA-FSIS inspected mobile slaughter units (grant of inspection,
  HACCP, SSOPs, inspector scheduling), and state inspection. Virginia has no state
  meat-inspection program equivalent for red meat sold across state lines — confirm what
  VDACS actually runs and what Virginia's custom-exempt rules allow.
- **The unit itself.** MSU trailer builds and vendors, harvest-only vs harvest + cut-and-wrap,
  the fixed-base cooler/cut facility that mobile units still need, offal/blood/hide disposal
  and rendering, water and wastewater.
- **Unit economics.** Capex, per-head fees in the region, throughput per day, labor, insurance,
  break-even head counts, seasonality (fall rush), and published financial models
  (USDA/AMS, extension services, Niche Meat Processor Assistance Network).
- **Market and demand.** Shenandoah Valley cattle numbers, direct-to-consumer beef demand,
  existing processor wait times and capacity, competing custom processors.
- **Funding.** USDA MPPEP, MPIRG, Value-Added Producer Grants, Virginia AFID, FSA and
  Farm Credit lending.
- **Case studies and failures.** Operating MSUs and ones that shut down, with the reasons.

Out of scope:

- Poultry and small-ruminant-only operations, except where they share an MSU or bear on
  beef economics.
- Large fixed-plant (>100 head/day) slaughter design.
- Cattle husbandry and finishing, except as it drives demand for processing.

## Conventions

- **Name the regulatory model on every claim.** A per-head fee, throughput figure, or legal
  permission means different things under custom-exempt vs USDA-inspected. Never quote one
  without saying which.
- **Date every number.** Fees, grant amounts, and equipment costs drift; carry the year the
  source reported.
- **Separate Virginia-specific from national.** National case studies are evidence, not
  local fact. Mark what has been checked against Virginia rules or Virginia data.
- **Failures are first-class evidence.** Record why mobile units shut down as carefully as
  why they succeed.
